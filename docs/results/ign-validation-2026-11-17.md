## Inventory and annual-body scope

All 382 published year-directory catalogs were read: 372 network requests and 10 cached catalogs, with no request failures. They contain 175,137 `.txt` objects: 174,983 valid station/year daily filenames and 154 malformed or misplaced names. Valid names comprise 163,340 catalogued water-level files, 8,918 excluded auxiliary files and 2,725 files from four unlisted candidate channels. This is a filename census, not a validation of 175,137 daily bodies.

All 825 exports linked from the twelve gauges' `integros`, `horarios` and `observados/ficheros_anuales` catalogs downloaded successfully: 4,053,235,828 bytes. That inventory contains 797 current-name water-level exports, two ALAC1 `_old` hourly backups and 26 pressure exports. The 797 comprise 162 íntegros, 203 hourly and 432 raw exports. Misplaced annual-looking files inside daily directories are outside this body audit.

The standalone row audit read all 825 files and 141,022,573 timestamped rows. Current-name water-level rows total 139,646,102: 63,237,575 íntegros, 1,779,528 hourly and 74,628,999 raw. These counts include missing markers and malformed timestamped records; they are not counts of usable observations. [Download receipts](ign-validation-2026-11-17/annual-files.csv) contain URLs, byte sizes and SHA-256 hashes. [Annual audit](ign-validation-2026-11-17/annual-audit.json) records unit variants, headers, markers and anomalies. [Directory counts](ign-validation-2026-11-17/daily-directory-counts.csv) give each sensor/year's filename count and extent.

The production parser was also run on every canonical annual file as a syntax check, without asserting a calibration or applying a surveyed conversion: 784 of 797 parse, while 13 are refused. Identical adjacent reviewed rows are deduplicated; conflicting duplicates still fail. Its per-file results are in [syntax.csv](ign-validation-2026-11-17/syntax.csv). This is not an end-to-end adapter ingest: filename selection, named-mark routing, QC and store merging are separate checks. The parser accepts explicit missing values as transient invalidations; those are never persisted as NaN observations.

## Source defects and calibration evidence

- ALAC1/0702's raw and reviewed directions change between epochs. At 2003-02-01 00–03h the raw values are 1062, 1076, 1120 and 1102 mm, while reviewed values are −3411, −3397, −3353 and −3371 mm: reviewed = raw − 4473 exactly. Other months have negative unit slope. Negating every historical digital value is wrong for the positively aligned periods.
- ALAC1/0501 in 2000 has 4,294 common valid hourly pairs. January/February correlations are −0.351/−0.473; April–June correlations are 0.946/0.961/0.977 but slopes are 2.218/2.315/2.357. A ±48-hour lag search does not explain the early months. No scale or clock correction is inferred.
- Of 432 raw annual files, 290 have same-gauge/year reviewed hourly overlap, giving 2,223,611 pairs across 289 sufficiently populated comparisons and 34 instrument series. Strong correlation supports relative direction only, not a surveyed datum, UTC or stable calibration. It cannot establish an unobserved period.
- MURC1/0503 in 2001 has 7,386 common hours and correlation −0.9951; January has exact negative unit slope. Evidence for 2002–2003 is weaker. MURC2/0503 has no published annual or daily files in the complete inventories; its direction cannot be inferred from the instrument code.
- ALAC1/0702's annual 2006 export is byte-identical to 2005, including its 2005 timestamps (SHA-256 `3f81299bae1849e2d0aa8a6af2a5800440123b6a4d6c4ce37d1397ece867ebce`). It provides no 2006 coverage beyond a terminal boundary. Other exports spill into preceding years; they are not equivalent to one terminal midnight.
- MURC2's reviewed 2024 export contains 1,440 nonexistent `31/11/2024` rows. Those rows explicitly contain no measurement and cannot name a real instant: they are discarded without date rollover or touching another timestamp. The resulting local year request wrote and read 476,597 finite observations, ending on 27 November; it does not invent the absent later observations. TN031's reviewed 2019 export contains `07/05/2109 00:17:00 -9999`. JavaScript date rollover or a guessed year is not a correction.
- ACOR1/0601 annual 1998 ends in a truncated timestamp/value row; ACOR1/0701 annual 1997 ends with an incomplete unit. ALBO1 reviewed 2017–2019 contain malformed double-minus numbers. ALME2 reviewed 2022 lacks a header. Other source anomalies include reordered rows and a misnumbered ACOR1 annual file.
- Four unlisted candidate channels have 2,725 daily files: ACOR1/0801, ALAC1/1703, ALAC2/0501 and ALAC2/0208. Eight endpoint bodies were fetched, 8 of 2,725: all label millimeters, with 367 numeric and 49 blank rows. Their identity, direction, calibration and reference are not confirmed. They are not silently added to an existing instrument.

Four sheet-listed raw series have neither annual nor daily exports: ACOR1/1202, ALAC1/0401, FUER1/1103 and MURC2/0503. Five other series without annual exports do have 5,865 daily files. Across all catalogued series, annual availability leaves 24,239 daily fallback files; the per-timestamp union additionally reads daily records overlapping annual files. Those daily bodies are not all validated.

## Independent clock screening

Every one of the 797 canonical annual files was screened separately under published and reversed signs, using its first valid actual sample in each three-hour bucket, at most 2,928 per calendar year. Lags run from −180 to +180 minutes every ten minutes. K1 is fitted where elapsed span reaches 183 days; observed-day coverage is also reported. An independent second parser matched numerical and sampled counts for 797/797 files. Both signs of 9999 are omitted only for these statistics, not declared interchangeable scientifically. Annual fits can conceal within-file changes.

[Per-file screens](ign-validation-2026-11-17/clock-screens.csv) and the [summary](ign-validation-2026-11-17/clock-summary.json) distinguish 206 strong same-port tidal alignments, 28 unresolved clock/polarity cases, 442 weak-signal/calibration cases, 100 distant-reference cases, 11 short-span/no-K1 cases and 10 insufficient-coverage cases. These descriptive categories are not publication approvals. No inferred time, sign or scale correction is applied.

IEO Coruña, REDMAR Almería and REDMAR Tenerife are within 0.7 km of the corresponding current IGN positions. Other comparisons use more distant gauges, including Gandia for Alicante (78 km), Carboneras for Cartagena (107 km) and IEO La Palma for TN021 (122 km). Distant or weak-tide agreement does not establish a precise clock. CMEMS copies explicitly originating at IGN are not independent references. GESLA headers and metadata identify the selected REDMAR references as Puertos del Estado; [IEO's own network](https://mareografo.ieo.es/) documents its separate gauges.

All 24 Coruña reviewed hourly years 2000–2023 also received full hourly-sample checks: correlation at zero lag 0.98686–0.99501, best lag −10 to +10 minutes and K1 −4.87 to +15.90 minutes. That supports those reviewed clocks, not the raw archive. Raw counterexamples include:

- ACOR1/0302/1950: monthly best lags approximately +90 to +140 minutes; 1953–1969 generally +60 to +80 minutes.
- ACOR1/0601/1998: May +60, June +70, July +130 minutes, then September–December near zero.
- TN013/1301/2013: approximately +60 minutes against same-port REDMAR, correlation 0.99579, K1 +55.16 minutes.
- FUER1/0805 and /1607 in 2015: approximately −30 and −40 minutes against same-port REDMAR.

[IGN's server README](https://datos-geodesia.ign.es/Red_de_Mareografos/Leeme.txt) documents millimeters and says reviewed processing corrects temporal phase. Its [query page](https://www.ign.es/web/gds-red-mareografos) describes raw arbitrary instrumental origins and reviewed JM heights. Neither establishes UTC for every historical period. [IGN's reply](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/issues/181#issuecomment-6945552785) confirms CC BY 4.0, island-local height origins and reviewed status, but does not supply historical clock conventions, island epochs, dated calibrations or raw revision rules.

## Review and verification

The first three before-merge corrective review rounds completed with Opus and Sonnet. Both found the preceding-file boundary refusal; the adapter now validates only the closing row when that alone overlaps a request, retaining header validation. Ordinary out-of-range reviewed filenames no longer block unrelated years. Errata record Alborán’s mark spelling and the supported unit/boundary conventions. Opus then identified stale recent rows undoing backfill revisions during compaction; a single-station backfill now synchronizes those rows first, preserving arrival times and untouched rows. The follow-up repair serializes those operations per path for concurrent stations in one backfill process; separate writer processes still require the existing workflow concurrency group or non-overlapping manual runs. A regression covers withdrawal followed by numeric restoration and fails when synchronization is disabled.

The second-round BM-height concern was checked against all reviewed export receipts: ALAC1, ALAC2, FUER2 and MURC1 have 99 reviewed exports and zero BM exports. A new unsupported BM remains deliberately refused. Whole-fetch refusal when a minute product is malformed remains deliberate and documented; valid hourly data are not claimed as successfully ingested in that case. Daily/annual precedence remains provisional and is an explicit agency question. Expensive annual/boundary downloads, unknown raw names and full monthly runtime remain limitations. The PR stays draft to prevent deployment before scientific clearance.

The intermediate full check before serialization passed 1,297 tests in 223 suites with zero failures or skips, plus lint/typecheck. That intermediate suite did not exercise concurrent backfill station rewrites. Opus verified that the backfill pool defaults to concurrency four: two stations can read the same recent file and overwrite each other’s revisions. A subsequent repair serializes recent-file read/modify/write operations per path. The new two-station regression failed before that repair and now passes, including subsequent compaction. The final full-suite result and fourth review must be recorded separately.

Sonnet also requires source errata and refusal/flagging for the known clock/calibration counterexamples before deployment. Both reviewers flag automatic publication if the branch were merged while these blockers remain. The PR remains draft. Whole-file boundary download cost and monthly runtime remain unmeasured; the current-year annual/daily policy can still omit an annual-only timestamp when a daily directory exists. Those limits are unresolved.

Corrective implementation stopped at the three-round limit, then resumed under the user’s explicit instruction to run another round. All validation fixes and these evidence artifacts remain uncommitted in the local worktree; none of them has been pushed. No clean-review or merge-ready claim is made.

# Evaluation of Stillwell's quality control

This evaluates the store's QC of observed levels against published agency and research practice, for correctness first and cost second. Code references are to `main` at 4caa051e, the commit [current-pipeline.md](current-pipeline.md) maps. Practice references are to the notes in [sources/](sources/), with the page or section each note gives. The measurements were taken in November 2026 with the scripts in [scripts/](scripts/), which rerun them against the public store.

## Summary

The store's per-value QC is a small, well-documented set of rules that never edits a level, keeps the source's flag beside its own, and reruns from the source's flags whenever rules change. That design matches what every reference asks for. The rules themselves have three measured weaknesses. First, the spike rule judges a value against the median of its neighbors on both sides within three sampling intervals, so next to a gap it is biased by the slope of the tide: at Jersey (BODC) it marks 1,246 good values suspect in 2025, 8.4% of the year, while a 0.0 m reading in the same series stays good because it sits next to a gap and is never judged. Second, nothing in automatic QC looks at the series after removing the tide or compares neighbors, which is where every reference finds offsets, datum shifts and clock errors: a 7 m offset run at Kilrush Lough passes, and a one-off run of GESLA's datum-jump rule over other sources found 26 erroneous level shifts that QC had passed and that errata flag. Third, the range checks are not tied to each station's history: in the 17 station-years rerun for this evaluation, all with the outlier rule on, it flagged no value that another rule had not already flagged. One adapter mapping may be wrong in the other direction: at Dauphin Island, CO-OPS values that CO-OPS has verified are marked suspect when they still carry the rate-of-change flag, which removes real storm values, but the flag is rare elsewhere and needs a wider count before the mapping changes. A residual-based jump-and-return rule, prototyped here, flags the known bad values on the evidence stations and nothing in eleven storm and hurricane windows. The bad values this evaluation measured have no errata yet, so they read as usable to anyone filtering `flag <= 2`.

Top findings, ranked by effect on data quality:

1. The spike rule marks good values suspect next to gaps on steep tides: 1,246 at Jersey in 2025, 1,471 at Newport and 898 at Liverpool in 2024, nearly all within three values of a gap. Judging each value only against neighbors at mirrored times on both sides brings Jersey to 11. Confirmed.
2. A single bad value next to a gap is never judged, so it passes: Jersey's 0.0 m on 14 October 2025, Este's 3.20 m on 3 June 2025, and PEGELONLINE's 0.0 m placeholders at 693320 and 693330 on 10 November 2026. No fix-now item catches these; only the residual rule of recommendation 5 does. Confirmed.
3. Offset runs pass per-value QC: 837 values below −4 m at Kilrush Lough in 2025, all outside the span its erratum flags, are stored preliminary, not suspect. No automatic test looks at residuals or level steps. Confirmed.
4. CO-OPS verified values that carry the acquisition flat (F) or rate of change (R) flag are mapped to suspect, so real storm values are flagged: 315 at Dauphin Island in 2020, including Hurricane Delta. Confirmed there; R appears in none of the 21 station-months the erratum `coops-stale-limit-flag` was built on, so how widely it matters is open.
5. A stuck gauge that varies by a millimeter or two escapes both the flatline rule (exact equality) and the held-level rule (skips runs as long as a flatline), and there is no attenuated-signal test: Kilrush Lough's 4 to 7 August 2025 drift passes. Confirmed on synthetic input and on data.
6. Range checks are not per station: `limit_m` is ±20 m from the window's median and the outlier floor is ten times the window's spread. In 17 station-years rerun, the outlier rule flagged no value that another rule had not already flagged. Confirmed.
7. Non-finite levels in a window break `qc` two ways: the outlier rule's center and spread come out wrong and flag good values (2,072 in a 30-day synthetic window with two NaN), or the spike threshold becomes NaN and the spike and held tests stop for the whole window (a 3 m spike missed in 12 of 200 synthetic 7-day windows). No non-finite level was found in the recent files, so this is latent; GESLA's parser does not check for it. Confirmed.
8. Comparative checks only report, cover three regions, skip rivers and lakes and read only epoch 1. Confirmed.
9. A `RULES` change has no check that enforces its bump, and its requalify runs under a 120-minute monthly budget per source, so a large source can run under mixed rules for months; files don't record the rules that wrote them. Confirmed.
10. The flag scheme mixes two things that practice keeps apart, whether the source checked a value and whether it passed QC, and records no reason for a flag; GESLA's "no QC" maps to good. Confirmed.

## Method

- Read every source note in [sources/](sources/) and the QC code: [src/qc.ts](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts), [src/lake/recent.ts](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/recent.ts), [src/lake/requalify.ts](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/requalify.ts), [src/lake/analysis.ts](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/analysis.ts), [src/lake/merge.ts](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/merge.ts), [src/errata.ts](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/errata.ts), [src/backfill.ts](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/backfill.ts), [scripts/ingest.ts](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/scripts/ingest.ts), [scripts/cross-check.ts](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/scripts/cross-check.ts) and the adapters' flag mappings.
- Checked each suspected gap in [current-pipeline.md](current-pipeline.md#gaps-and-inconsistencies-noticed-while-mapping) against the code, then against synthetic input run through `qc` itself, then against the public store where a query was cheap.
- Reran `qc` from [src/qc.ts](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts) on real station-years read from the store, with `fromSource` and the neighboring years as context, as `requalify` does. On Jersey 2025, Ian at MARKH 2022 and the 2026 years of PEGELONLINE 9420020, 5950081 and EA E71924 and E72039, the rerun reproduced the stored flags exactly, so the harness matches production. Kilrush Lough differs only by its `errata.json` spans, which the harness leaves out. The 17 station-years rerun are those, Kilrush Lough 2025, Galway 2025, PEGELONLINE 9420020 and 5950081 2025, Cyremort Point 2019 and the six CO-OPS years below.
- Prototyped candidate rules on the same station-years to state what they would flag. The prototypes are evaluation scripts in [scripts/](scripts/), not store code. Changes proposed to [`src/qc.ts`](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts) and `qcFor` were applied to a scratch copy of the repository to see which tests they break and what they flag.
- A few figures have no script: the source flag shares (summed from the years files), the 69 JMA files checked for a sentinel, the check of BODC's ERDDAP for a missing row, and the one-off `datumJumps` run, whose results are the errata's own evidence. [scripts/README.md](scripts/README.md) lists them. The harmonic fit is the store's own ([src/harmonic.ts](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/harmonic.ts), t_tide's standard set).
- Evidence stations come from the outlier gap analysis over 120 tidal station-years (Kilrush Lough, Jersey, Este) and its list of real surges any rule must keep (German Bight January 2025, Eowyn and Amy at Marine Institute gauges, Ian at SFWMD MARKH, Barry at USACE Cyremort Point), plus microtidal CO-OPS hurricane years added here (Pensacola 2020, Shell Beach 2020 and 2021, Dauphin Island 2020, Carrollton and Berwick 2019).

## Findings

Each suspected gap from [current-pipeline.md](current-pipeline.md#gaps-and-inconsistencies-noticed-while-mapping), then findings the map did not list.

### Suspected gaps

1. **Recent-window QC differs from year QC.** Confirmed in code, with no effect measured. The recent window runs `qc` with no context ([src/lake/recent.ts:246](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/recent.ts)) over `keepDays`, 7 days ([:111](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/recent.ts)), and compaction folds those flags into the year file with `upsert` ([src/lake/merge.ts:80-102](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/merge.ts)). Rerunning year QC on the stored 2026 files of PEGELONLINE 9420020 and 5950081 and EA E71924 and E72039 changed no flag. Synthetic input shows why the outlier rule rarely differs: inside a 7-day window a surge widens the spread it is judged against.
2. **A near-flat run within 1 cm escapes both rules.** Confirmed. The held rule passes a run that reaches `flatline_s` whole ([src/qc.ts:166-172](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)) and the flatline rule needs exact equality ([src/qc.ts:116](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)). Synthetic: an 8-hour stuck reading dithering ±1 mm on a 1 m tide had 1 of 80 values flagged; the same run exactly flat had 80 of 80.
3. **Flatline runs ignore time gaps.** Confirmed. Four equal hourly values, two before and two after a 60-hour gap, were all flagged under still water's 48 hours ([src/qc.ts:114-125](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)).
4. **One `spacing` per window.** Confirmed on synthetic input. In a year of 6-minute values followed by hourly ones, a 3 m spike in the hourly part was not flagged; the hourly part alone flagged it ([src/qc.ts:130](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts), [:135-139](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)). How many station-years mix intervals was not measured.
5. **The outlier floor flags surges at small-tide stations.** Refuted on real data. The outlier rule flagged no value in Pensacola 2020, Shell Beach 2020 and 2021, Dauphin Island 2020, Carrollton 2019, Berwick 2019, MARKH 2022 or Cyremort Point 2019, all tidal stations with `tide_m` from 0.08 to 0.23 m and outliers on. Synthetic input with no weather variance does trip it in a year window (a 1.2 m surge on a 0.1 m tide: 136 of 400 values), so the risk exists only where a station's level barely varies apart from the tide.
6. **`judge`'s `tide` pass uses plain rules for kinds other than river and lake.** Confirmed in code ([src/lake/analysis.ts:133-138](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/analysis.ts)), and intended: the comment at [:124-132](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/analysis.ts) says a tidal verdict turns the outlier check on there, so every hold six hours flags counts. Only estuary and unset kinds are affected, since coastal ignores `drains`.
7. **The recent window catches every `suspect` error.** Confirmed ([src/lake/recent.ts:231-235](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/recent.ts) against [src/lake/requalify.ts:56-57](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/requalify.ts)). The window then keeps its stored flags, so an adapter bug in `suspect` goes unnoticed in hourly runs.
8. **Nothing enforces `RULES` or `JUDGE` bumps.** Confirmed. No test compares a fingerprint of [src/qc.ts](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts) or `qcFor` with `RULES` ([src/lake/analysis.ts:60-72](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/analysis.ts)); tests only read the constant ([`test/analysis.test.ts:188`](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/test/analysis.test.ts#L188)).
9. **No unit tests for `wholeCentimeters` thresholds or non-finite `limit_m`.** Confirmed. `qc` tests are in [`test/lake.test.ts:310-457`](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/test/lake.test.ts#L310-L457); there is no `test/qc.test.ts`.
10. **A source-flagged preliminary value is never refetched.** Partly confirmed. The refetch starts at the earliest value whose `coalesce(source_flag, flag)` is preliminary ([src/lake/parquet.ts:307](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/parquet.ts), [:753](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/parquet.ts)) and then refetches everything after it, so source-suspect values after that point do come back verified. A stretch or year whose only unverified values are source-suspect, such as USGS `ICE` all winter, counts as done ([src/backfill.ts:72-82](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/backfill.ts)), as [src/backfill.ts:53-56](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/backfill.ts) says.
11. **Unmapped codes fall through inconsistently.** Confirmed in code: DMI's `qcResult` outside 1 to 6 becomes good when `qcStatus` is `manual` ([src/sources/dmi/index.ts:261-265](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/dmi/index.ts)); CO-OPS `q` other than `p` starts good ([src/sources/coops/index.ts:330](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/coops/index.ts)); Marine Institute and UHSLC compare with `===` ([src/sources/marine-institute/index.ts:203](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/marine-institute/index.ts), [src/sources/uhslc/index.ts:505](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/uhslc/index.ts)). No value affected by these was found; Kilrush Lough's 2025 file has no source-suspect value.
12. **PEGELONLINE's REST path turns a null into 0 m.** Confirmed in code ([src/sources/pegelonline/index.ts:183](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/pegelonline/index.ts) has no null filter, unlike [:254](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/pegelonline/index.ts)). The three isolated 0.0 m values in the PEGELONLINE recent file this week are not this bug: PEGELONLINE's own API returns `0.0` at 693330 at 03:30 UTC on 10 November 2026. JMA's parser accepts any integer cell ([src/sources/jma/index.ts:391-398](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/jma/index.ts)); none of 69 JMA 2024 files holds a 9.99 m or −9.99 m value, so no sentinel was found.
13. **`bad` comes from adapters only at BODC, DMI, GESLA and USACE.** Confirmed by search of `Flag.bad` in [`src/sources/`](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources). It changes little for readers, since `flag <= 2` drops suspect and bad alike ([docs/data.md:30](../data.md)).
14. **GESLA's "no QC" maps to good.** Confirmed ([src/sources/gesla/index.ts:440](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/gesla/index.ts)), deliberately: the comment says a static release never finalizes.
15. **Extremes and predictions get no QC or errata marks.** Confirmed ([src/backfill.ts:485-497](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/backfill.ts)).
16. **Backfill does not re-QC the neighboring years.** Confirmed ([src/lake/requalify.ts:124-138](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/requalify.ts)).
17. **Retiring or adding a flagged erratum needs a manual requalify.** Confirmed ([CONTRIBUTING.md:229](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/CONTRIBUTING.md)); no workflow runs it.
18. **Cross-check covers little.** Confirmed: epoch 1 only through `seriesPath`'s default ([scripts/cross-check.ts:196](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/scripts/cross-check.ts)), `flag IN (0, 2)` ([:206](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/scripts/cross-check.ts)), rivers and lakes left out ([:112](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/scripts/cross-check.ts)), and it writes nothing to the store ([scripts/cross-check.ts:1-11](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/scripts/cross-check.ts)).
19. **Docs that disagree with code.** All six confirmed: [docs/data.md:30](../data.md) says requalify runs when someone runs it, but `analyze --requalify` runs monthly ([.github/workflows/monthly.yml:151](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/.github/workflows/monthly.yml)); [docs/data.md:31](../data.md) says an adapter marks Pointe-à-Pitre, which is the erratum `refmar-pointe-a-pitre-clock` ([src/sources/refmar/errata.json:4](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/refmar/errata.json)); [CONTRIBUTING.md:41](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/CONTRIBUTING.md) leaves `analyze` out of the commands that [:54](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/CONTRIBUTING.md) lists; [docs/data.md:49](../data.md) leaves out that still water has no held-level check; the RWS README names two statuses and the code accepts `Definitief` too ([src/sources/rws/index.ts:388](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/rws/index.ts)); and [src/qc.ts:6](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts) says QC runs in the Worker, but [`workers/`](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/workers) holds only `cron`. [CONTRIBUTING.md:116](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/CONTRIBUTING.md) also calls the Pointe-à-Pitre span REFMAR's `suspect`, and two code comments make the same claim as [docs/data.md:31](../data.md): [src/qc.ts:44-45](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts) and [src/backfill.ts:59-60](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/backfill.ts) say REFMAR's adapter marks suspect spans and sets `source_flag`, but REFMAR defines no `suspect`.
20. **Analysis and requalify cost.** Confirmed in code ([src/lake/analysis.ts:261](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/analysis.ts), [scripts/ingest.ts:922-944](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/scripts/ingest.ts)). Only stations with values are due, so the monthly cost of null verdicts is a few hundred stations. The cost that matters is a `RULES` bump; see [Efficiency](#efficiency).
21. **`FLOAT` storage and 0.1 mm equality.** Confirmed at six stations. A 0.1 mm value survives the trip through a 32-bit float and back exactly below 1,024 m; above that, 14% to 59% of values come back 0.1 mm off ([scripts/float32.py](scripts/float32.py)). A scan of every year file's Parquet statistics on 11 November 2026, 281,126 files ([scripts/max-level.py](scripts/max-level.py)), found six USGS stations whose 2026 files hold only levels above 1,024 m, their `GAGE` datum being an elevation: 09384600 Lyman Lake (1,813 to 1,817 m), 13236000 Deadwood Reservoir, 13190000 Anderson Ranch Reservoir, 11507200 and 11504290 on the Klamath, and 09328990 on the Colorado above Hite (1,087 to 1,626 m). At Lyman Lake, Anderson Ranch and Deadwood, 18% to 22% of stored values sit 0.1 mm from the nearest 0.01 ft value the source reports. Values read back from storage stay equal to each other, so flatline runs among stored values are judged right; a freshly fetched value can differ by 0.1 mm from its stored copy, which can split a run where the recent window or backfill meets stored values. Every other file above 1,024 m holds sentinels, below in [Sentinels the limit rule misses](#sentinels-the-limit-rule-misses).

### Spike rule false positives next to gaps

The spike rule compares each value with the median of its neighbors within three sampling intervals on both sides ([src/qc.ts:131-140](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)). When a gap leaves more neighbors on one side than the other, the median sits on the side with more, and on a steep tide that offset exceeds the 0.25 m floor. At Jersey on 10 March 2025, 6.393 m at 00:30, between 6.100 at 00:15 and 6.967 at 01:00 with 00:45 missing, is flagged suspect though it lies on the curve: its neighbors within 45 minutes are 23:45, 00:00 and 00:15 before and 01:00 and 01:15 after, so the median of the five is the 00:15 value, 15 minutes behind it on a tide rising about 2 cm a minute. All 1,246 values QC flags at Jersey in 2025 (8.4% of the year) are within three values of a gap; at Newport 1,283 of 1,471 and at Liverpool 896 of 898 are (2024). BODC's service leaves out one value about every 7 h 45 min (751 one-value gaps in the roughly 5,850 hours Liverpool's 2024 file covers, which lacks January, February and two other months; BODC's ERDDAP has no row at 07:30 on 1 March 2024), so every BODC series meets this rule constantly. Synthetic: a 9 m tide at 15-minute sampling with one 45-minute gap gave 68 false flags over 12 tidal phases.

The reference has to sit at the value's own time. Five variants of `qc`, each changing only that reference ([scripts/spike-variants.ts](scripts/spike-variants.ts)), on 36 synthetic cases (a 9 m tide at 15 minutes with a gap of one to three values, at 12 tidal phases) and on real years, each run with the options `qcFor` gives the station, as production does. The table counts values QC raises over the source's flag; the last column counts how many values the spike rule judges at all, out of the six station-years' 825,709.

An equal count still leaves the reference off the value's time when a gap sits on one side: at Jersey's 00:30 the two nearest each side are 00:00, 00:15, 01:00 and 01:15, whose median is the level of about 00:37, 0.14 m above the curve at 00:30, and up to 0.28 m where a 9 m tide is steepest. Mirrored times put the reference at the value's own time for a straight slope and keep the median's resistance to a bad neighbor. A line through the nearest neighbors has no slope bias at all but follows a bad neighbor: at Este, where the gauge glitches for a minute at a time, it flags 270 values, most of them the good values beside each glitch. At Jersey, 2 of the 11 left under mirrored times are 18:45 and 19:00 on 14 October, the good values before the 0.0 m, whose mirrored neighbor is the 0.0 m itself. Mirrored times alone stop judging 4,558 values that today's rule judges, 4,548 of them at MARKH, whose sampling is irregular, so a bad value there could pass; falling back to today's median where no mirrored pair exists judges every value today's rule judges and gave the same counts.

### Single bad values next to gaps

The same rule needs neighbors on both sides ([src/qc.ts:139](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)), so a value with a gap on one side is never judged. At Jersey on 14 October 2025, 0.0 m at 19:15 sits between 5.56 m and a one-hour gap, flagged good; its two good neighbors before it are flagged suspect instead. At Este (PEGELONLINE 5950081) on 3 June 2025, 3.20 m at 08:10 follows 6.40 m and precedes a gap, flagged preliminary. This week PEGELONLINE sent 0.0 m at 693320 and 693330 at 03:30 UTC on 10 November 2026, each before a 105-minute gap, and both are stored as preliminary. A gauge often sends a bad value just as it fails, so this case is common, not rare. No change to the neighbor reference can judge these values, since the side that would show them wrong is missing. The prototype residual rule of recommendation 5 flags them for a different reason: a residual jump of more than 0.5 m followed by a gap, with no jump back, flags the values from the jump to the gap.

### Offset runs and level shifts

At Kilrush Lough on 9 January 2025 the level drops from 1.477 m to −5.653 m in five minutes, stays near −5.7 to −6.2 m for 4 h 20 min and returns to −0.845 m. QC flags the two ends and none of the 51 values between. In all, 837 values below −4 m are stored preliminary in 2025: those 51, 773 from 5 to 10 August and 13 on 9 September, all before the span that erratum `marine-institute-kilrush-2025-offsets` flags (10 September to 30 November). The held rule needs values within 1 cm ([src/qc.ts:163-164](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)); the outlier limit there is about 17 m. On 4 to 7 August 2025 the same gauge drifts down to −6 m over three days with its daily range shrinking from 3.5 m to 1 m, which no rule addresses and no erratum covers.

Per-value QC never compares a station with its tide or its neighbors. `datumJumps`, GESLA's rule for a 30-day median shift of more than 0.75 m ([src/sources/gesla/index.ts:513-560](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/gesla/index.ts)), runs automatically only for GESLA's coastal records ([:811-839](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/gesla/index.ts)). Run once by hand over every coastal record, it found the shifts behind 33 current errata. Seven are real changes of datum that the store keeps as served or corrects (`refmar-hiva-oa-uhslc-2020-datum`, `refmar-huahine-2021-datum`, `refmar-dumont-durville-datums`, `rws-kralendijk-2026-level`, `uhslc-qaqortoq-2008-datum`, `uhslc-sao-tome-2004-datum`, `uhslc-gibraltar-2023-datum`). The other 26 are erroneous values that QC had passed and that errata flag: BODC (1), CO-OPS (7), EA (3), Marine Institute (6), PEGELONLINE (1), REFMAR (3), SFWMD (2) and UHSLC (3), among them Malin Head's 3.9 m offset for a month in 2026.

### CO-OPS verified values marked suspect

`flagOf` marks a 6-minute value suspect when its `f` list has F or R set, verified or not, and drops only L on verified values as stale ([src/sources/coops/index.ts:328-337](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/coops/index.ts)). That split is deliberate: erratum `coops-stale-limit-flag` ([src/sources/coops/errata.json:72-77](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/coops/errata.json)) found L on ordinary verified values and says "flat (F) and rate-of-change (R) still do" make a value suspect, and the comment at [src/sources/coops/index.ts:316-326](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/coops/index.ts), the adapter README ([src/sources/coops/README.md:9](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/coops/README.md)), the assertions at [`test/sources.test.ts:1154-1155`](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/test/sources.test.ts#L1154-L1155) and the fixture at [`test/sources.test.ts:1176-1181`](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/test/sources.test.ts#L1176-L1181) all state it. The erratum's evidence covers L only; it did not look at F or R. F and R are the flags CO-OPS's acquisition software sets as each value arrives ([noaa-co-ops-tr-030-qc-requirements.md](sources/noaa-co-ops-tr-030-qc-requirements.md), section 3.1); verification is a two-person monthly review that removes spikes, and "in most cases, the raw 6-minute data points end up becoming the verified 6-minute data, minus spikes and gaps" ([noaa-co-ops-data-flags-and-verification.md](sources/noaa-co-ops-data-flags-and-verification.md), Verification). A verified value still in the product is one the analyst kept. At Dauphin Island, 315 verified values in 2020 are source-suspect, clustered on storm days: on 9 October 2020, during Hurricane Delta, 1.326 m at 20:12 between 1.322 and 1.306 m is suspect. CO-OPS's API for October 2020 there shows the same evidence the erratum used for L: of 7,440 verified values, 175 carry R and none F, and the R values sit a median of 3.1 cm and at most 14.8 cm from the mean of their two neighbors, against 1.2 cm and 15.9 cm for the unflagged ones, all under the store's own 0.25 m spike floor. They are ordinary values on rough days, which is when waves trip the rate-of-change test ([noaa-co-ops-tr-030-qc-requirements.md](sources/noaa-co-ops-tr-030-qc-requirements.md), p. 7-8), so the mapping removes values from exactly the events users study. No verified F was seen, so this evidence covers R only. The same count over the seven stations and three Marches the erratum used ([scripts/coops-r.py](scripts/coops-r.py)) found no verified R in any of the 21 station-months (156,239 values) and 8 verified F, so R on verified values is rare, and one station-month is too narrow a base to overturn the erratum's choice; see [Open questions](#open-questions).

### Sentinels the limit rule misses

The same scan found 254 files at 117 stations whose levels reach beyond 1,024 m, all but the six above from sentinels or scale errors: USACE (61 stations; ±30,479.695 m and 3,047.7 m, which are ±100,000 ft and 10,000 ft, and up to 3,048,026 m), GESLA (31; Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon's CMEMS copy reaches 2.4 × 10¹² m in 2024), CDEC (14), USGS (3 more) and PEGELONLINE (2). The limit rule marks a value bad only beyond 20 m from the window's own median ([src/qc.ts:107-110](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)), so a window that is mostly sentinel judges the sentinel normal: four USACE MVS files of 2026 hold only sentinels of 30,479.695 m and more, and at Sullivan all 29,706 values are flagged suspect, by the flatline rule, not bad; Marine Institute Skerries' 2017 file holds two values of 327.73 m and both are stored preliminary. Recommendation 9's per-station limits from history would mark them.

### Measured bad values without errata

The bad values measured here have no erratum, so their flags come only from QC, which passes them: they are stored preliminary or good, and a reader who filters `flag <= 2`, as [docs/data.md:30](../data.md) advises, reads them as water. They are Kilrush Lough's run on 9 January 2025, its drift and low values from 4 to 10 August 2025 and its low values on 9 September 2025 (837 values below −4 m in all), Jersey's 0.0 m at 19:15 on 14 October 2025, Este's 3.20 m at 08:10 on 3 June 2025, and PEGELONLINE's 0.0 m at 693320 and 693330 at 03:30 on 10 November 2026. [CONTRIBUTING.md:227](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/CONTRIBUTING.md) asks for an erratum for each source error whether the adapter works around it or not; recommendation 2 lists them.

### Non-finite levels

`median` sorts with a numeric comparator ([src/qc.ts:228-233](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)); a NaN makes the comparator return NaN, the sort's order is then arbitrary, and the middle element may be wrong or NaN. Only the limit rule and the context rows skip non-finite levels ([src/qc.ts:101](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts), [:107-109](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)). Two paths fail ([scripts/non-finite.ts](scripts/non-finite.ts)):

- **False flags, from the outlier rule.** It takes the median and spread of every own level ([src/qc.ts:191-193](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)). With NaN at two rows of a 30-day window, 2,072 good values were flagged suspect (and the two NaN bad); with the outlier rule off, or its floor raised, none were. The held rule played no part.
- **Tests silently off, from the spike threshold.** A NaN row's own residual is NaN minus its neighbors' median ([src/qc.ts:139](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)) and passes the filter into `mad`, which drops only `undefined` ([:143](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)). When `mad` comes out NaN, `Math.max(spike_m, NaN)` is NaN, so no residual exceeds it and the spike test ([:146](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)) and the held test ([:180](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)) flag nothing in the whole window. In 200 synthetic 7-day windows with 1 to 20 NaN at random rows, a 3 m spike went unflagged in 12 and 43 windows had more than 10 false flags, as the script prints under Node 24.22.1, the 24.x release `.mise.toml` (`node = "24"`) resolved to when measured, so another 24.x release may print a different count; replacing the NaN with numbers brought each missed flag back. Under Node 26.13.0 it prints 14 missed, since the order a sort with NaN leaves is up to the engine.

No null or non-finite level was found in the recent files of the 13 sources that have one, so this is latent. Most adapters drop non-finite values, but not all: GESLA's parser takes `Number(fields[0])` with no finiteness check ([src/sources/gesla/index.ts:473](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/gesla/index.ts)), and GESLA has no recent file to scan.

### Flags and provenance

- QC records no reason for a flag, so a reader can't tell a spike from an erratum span from the source's mark.
- Clock corrections in adapters, such as GESLA's WSV hourly means ([src/sources/gesla/index.ts:492-494](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/gesla/index.ts)), shift times with no mark on the values; the shift is documented only in `errata.json`.
- Files don't record the `RULES` that wrote their flags; the years file's `version` is a hash of the rows ([docs/data.md:273](../data.md)).

### Source flag shares

From the years files of 18 sources (281,983 year files, 8.01 billion values): BODC has 43.7% suspect and 8.9% bad, driven by whole station-years the source marks improbable (Bangor 2023 to 2025 all suspect; Fishguard and Bournemouth 2023 to 2025 all bad from the 0 mm placeholder the adapter documents). CRMS has 7.0% suspect. Every other source is under 3.4%.

## Comparison with practice

Each part says what the references prescribe, what the store does and the gap.

### Range and climatology

- **Practice.** QARTOD's gross range test fails values outside the sensor's span and marks suspect those outside an operator span, and its climatology test marks suspect outside seasonal limits with no fail flag "because of the potential for extreme water levels" ([ioos-qartod-water-level-v2.1.md](sources/ioos-qartod-water-level-v2.1.md), tests 4 and 5, p. 17). Limits are "dependent on the station and included in the metadata for each station" ([ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md](sources/ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md), section 2.3, p. 11) and updated yearly from historical statistics (section 2.3.2). CO-OPS derives each station's limits from its period of record and flags beyond them plus 3 m ([noaa-co-ops-tr-030-qc-requirements.md](sources/noaa-co-ops-tr-030-qc-requirements.md), p. 7). Copernicus delayed mode uses ±3 times the 99th percentile of historical data, on total levels and on residuals ([cmems-sea-level-rep-quid-2025.md](sources/cmems-sea-level-rep-quid-2025.md), section III.1.2). For rivers, WMO sets high and low limits at the extremes of the past three to five years and reviews them every three years ([wmo-1044.md](sources/wmo-1044.md), 6.5.3); USGS requires very-high and very-low thresholds per site ([usgs-wrd-99-34-realtime-qa.md](sources/usgs-wrd-99-34-realtime-qa.md)).
- **Stillwell.** Bad beyond 20 m from the window's median ([src/qc.ts:107-110](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)); suspect beyond the larger of 10 times the window's spread and 1 m, tidal stations other than river or lake only ([src/qc.ts:190-197](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts), [src/lake/recent.ts:818-828](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/recent.ts)).
- **Gap.** No per-station limits from history. The window's own spread is the reference, so a long bad stretch widens its own limit (Kilrush Lough's is about 17 m). In 17 year-long station-years rerun, all with the rule on, the outlier rule added no flag. In a 7-day window it does catch what other rules miss, a 2 m rise after six flat days ([`test/lake.test.ts:415-424`](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/test/lake.test.ts#L415-L424)), so its value is in the recent window, where no history is at hand. The residual range test is not safe as a suspect flag at small-tide stations: in a prototype of Copernicus's ±3 q99 on residuals, Ian at MARKH put 99 values beyond it and Eowyn at Galway 32, all real water.

### Spike

- **Practice.** QARTOD compares a value with the mean of its two neighbors, with thresholds fixed or "a multiple of the standard deviation over an operator-selected period", and leaves the newest value "not evaluated" until the next arrives ([ioos-qartod-water-level-v2.1.md](sources/ioos-qartod-water-level-v2.1.md), test 6, p. 18; section 3.3.1, p. 15). SELENE, used by MyOcean and Copernicus, fits a degree-2 spline over 12 to 16 hours and flags values beyond 3 sigma, then reruns on residuals ([ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md](sources/ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md), section 2.3.1, p. 13-17; [eurogoos-nrtqc-2010.md](sources/eurogoos-nrtqc-2010.md), section 6). Copernicus delayed mode sizes the window by sampling and adds a persistent-spike test ([cmems-sea-level-rep-quid-2025.md](sources/cmems-sea-level-rep-quid-2025.md), section III.1.2). A fitted curve, not a neighbor median, is the reference in each, so a slope is not a spike.
- **Stillwell.** Median of neighbors within three sampling intervals, both sides required, threshold the larger of 0.25 m and 8 times the spread of residuals ([src/qc.ts:127-147](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)).
- **Gap.** The median reference is biased by slope when the neighbors are unbalanced (Jersey), and a value with neighbors on one side is never judged (Jersey's 0.0 m, Este's 3.20 m). The 0.25 m floor is fixed for every station, where references derive thresholds per station.

### Rate of change

- **Practice.** QARTOD marks suspect a step beyond N standard deviations over 25 hours, or "0.1 meter +2SD", and notes "upon failure, it is unknown which point is bad" ([ioos-qartod-water-level-v2.1.md](sources/ioos-qartod-water-level-v2.1.md), test 7). CO-OPS uses third differences with a per-station limit at 5 standard deviations of history ([noaa-co-ops-tr-030-qc-requirements.md](sources/noaa-co-ops-tr-030-qc-requirements.md), p. 7-8) and first differences from each site's distribution ([noaa-co-ops-tr-061-microwave-qc.md](sources/noaa-co-ops-tr-061-microwave-qc.md), p. 30-31). MG 83 warns it is hard to apply to 1-minute data ([ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md](sources/ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md), section 2.3, item 7). WMO's maximum-difference threshold comes from the largest valid step in three to five years ([wmo-1044.md](sources/wmo-1044.md), 6.5.3).
- **Stillwell.** None.
- **Gap.** A step test is how references catch the onset of offset runs and multi-point spikes. On total level it trips on steep tides and seiches; on residuals it doesn't.

### Flat line and attenuated signal

- **Practice.** QARTOD marks suspect three values and fails five "equal within EPS", example 1 mm, with an exception for lakes and estuaries ([ioos-qartod-water-level-v2.1.md](sources/ioos-qartod-water-level-v2.1.md), test 8). CO-OPS flags a value equal to the two before and two after ([noaa-co-ops-tr-030-qc-requirements.md](sources/noaa-co-ops-tr-030-qc-requirements.md), p. 7). EuroGOOS gives 3 values for 5-minute data ([eurogoos-nrtqc-2010.md](sources/eurogoos-nrtqc-2010.md), section 6). Copernicus delayed mode starts at 10 equal values and adds 15 for centimeter data, 10 for series that are often stuck, and up to 40 after visual review ([cmems-sea-level-rep-quid-2025.md](sources/cmems-sea-level-rep-quid-2025.md), section III.1.2). MG 83 says the count "depends on the location of the gauge, the point within the tidal cycle and the sampling interval" ([ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md](sources/ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md), section 2.3, item 4). For a series "nearly but not exactly a flat line", QARTOD's attenuated-signal test fails a 12-hour range or SD under a minimum ([ioos-qartod-water-level-v2.1.md](sources/ioos-qartod-water-level-v2.1.md), test 10), and Copernicus tests attenuation on hourly values against the station's historical spread ([cmems-sea-level-rep-quid-2025.md](sources/cmems-sea-level-rep-quid-2025.md), section III.1.2). WMO lists the 20 longest unchanged periods for review ([wmo-1044.md](sources/wmo-1044.md), 6.5.3) and names rounded peaks and flat recessions as a blocked intake ([wmo-168.md](sources/wmo-168.md), 9.6.2.1).
- **Stillwell.** Exact equality for 6 hours at tidal water, 48 hours at still water or where a small tide moves in whole centimeters ([src/qc.ts:112-125](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts), [src/lake/recent.ts:818-828](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/recent.ts)); the held-level rule catches runs within 1 cm shorter than a flatline that sit beyond the spike threshold from both sides ([src/qc.ts:149-186](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)). No attenuation test.
- **Gap.** The store's limit is far longer than any reference's count (6 hours is 60 values at 6 minutes, 360 at 1 minute, against 3 to 25), which is a deliberate trade against centimeter data and still water, and the store measures resolution locally where references set it per station. Exact equality misses a stuck sensor that dithers (synthetic, 1 of 80). No test sees a signal that keeps moving but loses its tide (Kilrush Lough in August 2025). In a prototype comparing each day's observed range with the predicted tide's range, Kilrush Lough had 19 days under half, including 6 August; Galway, Jersey, PEGELONLINE 9420020, Cyremort Point and MARKH had none. The same comparison would find a fouled stilling well, whose recorded tide shrinks and lags more each day until the well is cleaned ([shih-rogers-1981-stilling-well-errors.md](sources/shih-rogers-1981-stilling-well-errors.md), section 3.5).

### Timing and gaps

- **Practice.** QARTOD's timing and gap test marks a missing value when the next is late, and says it "does not address all clock drift/jump issues" ([ioos-qartod-water-level-v2.1.md](sources/ioos-qartod-water-level-v2.1.md), test 1). Clock errors are found in residuals, where "timing errors appear as tides in non-tidal residuals", and by lag correlation or M2 phase ([ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md](sources/ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md), sections 2.3 and 2.3.2; [vilibic-2006-timing-errors-split.md](sources/vilibic-2006-timing-errors-split.md)); Copernicus flags them by eye and says "this type of error is not flagged automatically" ([cmems-sea-level-rep-quid-2025.md](sources/cmems-sea-level-rep-quid-2025.md), section III.1.4). Even curated archives miss some ([pan-2025-timing-errors.md](sources/pan-2025-timing-errors.md); [thompson-2025-timing-errors-reply.md](sources/thompson-2025-timing-errors-reply.md)).
- **Stillwell.** Duplicate timestamps are dropped ([src/qc.ts:223-226](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)); stale stations are reported, not flagged ([src/lake/recent.ts:271-273](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/recent.ts)); cross-check measures tidal phase in 15-day windows and reports runs off the station's median ([src/cross-check.ts:118-176](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/cross-check.ts)) for three regions monthly.
- **Gap.** Matches practice in method (phase against a fit), short of it in coverage. Treating timing as a reviewed report, not an automatic flag, is what Copernicus does.

### Residual-based QC

- **Practice.** Every delayed-mode reference makes the non-tidal residual its main tool: "The primary quality control of sea level is based on the visual inspection of both recorded data and non-tidal residuals" ([ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md](sources/ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md), section 2.3.2; [seadatanet-qc-procedures-2010.md](sources/seadatanet-qc-procedures-2010.md), 5.4.1; [uhslc-caldwell-1998-processing-manual.md](sources/uhslc-caldwell-1998-processing-manual.md), 5.1; [bodc-uk-tide-gauge-network-2015.md](sources/bodc-uk-tide-gauge-network-2015.md), p. 7-10). SELENE reruns its spike test on residuals and flags a reference change the first pass missed ([ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md](sources/ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md), section 2.3.1). Copernicus says real surges "evolve more gradually" while bad data "present as abrupt changes in the non-tidal residual" ([cmems-reprocessing-lin-ye-2023.md](sources/cmems-reprocessing-lin-ye-2023.md), p. 1746-1747), and that computing residuals in near real time "would significantly improve the detection of incorrect data" ([cmems-sea-level-rep-quid-2025.md](sources/cmems-sea-level-rep-quid-2025.md), section III). CO-OPS checks each value against its prediction ([noaa-co-ops-tr-030-qc-requirements.md](sources/noaa-co-ops-tr-030-qc-requirements.md), p. 11), and BODC flags differences over about 20 mm with judgment ([bodc-uk-tide-gauge-network-2015.md](sources/bodc-uk-tide-gauge-network-2015.md)). Predictions are poor where tides are nonlinear or river-driven, and constants should be fitted fresh each year ([ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md](sources/ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md), section 2.3.2; [uhslc-caldwell-1998-processing-manual.md](sources/uhslc-caldwell-1998-processing-manual.md), 5.1).
- **Stillwell.** None in QC. The store already fits harmonics for each station's verdict ([src/tidal.ts:20-38](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/tidal.ts)) and for cross-check timing.
- **Gap.** The largest. A prototype jump-and-return rule on residuals (below) flagged the Jersey 0.0 m, Este's 3.20 m, 53 of the 60 values in Kilrush Lough's 9 January run and Galway's −32.758 m placeholders, and nothing at Jersey's 1,246 false positives or in any storm window.

### Comparative checks

- **Practice.** Buddy checks of daily or monthly means against neighbors are how small datum shifts and drift are found: PSMSL within 400 km ([psmsl-data-checks-rlr.md](sources/psmsl-data-checks-rlr.md), check 7), UHSLC on residuals and daily differences with a 1.5 cm rule of thumb ([uhslc-caldwell-1998-processing-manual.md](sources/uhslc-caldwell-1998-processing-manual.md), 5.2; [uhslc-jasl-quality-assessments.md](sources/uhslc-jasl-quality-assessments.md)), Copernicus within 0.1 to 0.5 degrees with 1.0 m and 0.5 m limits and a 100 mm per month gradient test for datum problems ([cmems-sea-level-rep-quid-2025.md](sources/cmems-sea-level-rep-quid-2025.md), section III.1.2), Hogarth's changepoints confirmed by a second source ([hogarth-2020-british-isles-datum-steps.md](sources/hogarth-2020-british-isles-datum-steps.md), sections 4.2 and 4.4), SHOM's mean-level differences ([shom-refmar-qc-2011.md](sources/shom-refmar-qc-2011.md), IV.C). For rivers, neighbor comparison works within a watershed with lags ([wmo-168.md](sources/wmo-168.md), 9.8). Datum problems go back to the originator first ([uhslc-jasl-rqds-ncei.md](sources/uhslc-jasl-rqds-ncei.md); [woce-sea-level-dacs-1997.md](sources/woce-sea-level-dacs-1997.md)). The gauge itself can bias every value: a float in a stilling well reads low in currents and waves, by up to about 20 cm in NOAA's 1981 case studies ([shih-rogers-1981-stilling-well-errors.md](sources/shih-rogers-1981-stilling-well-errors.md), p. vii; [shih-porter-1981-stilling-well-error-models.md](sources/shih-porter-1981-stilling-well-error-models.md)), and CO-OPS's acoustic gauges read a few centimeters lower than microwave beside them where there are waves or temperature gradients ([noaa-co-ops-tr-075-acoustic-microwave.md](sources/noaa-co-ops-tr-075-acoustic-microwave.md), pp. vii and 19). A bias like that passes every per-value test and shows only against another gauge, often as a step where a station changed sensors.
- **Stillwell.** Cross-check: hourly values, four nearest neighbors within 150 km, steps of 0.08 m and 3 times the spread seen by two neighbors, plus copied months and timing; reports only, three regions, monthly ([src/cross-check.ts:221-362](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/cross-check.ts), [.github/workflows/monthly.yml:285-321](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/.github/workflows/monthly.yml)). Errata follow by hand.
- **Gap.** The method matches practice; coverage and follow-through don't. The one store-wide run of `datumJumps` found 26 erroneous shifts that errata flag, and 7 real datum changes, which shows the yield of running checks everywhere.

### Real-time versus delayed mode

- **Practice.** Real-time QC is light and must not remove tsunamis or surges; delayed mode works on a year or more and adds residuals, buddy checks and human review ([ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md](sources/ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md), section 2.1; [woce-sea-level-dacs-1997.md](sources/woce-sea-level-dacs-1997.md)). QARTOD keeps real-time flags and writes post-processing results as "another set of flags" ([ioos-qartod-water-level-v2.1.md](sources/ioos-qartod-water-level-v2.1.md), section 3.1). Delayed mode flags far more than near real time: 1.9% against 0.3% at Copernicus ([cmems-reprocessing-lin-ye-2023.md](sources/cmems-reprocessing-lin-ye-2023.md), sections 3 and 4).
- **Stillwell.** One rule set for both: the recent window runs it over 7 days, backfill and requalify over a year with neighbors ([current-pipeline.md](current-pipeline.md#when-and-where-qc-runs-and-what-it-rewrites)). `source_flag` keeps the source's own verdict; `flag` is rewritten whenever rules change.
- **Gap.** The store has no delayed-mode tier: nothing runs only on whole years, where a tide fit and neighbor comparison are possible. Rewriting `flag` while keeping `source_flag` is sound; what's missing is a record of which rules wrote it.

### Never overwriting originals

- **Practice.** "The original series must always be preserved alongside any modified series" ([ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md](sources/ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md), section 2); SeaDataNet's flag 5 marks a changed value and asks to keep the old one ([seadatanet-qc-procedures-2010.md](sources/seadatanet-qc-procedures-2010.md), section 6); BoM keeps primary data unedited with traceable edits ([bom-national-industry-guidelines.md](sources/bom-national-industry-guidelines.md), Part 5); WMO archives unit values untouched ([wmo-1044.md](sources/wmo-1044.md), 6.5.3).
- **Stillwell.** QC never changes a level; `withSourceFlag` rounds to 0.1 mm ([src/qc.ts:47-54](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)). Adapters shift times for documented clock errors (for example [src/sources/gesla/index.ts:492-494](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/gesla/index.ts)).
- **Gap.** Small: corrected values carry no mark of the correction, and the original times are not kept.

### Flag scheme

- **Practice.** IOC MG 54 and QARTOD: 1 good, 2 not evaluated, 3 suspect, 4 bad, 9 missing, with optional secondary codes for which test failed ([ioc-mg54-v3-quality-flag-scheme.md](sources/ioc-mg54-v3-quality-flag-scheme.md), sections 5.1 and 5.2). QARTOD's "pass" means "adequate for use as preliminary data" ([ioos-qartod-water-level-v2.1.md](sources/ioos-qartod-water-level-v2.1.md), Table 3-1). SeaDataNet, EuroGOOS, MyOcean and Copernicus: 0 no QC, 1 good, 2 probably good, 3 probably bad or bad but correctable, 4 bad, 5 changed, 8 interpolated, 9 missing ([seadatanet-qc-procedures-2010.md](sources/seadatanet-qc-procedures-2010.md), section 6; [cmems-insitu-qc-procedure-2025.md](sources/cmems-insitu-qc-procedure-2025.md), Table 1). MG 83 drops 2 because schemes disagree on it ([ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md](sources/ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md), section 2.7). GESLA uses 2 for interpolated ([gesla3-haigh-2023.md](sources/gesla3-haigh-2023.md), p. 305).
- **Stillwell.** 0 good, 1 interpolated, 2 preliminary, 3 suspect, 4 bad ([src/schema.ts:7-13](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/schema.ts)).
- **Crosswalk.** Stillwell 0 is MG 54 1 and SeaDataNet 1. Stillwell 1 is SeaDataNet 8 and GESLA 2, and MG 54 1 with a secondary "interpolated" code. Stillwell 2, a value the source hasn't checked that passed the store's QC, is QARTOD's 1 "pass ... preliminary", closest to SeaDataNet 2. Stillwell 3 is MG 54 3 and SeaDataNet 3 (MG 83 "probably bad"; Copernicus reads 3 as bad but correctable). Stillwell 4 is 4 everywhere.
- **Gap.** The store's scale folds two axes, source verification and QC verdict, into one number, which is why a source-verified value that QC marks suspect needs `source_flag` to stay visible. That works. What practice has and the store lacks is a reason for each flag (MG 54 secondary codes). GESLA's 0, "no quality control" in every scheme ([gesla2-woodworth-2016.md](sources/gesla2-woodworth-2016.md), Table 1), becomes good ([src/sources/gesla/index.ts:440](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/gesla/index.ts)), which makes unchecked values look verified.

### Preliminary and verified

- **Practice.** A source's verification state is the source's: USGS Approved after a second hydrographer ([usgs-osw-records-policy.md](sources/usgs-osw-records-policy.md)), CO-OPS verified after a two-person review ([noaa-co-ops-data-flags-and-verification.md](sources/noaa-co-ops-data-flags-and-verification.md)), UHSLC research quality after fast delivery ([uhslc-jasl-rqds-ncei.md](sources/uhslc-jasl-rqds-ncei.md)), NEMS QC 200 until verified ([nems-water-level.md](sources/nems-water-level.md)).
- **Stillwell.** Preliminary values are refetched monthly over three years for sources that finalize ([src/sources/jobs.ts:38](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/jobs.ts), [src/lake/recent.ts:475](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/recent.ts)); a finalized value is never replaced by an unfinalized one ([src/lake/merge.ts:80-102](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/merge.ts)).
- **Gap.** Sound in design. Two mappings put the store's judgment in the source's place: CO-OPS verified values with F or R become suspect (above), and Kartverket values become good at a year old ([src/sources/kartverket/index.ts:58](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/kartverket/index.ts), [:289](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/sources/kartverket/index.ts)), an inference, not a source statement.

### Interpolated values

- **Practice.** Interpolation is limited to short gaps, under 10 to 25 minutes in high-rate data and under 24 or 25 hours in hourly data, and flagged ([ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md](sources/ioc-mg83-sea-level-qc-2020.md), sections 2.3.1 and 2.3.2; [uhslc-jasl-rqds-ncei.md](sources/uhslc-jasl-rqds-ncei.md)); never across storms or with predictions ([ioc-mg14-sea-level-manual.md](sources/ioc-mg14-sea-level-manual.md), Volume I; [psmsl-data-checks-rlr.md](sources/psmsl-data-checks-rlr.md)); a lake with seiche is never filled ([wmo-168.md](sources/wmo-168.md), 9.7.2). Copernicus counts interpolated values as good ([cmems-insitu-qc-procedure-2025.md](sources/cmems-insitu-qc-procedure-2025.md), Table 1); TICON uses them in harmonic fits ([ticon-piccioni-2019.md](sources/ticon-piccioni-2019.md), p. 99).
- **Stillwell.** The store never interpolates; sources' interpolated or estimated codes map to 1 (BODC T, GESLA 2, DWR 70 and 76, USACE replacement, USGS `ESTIMATED`, SFWMD `E`, CO-OPS I), and QC treats them like any other value.
- **Gap.** None that matters. USGS "estimated" can be a model fill, not an interpolation; flag 1 doesn't tell them apart.

### River and lake stage

- **Practice.** River stage QC is per-site thresholds from history (high, low, maximum step, longest flat spot) plus review ([wmo-1044.md](sources/wmo-1044.md), 6.5.3; [usgs-wrd-99-34-realtime-qa.md](sources/usgs-wrd-99-34-realtime-qa.md)); QARTOD's stream flow manual adds flat line with "repeated zero or other SF values may be accurate" ([ioos-qartod-stream-flow.md](sources/ioos-qartod-stream-flow.md)). Neighbors work within a watershed with lags ([wmo-168.md](sources/wmo-168.md), 9.8). Lakes have wind tilt and seiche ([nems-water-level.md](sources/nems-water-level.md), 1.6). Datum errors develop over months and only levelling finds them ([wmo-1044.md](sources/wmo-1044.md), 6.5.4; [usgs-tm3a7.md](sources/usgs-tm3a7.md)).
- **Stillwell.** Still water gets 48-hour flatlines, no outlier or held rule, the spike rule and the 20 m limit ([src/lake/recent.ts:818-828](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/recent.ts)).
- **Gap.** No per-site range or step limits from history. Within the store's means that is the main thing missing for rivers; the 48-hour flatline is reasonable against WMO's "longest unchanged periods" approach, which sets no number.

## Efficiency

Measured in Node on this machine: `qc` takes 1.37 s for a year of 1-minute values (525,600), 0.26 s for 5-minute and 0.04 s for 15-minute; a harmonic fit with the standard set on that year's hourly values takes 0.4 to 1.0 s; predicting every 1-minute value takes 7.9 s, which hourly prediction and interpolation would cut by about 60 times. The store holds 281,983 year files and 8.01 billion values, 2.68 billion at PEGELONLINE, 2.04 billion at USGS and 1.69 billion at GESLA.

- **Hourly recent window.** Every hour reruns QC over 7 days and rewrites each source's recent file ([src/lake/recent.ts:104-285](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/recent.ts)), so each value is judged about 168 times. Compute is small against the read and write; the cost is the file rewrite, which ingest needs anyway. Rerunning QC on the whole window is what lets a mark clear, so this is proportionate.
- **Compaction.** Rewrites each touched year file whole every day ([src/lake/recent.ts:299-470](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/recent.ts)); QC adds nothing to it. The rewrite is a storage choice outside QC.
- **Requalify on a `RULES` bump.** Every station of every source goes pending ([src/lake/analysis.ts:330](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/lake/analysis.ts)), and `analyze --requalify` starts batches of 100 stations for 120 minutes per source per month ([scripts/ingest.ts:922-944](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/scripts/ingest.ts)). Reading 8 billion values to rerun a rule on a subset of stations is out of proportion, and for USGS, PEGELONLINE and GESLA it leaves stored years under the old rules for months while recent and backfill use the new ones. Nothing records which rules wrote a file, so a reader can't tell.
- **Analysis.** Two years per due station and two `qc` passes plus a fit; small.
- **Cross-check.** Rereads six years of hourly values per station every month with no cache in the workflow ([.github/workflows/monthly.yml:300-321](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/.github/workflows/monthly.yml)). Small today, large if extended to every station.
- **Cheaper designs with the same or better quality.** A residual-based rule needs one fit per station-year, about 1 s, which analysis and cross-check already compute in other forms; storing each station-year's fitted constants once would serve QC, `tideOf` and cross-check timing. Daily means (and daily mean residuals) per station-year, computed when a year file is written, would let neighbor and datum-jump checks run over the whole store from kilobytes instead of rereading hourly series. Per-file rules stamps would let a rule bump requalify only the files whose rules changed and let a reader see what produced a flag.

### Design changes

5. **Add residual-based checks to stored-year QC.**
   - Problem: offset runs, placeholder values and level shifts pass, and residual work is the core of every delayed-mode reference.
   - Evidence: [Offset runs and level shifts](#offset-runs-and-level-shifts).
   - Practice: [Residual-based QC](#residual-based-qc).
   - Change, the rule as prototyped ([scripts/residual-prototype.ts](scripts/residual-prototype.ts)):
     - Fit the standard set once to the year's values on the hour whose flag after `fromSource` is better than suspect and that lie within 10 m of the year's median level. The residual is each value less the fit's prediction.
     - A step is the change in residual between consecutive values at most the larger of three median intervals and 30 minutes apart. J is the larger of 0.5 m and 10 times the scaled median absolute deviation of all such steps in the year.
     - A step larger than J opens a run, unless the value before it is already in one. The run closes at the first value within 24 hours of the opening whose step is the other way, larger than half the opening, and brings the residual back to within half the opening of the residual before it; the run is the values from the opening to the one before the close. A gap (a step longer than the limit above) met first ends the run at the value before the gap. A run that neither closes nor meets a gap within 24 hours, or reaches the end of the year, is not flagged.
     - The run is flagged suspect when every residual in it is within half the opening of the run's median residual. The value after a flagged run's last cannot open another.
   - On the evidence, as prototyped: Jersey 2025, 1 value (the 0.0 m); Kilrush Lough 2025, 53 of the 60 values of the 9 January run and 55 in the year; Este 2025, 108 values, 89 of them already flagged and including the 3.20 m; Galway 2025, the 43 −32.758 m placeholders; Cyremort Point 2019, 1 value already flagged. German Bight storms of 2 to 8 January 2025 at PEGELONLINE 9420020, Eowyn and Amy at Galway, Barry at Cyremort Point and Ian at MARKH: none. Kilrush Lough's August drift is not a jump and is not flagged.
   - Beyond the prototype, untested: running it only in `requalify` and backfill on whole years, only for tidal stations; fitting to values the store's current rules and errata leave usable rather than the source's flags alone; and refitting once without values beyond 5 times the residual spread. The prototype's fit at Kilrush Lough (RMS 1.47 m, against 0.16 m at Galway) shows why a cleaner fit is worth testing.
   - Risk: in eleven storm and hurricane windows the prototype flagged nothing, and the largest residual step in each was 0.04 m (German Bight at 9420020, 2 to 8 January 2025, 1-minute), 0.31 m (Eowyn at Galway, 5-minute), 0.14 m (Amy at Galway), 0.33 m (Barry at Cyremort Point, hourly), 0.03 m (Ian at MARKH), 0.06 m (Sally at Pensacola), 0.12 m and 0.18 m (Sally and Delta at Dauphin Island), 0.28 m and 0.08 m (Zeta and Ida at Shell Beach), and 0.03 m and 0.02 m (Barry at Carrollton and Berwick). The margin under the 0.5 m floor is smallest at hourly and 5-minute stations. Meteotsunamis and harbor seiches can step faster at 1-minute sampling; none of the evaluated stations has them, so the floor should be checked against a seiche-prone 1-minute station before release. A real rise of more than J followed within a day by an outage, with the residual held near its new level, would be flagged to the gap; gauges often fail in storms, and none of the eleven windows has a gap after a jump. Barrier and lock gauges (Este's inner gauge) hold levels the tide doesn't predict; the held-offset condition kept the prototype off Este's barrier closures.
   - Tests: new ones; none change, since the rule is new code.
   - Effort: a few days, plus a one-time requalify. About 1 s per station-year for the fit.
6. **Add an attenuated-signal test on residuals.**
   - Problem: a sensor that loses the tide without going flat passes.
   - Practice: QARTOD test 10, Copernicus's attenuation test ([cmems-sea-level-rep-quid-2025.md](sources/cmems-sea-level-rep-quid-2025.md), section III.1.2).
   - Change, as prototyped: with the fit of recommendation 5, mark suspect a UTC day whose observed range, over values within 10 m of the year's median, is under half the predicted range, on days with more than 80% of their expected values. Beyond the prototype, untested: limiting it to tidal stations with `tide_m` of 0.3 m or more.
   - On the evidence, as prototyped: Kilrush Lough 2025, 19 days, including 6 August and days inside the September to November erratum; none at Galway, Jersey, 9420020, Cyremort Point or MARKH.
   - Risk: a closed barrier or a frozen harbor also loses its tide, and should be flagged or known. Not tested at stations with strong river flow.
   - Tests: new ones; none change.
   - Effort: a day, with recommendation 5.
7. **Run `datumJumps` and the neighbor step check over every record, and flag what they find for review.**
   - Problem: level shifts are found only by one-off runs.
   - Evidence: one run found 26 erroneous shifts, now flagged errata, and 7 real datum changes.
   - Practice: [Comparative checks](#comparative-checks); Copernicus flags a potential datum problem and confirms it by eye.
   - Change: monthly, after compaction, run `datumJumps` on every coastal record and cross-check steps on every region, and write findings to a reviewed list (or as draft errata) rather than to flags. Keep the 0.75 m rule as is; add neighbor steps at 0.08 m where a neighbor exists.
   - Risk: none to stored flags until a finding is accepted. Rivers need lags and a watershed, and stay out.
   - Tests: new ones for the job; none change, since flags don't.
   - Effort: days; the rules exist.
8. **Tolerant flatline for tidal stations.**
   - Problem: [finding 2](#suspected-gaps). The held rule passes on any run within 1 cm of its first value that lasts `flatline_s` ([src/qc.ts:165-172](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)), and the flatline rule judges only exactly equal runs ([:116](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/src/qc.ts)), so a run that wavers but stays within 1 cm for six hours is judged by neither.
   - Change: in the flatline rule, count values within 2 mm of a run's first value as one run, for tidal stations only, through a new `qcFor` option; keep exact equality at still water. The held rule is unchanged, so each run is still judged by one rule: under six hours by the held rule, six hours or more by the flatline rule. Runs that waver by 2 mm to 1 cm for six hours stay unjudged.
   - Practice: QARTOD's EPS of 1 mm between values; measured from the run's first value, a ±1 mm wobble spans 2 mm.
   - On the evidence, with the change applied to a copy of the repository: the synthetic ±1 mm run goes from 1 of 80 values flagged to 80 of 80; on 12 real station-years (Jersey, Kilrush Lough, Galway, Este and 9420020 2025, MARKH, Cyremort Point, Pensacola, Shell Beach 2020, Carrollton, Berwick and EA E71924) it raises no new flag. A 1 cm tolerance, matching the held rule, flagged 11,369 values at Berwick in 2019, 760 at Carrollton and 83 and 88 at Pensacola and Shell Beach in 2020, where small tides stay within a centimeter for hours, so it is not proposed.
   - Tests: [`test/analysis.test.ts`](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/test/analysis.test.ts) "gives tidal water six hours and still water two days, by verdict, then by kind", which compares `qcFor`'s whole options, fails until the new option is added to its expectations; the other three QC test files pass.
   - Effort: an hour, under the design bump.
9. **Per-station range limits from history.**
   - Practice: [Range and climatology](#range-and-climatology).
   - Change: in the years file or analysis file, keep each station's 0.1st and 99.9th percentile of usable levels over its last ten years; mark suspect beyond them by more than the station's tidal range plus 1 m, never bad. Then keep the outlier rule only for the recent window, where it catches a pool-style rise that nothing else does ([`test/lake.test.ts:415-424`](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/test/lake.test.ts#L415-L424)); in year windows it added no flag in 17 station-years.
   - Risk: a record flood or surge beyond history plus the margin; suspect, not bad, as QARTOD's climatology test does.
   - Tests: not run. Keeping the outlier rule in the recent window keeps [`test/lake.test.ts:415-424`](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/test/lake.test.ts#L415-L424) as it is; making the outlier rule depend on the window would need a new `QcOptions` field, which the `qcFor` expectations in [`test/analysis.test.ts`](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/test/analysis.test.ts) would have to add.
   - Effort: a few days.
10. **Stamp each year file with the `RULES` that wrote it, and run requalify as a sharded job.** Adds a column to the years file; a bump then requalifies only stale files, readers can see the rules, and the 120-minute budget stops being the limit. Tests: not run; [`test/years.test.ts`](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/test/years.test.ts), which reads the years file's rows, would gain the column. Effort: a day or two.
11. **Give each flag a reason.** A small bitmask column (limit, flatline, spike, held, outlier, erratum, residual jump, attenuation, source) as MG 54's secondary codes. Tests: not run; the tests that write and read year files ([`test/lake.test.ts`](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/test/lake.test.ts), [`test/requalify.test.ts`](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/test/requalify.test.ts), [`test/recent.test.ts`](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/test/recent.test.ts), [`test/years.test.ts`](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/blob/4caa051e/test/years.test.ts)) compare whole rows and would gain the column. Effort: schema change and a requalify; worth doing with recommendation 10.

QARTOD's worked example flags clipped highs ([ioos-qartod-water-level-v2.1.md](sources/ioos-qartod-water-level-v2.1.md), section 3.3.2). A barrier holds real water that isn't the sea's.

- Should R on a verified CO-OPS value stop making it suspect, as L does under erratum `coops-stale-limit-flag`? At Dauphin Island in October 2020 the 175 verified R values sit a median of 3.1 cm and at most 14.8 cm from their neighbors' mean, against 1.2 cm and 15.9 cm for unflagged values, on storm days; across the erratum's seven stations and three Marches there is no verified R at all ([scripts/coops-r.py](scripts/coops-r.py)). A decision needs R counts and distances from the stations and years where R does appear, such as the Gulf coast's storm seasons.

## Part 5 minimum requirements (section 2, pp. 8 to 9)

- All primary measured data, including telemetered data, "shall be considered unvalidated data until it has been reviewed and validated against the spot check (observed reference field visit values)."
- Reference readings (spot checks) are captured "at regular intervals (at least annually)."
- Validation checks primary data against a calibrated reference value and checks logger time against local time, before a quality code is assigned. Comments recorded with the data are reviewed in validation.
- Primary measured data are permanently kept unedited. Every transfer, validation, edit, correction and estimation (for example correlation between adjacent stations or numerical simulation) is traceable with author, date and time stamp and an ability to undo, and every checked, edited, corrected or estimated value gets a quality code.
- Staff doing editing and estimation must be shown competent. A recommendation: the data system supports independent audit.

## Part 1 metadata and quality codes (sections 2 and 3, pp. 8 to 10)

- Required metadata includes units and recording interval, purpose of collection, site elevation and datum (for example AHD), instrument history and calibration, the time zone, and "necessary reference data to be able to convert level data to a standard datum." The tolerance achieved should be stated (for example plus or minus 0.01 m or 1 percent of range).
- Quality codes follow the Water Data Transfer Format 1.0.2 list: quality-A (best available given the technologies and objectives), quality-B (compromised in its ability to represent the parameter), quality-C (an estimate), quality-E (ability to represent the parameter is not known), quality-F (not of release quality, missing, void or known to be incorrect). quality-D is withdrawn. A code depends on the purpose the data were collected for.

## Part 2 items on datum and verification (sections 4.1.2 to 4.1.7, pp. 9 to 11)

- "All level measurements shall be taken relative to a datum," and a local datum is referenced to the Australian Height Datum (or AHD Tasmania) for national comparison. Points referenced to a datum are surveyed regularly.
- Inspection and maintenance at least once a year. Triggers for data capture are defined in writing.
- Verification means checking that the collected data are supported by spot checks "through the likely range of measurement," using site visits, independent observers, duplicate transducers or remote cameras.
- When an instrument fails or operates out of tolerance, the reading found, actions taken and results are recorded, and metadata for the affected data are tagged.

## Not covered

No numeric limits for range, rate of change, spikes or flat lines, no neighbor comparison, and no lake-specific content in the parts reviewed.

# Altimeter drift from tide gauges with GPS land motion (Watson et al. 2015)

## Citation

Watson, C.S., White, N.J., Church, J.A., King, M.A., Burgette, R.J., and Legresy, B., 2015, Unabated global mean sea-level rise over the satellite altimeter era, Nature Climate Change, 5, 565 to 568.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/nclimate2635

## License and local copy

Subscription article. Repository copies (University of Tasmania figshare 22924433, Newcastle University 34270947) are marked "In Copyright" and have no file attached. No PDF; link only. Only the abstract was read; the methods summary below comes from the abstract and is otherwise unverified.

## What it is (abstract)

The comparison runs the other way from tide gauge QC: tide gauges, corrected for vertical land motion (VLM) with GPS, are used as the reference to find instrumental drift in satellite altimeters. Satellite global mean sea level estimates "do not include an allowance for potential instrumental drifts (bias drift)." The authors report "improved bias drift estimates for individual altimeter missions from a refined estimation approach that incorporates new Global Positioning System (GPS) estimates of vertical land movement (VLM)" and find "significant non-zero systematic drifts that are satellite-specific, most notably affecting the first 6 years" of the record. Correcting them lowers the 1993 to mid-2014 rate from 3.2 ± 0.4 mm/yr to between 2.6 ± 0.4 and 2.9 ± 0.4 mm/yr, "depending on the choice of VLM applied."

## Relevance to gauge QC

The same altimetry minus gauge difference series is used in the other direction to check gauges (see [woppelmann-marcos-2016-vertical-land-motion.md](woppelmann-marcos-2016-vertical-land-motion.md) and [hogarth-2020-british-isles-datum-steps.md](hogarth-2020-british-isles-datum-steps.md)). In both directions the result depends on the VLM estimate; the abstract states the corrected rate changes with "the choice of VLM applied."

## Not verified

A search summary of the supplementary methods said it used hourly fast-delivery GLOSS gauge data and 1 Hz Jason-class altimetry, with solid Earth tides removed from the altimeter heights. This was not checked against the paper. No gauge selection rules, distances, or thresholds were read.

## Verification of unit values (6.5.3, pp. II.6-9 to II.6-11)

Unit values (regularly recorded stage, velocity and so on) "always should be carefully inspected and verified." Four methods: threshold comparisons, rating comparisons, direct reading comparisons, graphical methods. The original unit values are archived untouched before any editing, and all verification and editing is done on a copy.

Threshold set, run automatically on a period of record:

- (a) high-value threshold
- (b) low-value threshold
- (c) maximum difference threshold (change between consecutive values)
- (d) flat-spot threshold, the maximum time for constant values

Setting thresholds: high and low limits "at or near" the maximum and minimum values experienced in the past three to five years of record, and the difference threshold at or near the largest valid difference in the same period. The system should list the 20 highest and 20 lowest peak unit values, the 20 greatest differences between consecutive unit values, and the 20 longest periods with no change, and the thresholds are reviewed every three years or when changing them. If limits are set so tight that many in-range values fail, other verification methods are applied to failed values. USGS web display uses thresholds as filters: values outside them are not shown but stay in the database.

- Rating comparison: flags unit values above or below the range of the rating in use (a possible bad value or a needed rating extension).
- Direct readings: observer and hydrographer gage readings, max/min indicators, high-water marks, crest-stage gages and auxiliary or backup gages are plotted with the recorded series.
- Graphics are "the most important and easily used method": the base plot overlays thresholds, rating limits, direct readings and auxiliary records, and highlights values that trip the difference and flat-spot thresholds. Air temperature, precipitation and water temperature help judge ice effects.

## Time (6.5.1 to 6.5.2, pp. II.6-7 to II.6-9)

Clock error is prorated linearly between correct time readings; dropping or adding values to fix a time error "should not be used." Daylight saving changes are not a clock error. All unit values are stored in UTC (days with a time change then contain 23 or 25 hours). Satellite DCP times are treated as correct.

## Corrections (6.5.4, pp. II.6-10 to II.6-13)

- An erroneous reading is one where the recorder does not record the true value, judged against the reference gage. Instrument errors (malfunction, setting, calibration) usually appear over days or weeks and are found by comparison with the reference gage. Datum errors (a change in the reference gage) usually develop over months or years and are found only by running levels to a stable benchmark. They are corrected separately but with the same methods.
- Datum adjustments are prorated uniformly over the period when the time of the change is unknown, or applied from a known time. "Corrections for gauge-datum errors of 0.02 ft, or 0.006 mm [sic, evidently 0.006 m], or less are not applied" except where small errors matter (for example reservoir contents), since ratings and shifts absorb them.
- Conversion to a common datum (for example mean sea level) adds a constant. A gage-movement correction may be needed at the same time.
- Instrument corrections are constant, parameter-variable (for example stage-dependent drawdown from high velocity at an intake) or time-variable (proration), and are additive where they overlap.

## Flags (6.5.4, pp. II.6-13 to II.6-14)

Four recommended unit-value flags:

- Affected: values are correct and representative of true stage, but the rating is severely affected (backwater from irregular downstream conditions, ice). Not for normal shifting control.
- Erroneous: incorrect values, for example a float resting on mud in the stilling well.
- Missing: values expected but not recorded because of equipment malfunction.
- Estimated: attached automatically to values changed by the hydrographer. Estimated values are only in data sets generated after the original.

Affected, erroneous and missing flags describe the original archived values. Values flagged Affected or Erroneous are not used in primary computations. Where unit values cannot be corrected, daily values may be estimated instead.

## What it says

CO-OPS ran acoustic (Aquatrak in a protective well) and microwave radar sensors side by side at four NWLON stations, Monterey, La Jolla, Duck and Lake Worth, from April 2012 to November 2013, as the network moved its primary sensor to microwave.

- With little wave energy the two agree statistically; with waves, "a persistent bias was noted with acoustic water level estimates lower than that of the microwave sensor" (executive summary, p. vii).
- Most differences are errors in the acoustic system: speed-of-sound errors from temperature gradients along the sounding tube, wave-induced drawdown across the protective well's orifice, and resonance inside the well (conclusion, p. 20).
- Temperature errors of 5 cm and more are common at La Jolla and Duck, and 2 to 4 cm at Lake Worth (p. 19).
- The drawdown model is the one from [shih-rogers-1981-stilling-well-errors.md](shih-rogers-1981-stilling-well-errors.md); it accounts for one half to three fourths of the negative differences (figure 5, p. 6; pp. 13 and 19).
- The microwave sensor has its own limits, such as sidelobe interference, false targets and scattering in heavy rain, and the report is not a general recommendation to replace acoustic sensors (p. vii).

## Relevance to Stillwell

- CO-OPS records carry sensor-specific biases of a few centimeters that per-value QC cannot see. They show as a step or a change in noise where a station changed sensors, which is what a comparison with neighbors or with a second sensor finds.
- The six-minute standard deviation that CO-OPS reports tracks wave height (p. 20), so a jump in it can mark a sensor change as well as a storm.

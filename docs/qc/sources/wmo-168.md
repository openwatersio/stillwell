## Flags (section 9.3.5, p. I.9-4)

Qualification codes are single characters in two groups: status of the value and background conditions. Status flags "typically" include E (estimated value), U (uncertain, thought to be incorrect but no means to verify), G (value greater than calibration or measurement limit, set to the limit) and L (below detection limit, set to the limit). Missing data use a distinct code, either a flag M or a physically impossible value such as -999 (9.3.6). Modified data are to be "suitably flagged and/or comments filed" (9.5.6).

## Automated validation (section 9.8, pp. I.9-17 to I.9-19)

- Validation can never be fully automated: "the hydrologist need not check the flagged values" is not achievable; people accept, reject or correct flagged values, and the most extreme values "may prove to be correct and, if so, are vitally important."
- Absolute checks catch impossible values. Relative checks are (a) expected ranges, (b) maximum expected change between successive observations, and (c) maximum expected difference between adjacent stations.
- Tolerances start broad and are tightened as statistics accumulate, but not so broad that no flags result. Expected ranges are computed for several time aggregations (daily values against the month's expected range, monthly means against long-term monthly means), because variance falls as aggregation grows.
- Method (b), comparing a value with the preceding value(s), "is of particular relevance to variables exhibiting significant serial correlation, for example, most types of water-level data."
- Method (c), the neighbor comparison, "is particularly effective for river-stage (and river-flow) values from the same watershed, although with larger watersheds some means of lagging data will be necessary before inter-station comparisons are made."
- Regression of related variables (water level against rainfall) is a physio-statistical check, useful in sparse networks.
- Visual review of plots by experienced staff "is a very rapid and effective technique." Overplotting adjacent stations is the simplest inter-station check.

## Water-level series checks (9.6.2.1, p. I.9-14)

At the start of each batch, confirm start date, time and values match the previous batch's end, and get maximum and minimum gage heights for an initial range check. Plot the batch and look for:

- Blocked intake pipes or stilling wells: "rounded-off peaks and recessions that are unusually flat."
- Spikes: "a small number of values … obviously out of context … due to an unnaturally large change in value between adjacent data points," such as digital errors between sensor and logger.
- Gaps, with known explanations and recorded remedial action.
- Errors induced by field staff (for example pumping water into a well), restricted float or counterweight movement, vandalism or animals, and debris or backwater at the control.
- If the cause is not positively identified, full processing is delayed until it can be investigated on site.

## Stage verification (9.9.2, pp. I.9-20 to I.9-21)

Stage is checked separately and first, before flow, because flow carries rating error. Recommended minimum techniques: (a) compare with manual readings at the start and end of each batch and at intermediate visits; (b) plot stage with overplots of other stage values in the database (gaugings, water quality visits); (c) qualitative review for suspicious straight lines, steps, spikes, or freshets and recessions where not expected. Streamflow checks (9.9.1) add detecting steps larger than a nominated value (which can vary with stage and "will also normally detect spikes"), detecting missed values the software may interpolate, and listing periods of straight lines in a stage record longer than a user-set length ("can detect excessive compression or erroneous interpolation across gaps").

Diagnosis guide for anomalies:

- Sluggish peak and recession: silt-blocked stilling well or intake.
- Steps or spikes: sensor, float, logger or recorder malfunction or interference.
- A batch higher or lower than both neighbors: wrong corrections against manual readings or sensor offset.
- Straight lines: missing data wrongly interpolated, or instrument trouble such as a sticking float cable or the minimum or maximum range reached.
- "Uphill recessions" (rising between freshets): wrong stage corrections, or weed or sediment build-up on the channel.
- Regular daily fluctuations: sensor problems (moisture in a pressure system), icing on the control, or something real such as channel evaporation or daily freeze-thaw in the catchment.

## Adjustments, ice and datum (9.5.6, p. I.9-9; 5.1.3.1, p. I.5-2)

- Drift in clocks or sensors can be prorated automatically; discrete events (clock stoppage, electronic fault) may allow manual estimates. Ice at gauging stations means "corrected stage values would be manually computed for the affected period," under strictly controlled assumptions.
- Before adjusting a batch ask whether the reasons are valid, whether adjustments are consistent with the previous batch, and whether field follow-up is needed.
- Gauge datum: set the zero below the lowest anticipated stage, check the datum periodically, and tie the local datum to a national or regional datum (chapter 5). The datum also should be checked with periodic discharge measurements (5.4.5).

## Gaps (section 9.7.2, p. I.9-16)

Gaps are generally not filled with synthetic or interpolated data (approximate data are referenced in a comment). A gap in a water-level record may be filled with a straight line or curve only if all of these hold: the river is in a natural recession, with the level lower or equal at the end; no significant rain fell in the time of concentration; the catchment has no abstractions or discharges that modify natural flow; the plot is consistent with data on either side; an adjacent-station fill is allowed only if it is effectively the same record or correlation is 0.99 or higher; and "the station is not a lake that normally has seiche or wind tilt (these are often studied, and a synthetic record will not be able to recreate the phenomena)." If trained staff were on site for the whole period, manual observations may fill the gap. All estimated data are flagged or kept in a separate archive.

## Not covered

No numeric range or rate limits (they are site-derived), no thresholds for flat-line duration, no treatment of pools or regulated lakes, and no quantitative accuracy requirement for stage (see [wmo-1044.md](wmo-1044.md) and [usgs-tm3a7.md](usgs-tm3a7.md)).

# Timing errors in global sea level observations (Pan et al. 2025)

## Citation

Pan, H., Wang, D., Li, B., Xu, T., and Wei, Z., 2025, Timing errors in global sea level observations, Ocean Dynamics, 75, article 5 (published January 2025).

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10236-024-01652-5

## License and local copy

Subscription article, not open access (OpenAlex lists it as closed). No PDF; link only. Only the abstract was read, from the publisher page. Everything about the detection algorithm below is unverified.

## What it is (abstract)

The authors examined 595 tide gauges in the UHSLC research quality (hourly) dataset with "a newly developed detection algorithm" and report "significant timing errors in 35 tide gauges," errors that could change "tidal amplitude estimates of main constituents by more than 10 mm." At Tanjong Pagar and Manila, "abnormal tidal amplitude changes caused by timing errors can be larger than 50 mm." They also report "datum shifts and other exceptions." They conclude that "when analyzing 'science-ready' sea level observations, caution is warranted. Anomalous variations in sea levels and tidal parameters may not reflect physical phenomena but rather artifacts stemming from multifarious observation errors."

## Method

Not verified. The abstract does not describe the algorithm, the window length, or the threshold. The effect it reports (timing errors showing up as changes in fitted tidal amplitudes) is consistent with the mechanism in [vilibic-2006-timing-errors-split.md](vilibic-2006-timing-errors-split.md), where timing errors move tidal energy into the residual and reduce fitted amplitudes.

## Response

UHSLC, PSMSL, GESLA, and Copernicus authors published a reply arguing that most of the flagged cases were already documented in the station metadata. See [thompson-2025-timing-errors-reply.md](thompson-2025-timing-errors-reply.md).

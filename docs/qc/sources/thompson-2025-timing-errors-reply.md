## What it is

A reply from the curators of the UHSLC research quality dataset (RQ/JASL) and colleagues at PSMSL, BODC, Puertos del Estado, GESLA, and NCEI. It is the clearest statement of how the main hourly archive treats errors it finds but cannot fix: it documents them in metadata rather than altering or deleting the values.

## How RQ/JASL handles errors (section 2, pp. 2 to 3)

The goal of RQ/JASL quality assurance "is to identify problems in data submitted to RQ/JASL and, whenever possible, work with the data originator to assess the history of the station (e.g., natural events, changes in instrumentation, leveling records, etc.) and have the originator rectify errors at the source when possible. When errors cannot be addressed by the originator, they are documented in the metadata accompanying each record." Unaltered data are preserved where "the exact cause and/or effect of an observational error is not certain," so users can choose how to handle them. "Each sea-level record in the RQ/JASL database has an associated metadata file that describes potential problems and flags questionable data." The dataset "is not guaranteed to be free of errors."

At the time of writing RQ/JASL held nearly 170 million hourly values (more than 19,000 years) from 548 sites in 92 countries.

## Response to the timing-error claims (section 3, p. 3; Table 1)

Table 1 lists each station Pan et al. flagged for timing errors with the matching metadata note. In "almost every case" the metadata already documented the issue or flagged the same stretch as questionable. The notes show the kinds of timing problems recorded and their sizes, for example:

- Churchill: "the timing is off occasionally by about fifteen-minutes."
- Legaspi: "A timing offset of 30 minutes exists from 16Z 31 Jan - 15Z 11 Feb 1985. The timing has small offsets of 5-10 minutes during various periods throughout the record. 2014 has timing offset of -30 min to one hour."
- Buenaventura: residuals "show a large amount of periodic fluctuations due to both the inability of the tidal analysis to resolve completely all the tidal constituents and to phase drifts."
- Tanjong Pagar: "The residual series are very noisy, which is due to shallow water tides that are not resolved by the harmonic analysis or due to timing drifts of less than 15 minutes."
- Newlyn: "Prior to 1985, many time spans have questionable timing quality."

Two of the flagged periods (Mar del Plata, Male) were "Fast Delivery" data, not research quality. Several stations (Madeira, Santana) are "near a wide, strong river" with effects on tides and timing.

Scale: the 54 station-years Pan et al. highlighted are under 0.3% of the dataset, "and the actual fraction of affected data is even less given that the durations of significant timing issues are generally much shorter than a year." Most unresolved timing issues are in the 20th century and "do not affect calculations of modern tidal datums."

## Response to the datum claims (p. 3; Table 2)

Three of four "datum shifts and other exceptions" were already fixed or documented. Examples: Santana, where segments for 1975 to 76, 1984 to 85, 1996 to 97, and 2006 "each appear to have independent datums; thus, the series are broken into unique pieces"; Cristobal, built from three relocated segments linked by levelling, where the metadata caution that one link "appears suspiciously high on the order of 60 mm."

## Recommendation (section 4, p. 4)

Users who believe they have found unknown errors should contact the curators or data originators, so the issue can be fixed at the source or added to the metadata.

## Not covered

The reply does not describe UHSLC's detection methods or thresholds. The station notes it quotes show residual noise and phase drift in the residuals being used as timing evidence.

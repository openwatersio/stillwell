## Summary of the SW2 approval checklist

The checklist is what a second hydrographer verifies before stage is set to Approved. Section numbers are the checklist's own.

1. Field and level notes reviewed and archived; station description updated.
2. Levels. The period should not be approved if levels are overdue or invalid. Frequency: every year for new sites until 3 sets of levels are run (also for new reference gage installations), every year where a datum correction was found from the prior levels, every 3 years for long-term sites, every 5 years for long-term stable sites (documented stability). Levels follow TM 3-A19 (Kenney 2010). The checklist text also cites "T&M 3-A19" for the level standard; the companion analyze-approve-audit document says "T&M 3-A10," which looks like a typo for 3-A19 (not confirmed). A datum correction of 0.015 ft or more must be entered in correction set 1, must match the difference between gage datum and the reference gage found in levels, must have a stated and plausible cause, and must be applied (prorated or constant) in a way consistent with that cause. If it reaches into previously approved data, the revision criteria apply.
3. Gage-height edits. Erroneous recorded values are removed and the basis is explained. Backup data are used to fill transmission gaps where available. Ice-affected recorded gage heights are identified.
4. Gage-height corrections (correction set 2) agree with the differences between reference gage and recorder; timing of the correction is justified. "Larger corrections (> 0.03 ft)" need detailed discussion; blanket statements are acceptable for small instrument drift.
5. Other corrections (set 3): flushing or purge corrections must match pre- and post-flush differences between reference gage and recorder; drawdown corrections must come from direct observations of reference gage and recorder over a range of stage.
6. Peak stage verified per OSW Technical Memorandum 14.06 (independent peak value and device uncertainty), and compared with prior peaks in the water year.
7. Estimates are appropriate and documented.
8. Daily values checked for accuracy, completeness and correct qualifiers.
   9 to 11. Manuscript elements updated; written approval summary with a quality rating of the stage record; operational follow-up.

## Process document (analyze, approve, audit)

- The analyst merges backup time-series data to fill gaps and documents the source, the reason for the gap and the period.
- The analyst examines recorded gage height for erroneous values and "edits should be made to erroneous values to remove them from further analysis." Remaining gaps are documented, and the record quality for the period is stated explicitly.
- Ice-affected periods are listed in the station analysis.
- Gage-height corrections compare every reference gage reading with the recorded value.
- Estimates for gaps follow best practices (the document says "reference pending policy release"); estimated periods and methods are documented.
- Audits: "A minimum of 10 percent of a WSC's stage or elevation records should be audited at intervals of about 1 year or less," and no more than 90 percent of the data may stay unaudited longer than fifteen months. The audit rate rises if significant issues are found.

## Lake and reservoir records (SW4)

The SW4 checklists repeat the SW2 structure: levels frequency, datum corrections of 0.015 ft or more, gage-height edits, backup-data gap filling and ice-affected periods. They add reservoir content (storage) computation items. No lake-specific numeric thresholds (seiche, wind tilt) appear in either set.

## Not covered

No automated screening rules (ranges, rates, flat lines) and no flag scheme. These are human review checklists over data that have already been screened.

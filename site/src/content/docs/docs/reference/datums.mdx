A level is a height above a reference, its datum. Each source measures from its own: `NAP` in the Netherlands, `PNP` (gauge zero) at PEGELONLINE, `STND` (station datum) at CO-OPS. `stations.parquet` names each station's datum and gives the offsets that refer its levels to other datums: those the source publishes, and MSL, LAT and chart datum from slackwater-database, the tide prediction database, or from another series of the gauge. Each source's README lists its datums.

A station's reference can change: a gauge's zero is reset, or the source moves to another height system. Each span over which a station's levels share one reference is a datum epoch, with series files of its own. `datum` and `datums` describe the current epoch, and `epochs` lists them all.

## Schema

These columns are in `stations.parquet`. [Stations](/docs/reference/stations/) describes the rest.

## Where offsets come from

`datums_source` names one of three origins for each offset in `datums`:

- **`source`**: the source's own offset.
- **`slackwater-database`**: the station's own record in slackwater-database, joined by the source's station ID within 10 km, when the record's datums are measured from the same zero as the station's levels: NOAA's STND, Kartverket's chart datum, a GESLA file's gauge zero, or NAP and RH 2000 for TICON's RWS and SMHI records. Where several records join a station, one record gives all its offsets, so values are never mixed from two records: one whose datums can be used, then an accepted one, then the latest reduction. It replaces the source's MSL, LAT and chart datum. A record whose terms bar commercial use serves only a station whose terms do too.
- **`computed`**: carried from another station of the gauge (its `same_as`, see [Gauges](/docs/reference/gauges/)) through a reference both give an offset to: a national height system (NAP, NHN, NN, HN, NGVD29, RH 2000, ordnance datum, or NAVD88 with its realization) or Admiralty Chart Datum, which is fixed at each UK port. That's how a PEGELONLINE gauge gets BSH's SKN through NHN, and an EA gauge BODC's chart datum through ordnance datum. Between two stations within 1 km that share no reference, offsets are carried through the offset measured between their levels instead (see [Links](/docs/reference/links/)). That's how a PEGELONLINE gauge gets MSL and LAT from a GESLA file of it, and that GESLA file gets SKN back from PEGELONLINE.

MSL, LAT and other tidal datums are carried only between stations within 1 km of each other, since they change along an estuary. SKN and chart datum CD hold for a whole stretch and carry along any link. Nothing is carried from a station whose terms bar commercial use to one whose terms allow it.

Some records give nothing: a record slackwater-database keeps in the constituent frame (MSL = 0), one it rejects for anything but being another record's copy, and one whose reduction period overlaps an epoch that doesn't convert to the current one, or converts by more than 5 cm, since its datums would then mix two references. A carried tidal datum isn't carried to a station with an epoch in its period that doesn't convert. RWS's 2005 revision of NAP moved most Dutch gauges by up to 3 cm, so datums reduced across it can be off by up to that much.

## Chart datum and frame

`chart_datum` is the datum charts use at the station. A level's default datum is `chart_datum`, or `datum` when that's null.

`frame` says whether the station's levels line up with the predictions of its `tide_station`:

- `gauge`: the chart-datum offset is from the slackwater-database record that `tide_station` names, so levels and that station's predictions share a zero.
- `source`: the chart-datum offset is the source's, computed, or from another record of the gauge.
- `unknown`: there's no `chart_datum`, or the `tide_station`'s datums are in the constituent frame (MSL = 0), so its predictions can't be compared with levels in absolute terms.

## Credits

`slackwater-database` and `computed` offsets are owed credits of their own, in `datums_attribution`. Show them with those offsets wherever they're shown, beside the station's `attribution`, including offsets read from [offsets.parquet](/docs/reference/offsets/).

## Datum epochs

Each epoch has its own series files, and no file is ever rewritten into another epoch. Every file holds one `datum` on every row, and that datum matches its own epoch. Rows from different epochs are in different datums. `recent/<source>.parquet` says each row's epoch in its `epoch` column. Most stations have one epoch.

Rows in an epoch's files outside its `since` and `until` aren't part of the series. They're the uncertain stretch around a change, or, where an epoch opened with a start in the past, years written before it opened, which the new epoch's files hold again. A station's whole record in its current datum joins its files to `epochs` and adds `to_current` (see [Examples](#examples)).

### When an epoch opens

A station gets a new epoch when:

- the source reports another datum label;
- the source reports a new reference under the same label, such as a change to PEGELONLINE's `gaugeZero.validFrom`. A changed `gaugeZero.value` under the same `validFrom` is a re-survey of the zero's height, not a moved zero, so it only updates `datums`;
- a gauge zero reset entered by hand moves a station's reference, as for USACE gauges;
- a decision finds the day a zero moved without the source saying, from the record's values against another record of the gauge or a neighbor;
- the source serves its history in the reference of its time. RWS serves values from before the 2005 NAP revision against NAP as it was then, under the same label, so its stations whose revision offset isn't known have an epoch that ends on 2005-01-01.

The new epoch's `since` is the instant the source gives, or else a date entered by hand, read at the start of the source's local day, or else the time of the refresh that saw the change. A date without a time leaves the whole day out of both epochs, since nobody knows when in the day the change came. Where only the refresh saw it, the old epoch ends at its `seen`. Rows in between are in the old epoch's files but outside both spans.

### When epochs convert

`to_current` is set only when an epoch and the current one share a fixed reference R, and is then the epoch's `datums[R]` less the current epoch's.

- R is a geodetic datum in the same realization in both epochs: NAP, NHN, NN, HN, NGVD29, or NAVD88 with its realization recorded, such as `NAVD88(2009.55)`. Tidal datums such as MSL, MLLW and LAT never count, because each tidal epoch moves them.
- An epoch's own datum counts as R with offset 0. A PEGELONLINE station that goes from PNP, with an offset to NN, to NN converts exactly. PNP with an offset to NHN, to NN, doesn't, since NN and NHN are different height systems.
- Where the epochs share more than one R, the first in that order is used.
- A reset that keeps the label never converts on its own. Offsets that changed across it might mean the zero moved, or only that its height was re-surveyed, and offsets that stayed the same might mean they never followed the reset. A decision sets `to_current` for that epoch. Until then its rows stay out of the combined series, readable in their own datum.

Each epoch converts straight to the current one, so conversions never chain, and a station with many epochs gains no error from the number of changes. When the current epoch's offsets are re-levelled, every `to_current` follows at the next refresh.

### A station that changes more than once

Each change opens one more epoch. Nothing about earlier epochs changes except their `to_current`, which is worked out again against the new current epoch:

- epoch 1 converts to epoch 3 if they share a fixed reference, whether or not epoch 2 did. A return to the same label (NAP, then MSL, then NAP) never converts on its own, since the zero may have moved in between;
- a change back is a new epoch, not a return to an old one;
- several changes between two refreshes show up as one, unless the source's dates or a decision enter the epochs in between.

A change within 180 days of the current epoch's `since`, or a label returning to one of the station's earlier ones, opens the epoch as `held`. A held epoch has a null `to_current`, and the station isn't fetched until a decision confirms or rejects the change. A `rejected` epoch is one a decision found never changed the reference, such as a label an adapter misread. Its files are read as the epoch before it, with that epoch's `to_current` plus any offset the decision gives, and that epoch's span runs on to the next epoch that isn't rejected.

## Converting a level

Add an offset to a level to refer it to another datum. A level of 5.00 m above gauge zero at Cuxhaven, whose `datums['NHN']` is −5.033, is 5.00 − 5.033 = −0.033 m in NHN.

That works for the current epoch's levels. For an earlier epoch's, add its `to_current` first, then the offset, or read the offset for that epoch from [offsets.parquet](/docs/reference/offsets/), which has applied `to_current` already.

## Examples

Datum offsets are a map. Add one to a level to convert it:

<QueryResult
  sql={`SELECT id, datum, datums['NHN'] AS to_nhn_m
FROM 'https://stillwell.openwaters.io/stations.parquet'
WHERE id IN ('pegelonline/5990020', 'bsh/726A')
ORDER BY id`}
/>

Each offset at a station, where it came from, and the period a tidal datum was reduced over:

<QueryResult
  sql={`SELECT key AS datum, datums[key] AS offset_m, datums_source[key] AS source,
  datums_epoch[key].start AS reduced_from, datums_epoch[key]."end" AS reduced_to
FROM 'https://stillwell.openwaters.io/stations.parquet',
  unnest(map_keys(datums)) AS t(key)
WHERE id = 'pegelonline/5990020'
ORDER BY datum`}
/>

How many stations have a chart datum, by source and frame:

<QueryResult
  sql={`SELECT source, count(*) AS stations, count(chart_datum) AS with_chart_datum,
  count(*) FILTER (frame = 'gauge') AS gauge_frame
FROM 'https://stillwell.openwaters.io/stations.parquet'
WHERE status = 'active'
GROUP BY source ORDER BY source`}
/>

Stations with more than one epoch, and how many have an epoch that doesn't convert:

<QueryResult
  sql={`SELECT source, count(*) AS stations,
  count(*) FILTER (len([x FOR x IN epochs IF x.to_current IS NULL]) > 0) AS with_unconverted
FROM 'https://stillwell.openwaters.io/stations.parquet'
WHERE len(epochs) > 1
GROUP BY source ORDER BY stations DESC`}
/>

A station's whole record in its current datum, here a year of Cuxhaven as monthly means. Add a `SELECT` to `o` for each later epoch's files, from its own directory, with `UNION ALL BY NAME`, since some older files lack the `source_flag` column:

<QueryResult
  sql={`SET TimeZone = 'UTC';
SET VARIABLE base = 'https://stillwell.openwaters.io/obs/pegelonline/5990020';
WITH e AS (
  SELECT unnest(epochs, recursive := true)
  FROM 'https://stillwell.openwaters.io/stations.parquet' WHERE id = 'pegelonline/5990020'
),
o AS (
  SELECT 1 AS epoch, * FROM read_parquet(getvariable('base') || '/2024.parquet')
),
r AS (
  SELECT o.time, o.level_m + e.to_current AS level_m, o.flag
  FROM o JOIN e ON o.epoch = e.id
    AND (e.since IS NULL OR o.time >= e.since) AND (e.until IS NULL OR o.time < e.until)
  WHERE e.to_current IS NOT NULL
  -- after a rejected epoch, its files and the epoch before it can share a time; the latter wins
  QUALIFY row_number() OVER (PARTITION BY o.time ORDER BY e.state = 'rejected') = 1
)
SELECT date_trunc('month', time)::DATE AS month, count(*) AS n, round(avg(level_m), 3) AS mean_m
FROM r WHERE flag <= 2
GROUP BY month ORDER BY month`}
/>

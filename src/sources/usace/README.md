# USACE CWMS (`usace`)

Stage from the US Army Corps of Engineers' Corps Water Management System, read through the public [CWMS Data API](https://cwms-data.usace.army.mil/cwms-data/) (CDA; [docs](https://cwms-data-api.readthedocs.io/), [source](https://github.com/USACE/cwms-data-api)). It covers the district offices on the Mississippi, Ohio, Missouri and their tributaries: MVS, MVK, MVM, MVN, LRL, LRP, LRH, LRN and LRC.

- **License:** U.S. Government work, public domain. CDA has no license or terms page, and its responses carry no license, but the stage is data the Corps produces, which [17 U.S.C. § 105](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/105) keeps out of copyright, and [33 U.S.C. § 2342](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/33/2342) requires the Corps to "make publicly available, including on the Internet, all data in the custody of the Corps of Engineers" on its water projects' operation and water management, "as quickly as practicable." CDA's code is under the [MIT license](https://github.com/USACE/cwms-data-api/blob/develop/LICENSE.md). The Corps asks for no attribution; "U.S. Army Corps of Engineers" is carried. CDA's web page shows the standard DoD consent banner ("provided for USG-authorized use only", with consent to monitoring), which every DoD site shows; it's a monitoring notice, not a license, and the API serves the data without a key to any origin (`Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`). Older Corps district pages say the United States assumes no liability for the data's accuracy, which `terms` carries.
- **Contact for errors:** CEMVRRiverGages@usace.army.mil, the contact button on [RiverGages](https://rivergages.mvr.usace.army.mil/WaterControl/new/layout.cfm), which shows the same CWMS stages. CDA itself has no contact but its software's [GitHub discussions](https://github.com/USACE/cwms-data-api/discussions), which are public; a report on one district's gauges may get a quicker answer from that district.
- **Request limits:** none published and none measured. CDA sends no rate-limit headers, and 40 one-year requests in a row on 2026-11-04 all answered 200, in 0.5 to 2 seconds each. Throttling (429) and server errors are retried with backoff, as for every source. Stay polite anyway: a backfill runs four stations at a time and each station's requests one after another.
- **Stations:** one per CWMS location with an observed `Stage.Inst` series, from each office's `catalog/TIMESERIES` and `catalog/LOCATIONS`, 1,355 in all, all active, 1,215 of them `live`. Most are river gauges, but stage is any water surface the Corps measures, so the list also has reservoir pools, lock and dam headwaters and tailwaters, and the riverside and landside of pump stations. `kind` comes from the location's CWMS `kind` and its district's free-text `type`. In November 2026, of 1,355 stations: `river` (728), where CWMS's kind is `STREAM_LOCATION` or `STREAM` (414) or the type starts with Stream ("Stream Gage", "Stream Gauge", "Stram Gage") or is "RoR Project", a run-of-river dam whose pool is the river (314); `lake` (18), where CWMS's kind is `PROJECT` or the type is Reservoir, Corps Reservoir, Lake or Project, since a project's stage is its pool; and null (609), everything else: types such as DCP, Gage and Lock, no type at all, and pump stations, canals and lock sides that nothing types. A location whose name says tailwater (`Tailwater`, or `TW` between non-letters) is never `lake`, since five MVK tailwaters carry the type RESERVOIR of the dam above them; it's `river` where CWMS's kind or the type says stream, as at LRH's lock and dam tailwaters, and null otherwise. Piezometers and observation wells (`…-Piezometer B1`, `…-OW7L`), which read groundwater in a dam's foundation, aren't listed. The ID is `<office>.<location>`, such as `usace/MVN.Baton_Rouge`, since location names are only unique within an office, with anything but letters, digits, `.`, `_` and `-` replaced by `_` (`MVK.Columbia L&D-Lower` is `usace/MVK.Columbia_L_D-Lower`), so an ID needs no escaping in a store path, URL or shell argument; of two new locations that would share an ID, the first by name gets it. Some gauges appear in two offices' databases under different names (Baton Rouge is `MVN.Baton_Rouge` and `MVK.Baton_Rouge`); each is listed. About 620 stations link to a USGS gauge with `same_as`, and 592 of them prefer the USGS station, since districts relay the transmissions of gauges USGS runs. Positions are CWMS's own, except seven gauges placed 14 to 334 km from the same gauge in another district's catalog or at USGS, which `POSITIONS` in [src/positions.ts](../../positions.ts) moves there: MVN's Baton Rouge, 113 km south of MVK's (erratum `usace-baton-rouge-position`), and MVK's Olive Branch and Oxford, whose latitudes are 3° and 2° off, MVN's Amelia and Chicot Pass near Myette Point, and LRN's Roaring River near Hilham and West Fork Browns Creek (erratum `usace-positions`). Moved, six of them link to their USGS gauge; MVN's Baton Rouge doesn't, since MVK's Baton Rouge already has, and one group holds one USACE station, so the publish report lists it as skipped. A location's position is its `latitude` and `longitude`, or its `published-latitude` and `published-longitude` where it has neither, as at all 78 LRC locations in November 2026, and at no other office's listed stations. LRC's two stations are the Chicago River and Lake Michigan sides of the Chicago Harbor Lock, whose stage is near zero (about −1.8 and +0.4 ft in September 2026) and whose zero CWMS gives no elevation for, so they have no `datums`. A location placed outside the contiguous US isn't listed: some sit at 0,0 or at a placeholder such as 9.09, 9.09 (MVS's Anoka), and a few have the longitude's sign dropped (LRL's Buck Creek, MVK's Pine Bluff). An office that answers with no series or no locations fails the refresh, and the store's list stays as it was.
- **Refresh:** Ingest hourly reads the last 12 hours at the 1,215 live stations and Compact daily folds the last 6 days into the year files. The Monthly workflow refreshes the station list on the 2nd and, since USACE `finalizes` values, re-reads each station from its earliest preliminary value on the 9th.
- **Which series:** a location usually has several. CWMS names each `<location>.Stage.Inst.<interval>.<duration>.<version>`, and the version is each office's own label. Taken are regular series (5 minutes to a day, not versioned) whose version ends in `rev` or `raw` or is one of `OBS`, `OBS-USGS`, `Observed`, `MVM-PUB` and `historic-data-merge`. Left out are forecasts (`Fcst-NWS-…`, `CWMS-Forecast-…`, river forecast center `…Shef-CA` and the like), statistics (`AVG1935TO2024`, `Bankfull`) and versions that name a datum (`rev-NAVD88`, `NGVD-29`, `LWRP`), which are the stage converted to that datum. Of those, a location's series is the first by: still reporting (its catalog latest time within half a year, since the catalog's extents lag the data by weeks), finer than daily, revised (`rev`) over `raw`, finest interval, then longest record. So a daily series, such as an `MVDhist-rev` record back to 1861, is taken only where nothing finer is still reporting. The pick is kept as `series` in the source's list in the store, and backfill reads that series. A refresh keeps a station's series while CWMS still lists it, even when another would now rank first, so a station's files never switch series. A listed station's ID belongs to the location its series is at: another location that maps to the same ID is skipped, with a message, and if the station's own location is gone it goes unlisted, so an ID never moves to another gauge. When CWMS stops listing the station's series, the station keeps it but is marked inactive, and the refresh that marks it prints the series CWMS now offers, lists the station as retired by the source in its report, and exits nonzero, so the run fails. Left inactive, the station is retired; setting its `series` to the one CWMS now offers brings it back. A location that loses every observed series stops being listed, and goes inactive as any unlisted station does. `stations.parquet` doesn't carry `series`, so a station read from there takes it from `source_url`, which names it.
- **Datum:** every level is stage, meters above the gauge's zero, named `GZ`. That stays fixed when an office changes which vertical datum it quotes. Where the location gives the gauge zero's `elevation` and names NGVD29 or NAVD88 as its `vertical-datum`, `datums` carries that elevation, so `level_m + datums.NGVD29` is the level above NGVD29. Spellings such as `ngvd29` and `NAVD88(2009.55)` count; `LOCAL`, `COE1912` and blanks don't. An elevation of exactly 0 is left out: offices give 0 both to gauges set at the datum and to locations they have no elevation for (MVK lists Baton Rouge at 0 NAVD88, MVN's description puts it 0.71 ft off). Checked against USGS: St. Louis 379.58 ft NAVD88 and Vicksburg 46.23 ft NGVD29 match USGS 07010000 and 07289000. About 590 stations get an NGVD29 offset, 480 a NAVD88 one (83 of them from MVN descriptions and one relabeled, below), and 292 none. A refresh that sends no offset keeps the stored one, as for every source.
- **NAVD88 from the description:** the New Orleans District (MVN) gives nearly every location an elevation of 0 or none, and states the gauge zero's adjustment to NAVD88 only in the location's `description` ([described.ts](described.ts)): "Adjustment for vertical datum NAVD88 (OPUS 2021): -1.403 ft. (e.g.add -1.403' to the gage reading to get to NAVD88)", or "Current adjustment to NAVD88 2004.65 is -0.04'". The value is what to add to the stage, as every example in the descriptions says, in feet, and goes in `datums.NAVD88` in meters, over the catalog's elevation where both give one. Where a description gives several adjustments, each with its own "as of" date, the latest is taken; several that differ undated or on the same day (the Calcasieu Saltwater Barrier's, one per staff) give none, and so does one dated before the gauge's [`zeros.ts`](zeros.ts) reset, which is for the old zero. A value followed by meters isn't read. Where no adjustment is stated at all, a gauge whose description says its zero was reset to NAVD88 the day before its [`zeros.ts`](zeros.ts) date is 0, since every value backfill reads is on that zero; Empire, whose description says its readings have diverged since 2018, isn't. Red River Landing's description is a copy of Knox Landing's and isn't read (erratum `usace-lwrp-corrections-disagree`). Its catalog elevation, −0.27 ft labeled NGVD29, matches the NAVD88 correction MVN applies in its `rev-NAVD88` series, −0.26 ft, within 0.01 ft, so `MISLABELED` in [index.ts](index.ts) puts it in `datums.NAVD88` while CWMS still serves it that way (erratum `usace-red-river-datum-label`). The store's list keeps where each offset came from as `datums_from`, with the description's words, the realization they name (`OPUS 2021`, `2009.55`, or null where they name none) and the date they give: `{"NAVD88": {"description": "Adjustment for vertical datum NAVD88 (OPUS 2021): -1.403 ft", "realization": "OPUS 2021", "as_of": null}}`. `stations.parquet` doesn't carry it. A refresh whose description no longer gives an offset says so. If the station then has no offsets at all, the stored ones and their `datums_from` are kept, as for any refresh that sends none, so taking back an offset read wrongly takes an edit of the store's list; if the catalog still gives an NGVD29 elevation, that replaces them. Only MVN's descriptions are read. That reads 83 of MVN's 208 stations in November 2026; the rest give no adjustment. An adjustment holds as of the survey it comes from: the gauges subside and are re-surveyed, a description gives one value, and the store applies it to the whole record. Where MVN also publishes a `rev-NAVD88` series, the correction it applies there, `rev-NAVD88` minus `rev`, agrees with the description within 0.05 ft at Donaldsonville, Melville, New Orleans and Simmesport, and differs by 0.10 to 0.31 ft at Krotz Springs, Knox Landing, Reserve, Morgan City and Baton Rouge (erratum `usace-lwrp-corrections-disagree`).
- **LWRP:** the 2007 Low Water Reference Plane slopes along the river, and CWMS has no structured LWRP offset for stage; 16 MVN descriptions give the offset from NAVD88 to it in prose ("To adjust NAVD88 (2009.55) values to 2007 Low Water Reference Plane (LWRP) datum relative to NAVD88, subtract 0.6 ft."). `datums.LWRP` is the description's NAVD88 adjustment plus that offset, with `datums_from.LWRP` quoting it, where the LWRP sentence names the same realization as the adjustment or none: 13 gauges from Knox Landing to Venice. Baton Rouge's adjustment is to OPUS 2019 and its LWRP offset from OPUS 2010, so it has none, and neither do Empire, which has no NAVD88, and Red River Landing, whose description isn't read. IHNC Lock's description gives its offset twice, as "add -0.5" and "subtract 0.5", which agree. The offsets match MVN's LWRP white paper at Knox Landing, Baton Rouge, New Orleans and Venice ([#10](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/issues/10)); the NAVD88 adjustment under them has the limits above. CWMS's one `LWRP` stage series, at New Orleans, holds only 1936.
- **Gauge zeros that moved:** `GZ` is the gauge zero each value was read against, and at 33 stations, most in MVN, that zero moved partway through the series, typically from NGVD29 to NAVD88. CWMS keeps one series across the reset and says so only in the location's description, so the dates are entered by hand in [`zeros.ts`](zeros.ts), each with the description's words. Venice's zero moved on 2015-06-28, and its hourly stage drops 1.8 ft overnight, matching the 1.84 ft the description gives. Backfill never fetches a value from before 06:00 UTC on a station's date, which is local midnight or later at all nine offices (Central and Eastern, standard or daylight time), so each station's files hold one zero and `datums` applies to all of them. The refresh copies the date onto the station as `zero_since`, and keeps the later of that and [`zeros.ts`](zeros.ts)'s, so neither can be dropped on its own. Adding a station there later opens a datum epoch from its date ([docs/stations.md](../../../docs/stations.md)), so values already stored stay in epoch 1 and new ones go to files of their own. Steele Bayou's landside gauge (`MVK.Steele_Bayou`) has a date too, though its series today ends in July 2017. Left out on purpose: gauges "set to NAVD88" with no date and no earlier zero named (new installations), elevations updated without the zero moving (MVK Helena, MVK Bogalusa), MVN Head of Passes (new gauge set to the old one's water surface), and gauges that moved with no datum noted (several in MVM). Some descriptions also note bad stretches in prose (Reserve's values from 1991-10-04 to 1993-01-14 "are 1 foot too high"), which keep CWMS's flags.
- **Levels:** `timeseries` in SI units, a calendar year per request, following `next-page`. A response with values in anything but meters, or that doesn't say its unit, is refused. Values are UTC epoch milliseconds.
- **Flags:** CWMS quality is a bit field. Missing (4) drops the value, as does a null value; rejected (16) is bad; questionable (8) is suspect; unscreened (bit 0 clear, quality 0) and screened without a verdict (quality 1) are preliminary, since nobody has passed them; a value replaced by linear interpolation (replacement method 1, bits 11 to 14) is interpolated; anything else screened okay is good. The bit layout is USACE's own [CWMS-Data-Quality](https://github.com/USACE-WaterManagement/CWMS-Data-Quality) library's. Some offices screen nothing (St. Louis's series is all quality 0), so all their values are preliminary; MVN screens most of its values. The Monthly refetch passes `--skip-unscreened` for USACE, so a gauge with a year of record and no screened value isn't fetched again each month (a sample of four active gauges per office in November 2026 found MVS, MVK and LRN at 99 to 100% preliminary, MVM, LRL, LRH and LRP at none, and MVN mixed).
- **Quality control:** the store's shared checks, which follow each station's measured `tidal` ([stations.md](../../../docs/stations.md#analysis)). Navigation pools sit within centimeters for months and then rise meters in a flood, so they measure as still water: two days before a flatline is suspect, and no outlier rule, which marked Greenup pool's 2024 high water (1,153 values up to 7.42 m) suspect. Until a station's first analysis, or where its series is too coarse to judge, QC goes by its `kind`: a `river` or `lake` as still water, and one with no `kind` (the 607 that neither CWMS nor the district's type calls a stream, reservoir or project) as a tide gauge, with the outlier rule on. In November 2026 the gauges of coastal Louisiana measure as tidal: the Mississippi from New Orleans down, the Atchafalaya at Morgan City and the Calcasieu, with 8 to 25 cm of lunar tide.
- **Backfill size:** backfill isn't yet bounded by each series' catalog extents, so `backfill usace all` over a long range asks for every year it's given. The daily records reach back to 1861; shard long backfills by station and range.
- **Freshness:** responses whose range ends within the last 120 days, and the catalogs, are never cached, since offices revise recent values. Older years are cached for good, so a later screening pass on them reaches the store only once the cache is cleared.

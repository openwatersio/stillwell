# Servicio Mareográfico Nacional, UNAM (`unam`)

One-minute sea levels from each sensor at 26 of the 27 tide gauges of Mexico's Servicio Mareográfico Nacional (SMN), run by UNAM's Instituto de Geofísica, from the files behind its [station pages](https://www.mareografico.unam.mx/estaciones/). SMN serves only the last 5 days; its records before that are sent on request, so the store's record starts when the adapter starts reading.

- **License:** the site's footer says 「Esta página y sus contenidos pueden ser reproducidos con fines no lucrativos, siempre y cuando no se mutile, se cite la fuente completa y su dirección electrónica, de otra forma, se requiere permiso previo por escrito de la Institución」: its pages and contents may be reproduced for non-profit ends, unaltered and citing the full source and its address, and anything else needs UNAM's written permission. Permission to republish, and the historical records, were asked for on 8 November 2026 ([#48](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/issues/48)). The store carries them meanwhile, on the expectation that permission is given, with `commercial_use` false until SMN answers. The attribution is SMN's own citation, 「SMN <año>: Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México, Instituto de Geofísica, Servicio Mareográfico Nacional, México」, and its address, http://www.mareografico.unam.mx. Nothing in the store keeps the data unaltered: `terms` says what the store changes.
- **Contact for errors:** mareografico@igeofisica.unam.mx, from SMN's [contact page](https://www.mareografico.unam.mx/index.php/contacto/).
- **Stations:** the stations in the menu of a station page (`estaciones/?estacion=1`, whose menu leaves out station 1 itself, which the page's heading names), and a series for each sea-level sensor in each one's file. IDs are `unam/<number>-<sensor>`: `radar` for the averaged radar (`radar promedio`), `flotador` for the float gauge, `flotador-promedio` for its average, and `cbs` and `pls`, SMN's names for its other sensors, so `unam/14-radar` and `unam/14-flotador` at La Paz. GESLA and TICON number Ciudad del Carmen's radar 4, which is Celestún's number; SMN's is 5, which their records are matched to. That's SMN's station number and GESLA's sensor names for its SMN records (`lapaz_radar-14-mex-unam`). Each sensor measures from its own zero, centimeters to meters apart (Manzanillo's radar reads 7.7 m where its CBS reads 2.7 m), so each is a series of its own. SMN publishes no positions; they're in the adapter, from GESLA 4.1's records of the same gauges. Río Grijalva (40) has none there or on SMN's site, so it's left out. A new station without a position, a column the adapter doesn't know, or a page whose heading it can't read (the menu leaves out the page's own station), fails the list's refresh rather than be left out silently.
- **Files:** `estaciones/grdata/data-<number>_metros.csv`, rewritten every minute, about 300 KB: a header, `Tiempo,Pronostico,` then a column per sensor, and a line per minute for the last 5 days and a few hours, blank where a sensor has no value. `Pronostico` is SMN's tide prediction, which isn't read. The adapter reads the header (the first 512 bytes), then the end of the file by a byte range big enough for the hours asked for, at most 80 bytes a minute, about 60 KB for hourly ingest's 12 hours; the cut first line is skipped, and so is a last line without its newline, which the file's rewrite can leave cut mid-value. A station's sensors share both requests. A request that starts more than 5 days back is refused (see below).
- **Levels:** meters, raw: SMN flags nothing and reviews nothing in these files, so every value is `preliminary` and stays so (`finalizes` false).
- **Times:** UTC, whatever the station's zone. The chart's axis says 「Hora UTC」, the page's script turns the server's Mexico City time into UTC, and at 15:32 UTC on 9 November 2026 La Paz's last prediction was stamped 15:30 and its last observation 15:14, while the page's clock read 09:31. See the clock check below.
- **Clock check:** `scripts/clock-check.ts` over the 4.5 days SMN served on 9 November 2026, against TICON's records from UHSLC, which converts to UTC itself: La Paz (r 0.994, best lag −20 min) and Mazatlán (0.998, 0) on UTC−7, Puerto Vallarta (0.994, −10), Manzanillo (0.989, −10), Acapulco's float (0.949, +10) and Salina Cruz (0.926, 0) on UTC−6, and Veracruz (0.964, +10) on the Gulf. No series is off by an hour, so the files are UTC whatever the station's zone. On a range this short the script flags a lag of 20 minutes, since a 12-hour error would show as about 25 in the semidiurnal tide, and it flags La Paz's radar. That isn't a 12-hour error: La Paz's tide is mixed, so 12 hours would turn its diurnal half over, but it fits at r 0.989 at lag 0; Mazatlán, in the same zone, fits best at 0; and La Paz's float, against TICON's record of it, is 20 minutes the other way. Lags of 10 to 20 minutes either way are what 4 days against constants from other decades can tell apart, and 4 days are too few for K1. Puerto Morelos, in Quintana Roo (UTC−5), has too small a tide to tell (r 0.08); Isla Mujeres, nearby, fits its own TICON record at −10 minutes (r 0.78). The check needs a month of values unless the range asked for is shorter, so it reads SMN's 5 days.
- **Datum:** each sensor's zero, `ZERO`, the name GESLA gives it. SMN publishes no datum or benchmark heights, only that levels are measured from 「un nivel de referencia fijo」 (a fixed reference level), which was asked for in #48. A zero can still move: GESLA's copies of both Mazatlán sensors sit 0.5 to 2 m off their usual level for months of 2021 (erratum `gesla-datum-jumps`), and nothing in the adapter or QC detects a lasting shift like that.
- **Freshness:** a sensor's values run about 15 minutes behind, so the source is `hourly`, and every station is `live`. Hourly ingest reads the last 12 hours, so after a longer gap, run the backfill below within 5 days of its start, or the values are lost for good. A whole file that starts more than 2 minutes after the hours asked for, as when SMN restarts its logger, is refused, and so is one that ends more than 30 minutes before them, as a read caught mid-rewrite can, so a fetch doesn't replace stored values with fewer. A station whose file can't be read fails the list's refresh, which keeps the list it had.
- **Limits:** SMN publishes no rate limit and has no robots.txt. Its pages refresh themselves every 5 minutes, so `min_interval_s` is 300. Requests start half a second apart.
- **Not used:** SMN's prediction column, its weather files (`data-<number>_<unit>.csv` for temperature, pressure, wind and the sensors' battery and noise), and the historical records (TICON's `unam_hist`, back to 1946), which SMN sends only on request.

## Backfill

There's no history to backfill: SMN serves 5 days. The adapter refuses a request that starts earlier, since backfill replaces every month it brings values for, and a year's backfill would replace a month the store holds with its last 5 days. Monthly refreshes SMN's list on the 2nd; dispatch Monthly with `sources: unam` once the adapter is on `main`, and Ingest hourly takes the stations from its next run. To keep the days already served, run one backfill over them through the Ingest workflow once the list is in the store, from 4 days back to tomorrow, with the dates written out, such as on 12 November 2026:

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill unam all 2026-11-08 2026-11-13
```

That's 26 stations, two requests each; 4 days' range is bigger than the file, so each answers with all of it, about 8 MB in all, in one run. A wider range, or `backfill unam preliminary`, is refused for every station.

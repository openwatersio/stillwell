# Environment Agency (`ea`)

Tidal levels every 15 minutes at the Environment Agency's tide gauges, from its [real-time flood-monitoring API](https://environment.data.gov.uk/flood-monitoring/doc/reference): the UK National Tide Gauge Network around Great Britain, Northern Ireland, the Isle of Man and Jersey, which the EA owns, and the EA's own gauges on the English coast.

- **License:** the API is open data under the [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/), with no registration, and every response's `meta.licence` says so. That allows redistribution and commercial use, so every station goes to the public store. The EA asks users to acknowledge it with "this uses Environment Agency flood and river level data from the real-time data API (Beta)", which is the attribution. The National Tide Gauge Network's quality-controlled record is BODC's, which the `bodc` adapter reads; this adapter takes only the EA's real-time readings of the same gauges.
- **Contact for errors:** the [Defra Data Services Platform support page](https://environment.data.gov.uk/support), where the [API reference](https://environment.data.gov.uk/flood-monitoring/doc/reference) sends questions and problems.
- **Stations:** `GET /id/stations?type=TideGauge` lists 88 stations at 43 places (November 2026). Each National Tide Gauge Network gauge is listed twice, as `E7xx39` in meters above ordnance datum (mAOD) and `E7xx24` in meters above chart datum, and Newport a third time in millimeters; the readings are the same, a constant apart (2.25 m at Aberdeen). The adapter lists each station with a `Tidal Level` measure in mAOD, and the chart datum series of a gauge that has no mAOD one, 43 in all, by EA notation (`ea/E72239` is Newlyn). Until November 2026 four gauges had only a chart datum series (Avonmouth Portbury `E72624`, Bournemouth `E71924`, Hinkley Point `E72524` and Immingham `E71124`); the EA then added mAOD series (`E72639`, `E71939`, `E72539` and `E71139`), which the adapter lists in their place. A listed mAOD station names, in `same_as`, any chart datum series of the gauge the store already lists, so the old series stays in the gauge's group with its history. It takes only the 15-minute mean series (`-tidal_level-Mean-15_min-`). A chart datum series counts as an mAOD series' twin when it has the same label or lies within about 2 km. A station's page is Check for Flooding's, by its River Levels on the Internet ID, where it has one (18 of 43). Every station is `coastal`, and live unless `GET /id/measures?qualifier=Tidal Level` gives its series no latest reading and its readings hold nothing in the API's 28 days, which leaves it inactive and unpolled. In November 2026 that was Mumbles and Stornoway, which have never reported, and Jersey (stopped in March 2026) and Washpile (in April 2026). A day's outage keeps a reading in the 28 days, so it retires nothing, a series the measures list leaves out keeps its station active, and an answer where no station's series reports fails the refresh. A station the station list no longer gives, such as a chart datum series an mAOD one replaced, is retired: inactive and not live, with its history kept. The refresh that first lists a station inactive reports it as retired by the source, which fails that run once for review. A station that comes back isn't polled until the next refresh lists it active, which the refresh shows only as a changed `status` and `live`; the archive keeps about 380 days, so when a refresh shows one going from inactive to active, run `backfill ea <id> <year> <year+1>` over its silent stretch (named stations are fetched whatever their status). Country codes are GB but for Jersey (JE) and Port Erin (IM).
- **Datum:** `AOD`, ordnance datum: Newlyn in Great Britain, and the local ordnance datum in Northern Ireland, the Isle of Man and Jersey. `CD`, chart datum, at a gauge listed by its meters series, as the four that had no mAOD series did until November 2026 and keep in the store: the EA labels those series only "m", but where a gauge has both, the meters series is the mAOD one plus the chart datum's depth below ordnance datum (2.25 m at Aberdeen, about 5.8 m at Newport). No offsets between the two are given.
- **Levels:** the series are 15-minute means (`Mean-15_min`), every one `preliminary`, since the EA doesn't quality-control or revise them. `min_interval_s` is 900, the series' cadence. The readings API answers for the last 30 days or so (from 9 October on 7 November 2026), and the last 28 whole days come from it. Days before come from the archive's daily files, which hold every EA measure's readings for the day. The archive keeps about a year (from late October 2025 on 7 November 2026). A value can be blank, or two readings joined by `|`; both are dropped.
- **Gaps:** a day the archive no longer holds, or never published, gives no values, and the rest of its month still does. The EA never withdraws a reading, so the store keeps every value it holds, and a day the archive or the API doesn't answer for keeps what the store has. The archive never published 25 July, 16 August or 20 August 2026 (errata `ea-archive-missing-2026-07-25`, `ea-archive-missing-2026-08-16` and `ea-archive-missing-2026-08-20`). Since no stored value is ever removed, a span the EA got wrong is flagged by an erratum instead.
- **Freshness:** live readings are read hourly, the last 12 hours each time, and the store's record reaches back as far as the first backfill. The EA never finalizes a reading (`finalizes` false), so nothing reads a month again; a gap longer than hourly ingest's 12 hours, such as an Actions outage, stays until someone backfills that stretch, from the API within 28 days or the archive within the year. Mumbles and Stornoway, which have never reported, have no values in the store.
- **Clock check:** the readings API's 30 days (`2026-10-09/2026-11-08` when checked) fit best at 0 minutes at Newlyn (against UHSLC's record and BODC's), Lowestoft, Dover and Aberdeen (BODC's), with r from 0.88 at Lowestoft to 0.995 at Newlyn. Thirty days don't separate K1, so it shows `no K1`, and the check then fails a lag of 20 minutes or more, where a 12-hour error in a tide this semidiurnal shows; all five are 0.
- **TICON:** the TICON records of these gauges are BODC's and CMEMS's copies of the National Tide Gauge Network. The inventory matches them by position to the EA's gauge, `ready`. That record starts with the archive's year, so a fit from it needs the year or more the store gathers from now on.
- **Flags:** the API sends no quality code, so every value is `preliminary`. The source checks its values: no.

## Running ingest

- **Series IDs:** the 15-minute mean series is the one the archive files are read for. Its ID is the notation, `-level-tidal_level-Mean-15_min-` and the unit (`measureOf`), so a station from `stations.parquet` names its series without the list's extra fields.
- **Requests:** `GET /id/measures/<measure>/readings?since=…` serves the last 28 whole days. Days before come from `GET /archive/readings-<YYYY-MM-DD>.csv`, about 60 MB each, as `dateTime,measure,value`, and a day outside the archive answers 404. Backfill keeps every value the store holds and replaces only those at times it fetched (`keepsStored`). A 404 more than 350 days back is printed; a 404 for a day within the last 350 days is a gap in the archive, so it's raised as an error annotation on the run. A day file that answers but isn't the CSV, or holds no tide gauge reading, fails the station's year, and is remembered for the run rather than downloaded again by every station. While one day file is read, the next three download. A request that starts within the API's 28 days reads no archive file.
- **Archive reading:** a day file is downloaded once a run, never cached on disk (a year of them is about 22 GB), and its tide gauges' readings in meters are kept in memory for the other stations, as flat arrays of numbers, about 3 million readings and 50 MB for a year. Each measure ID is copied into a string of its own, since a substring would keep its whole day file in memory. So a backfill of every station over a year downloads about 365 files, 22 GB, whatever the number of stations; a run on one station costs the same as on all of them, and each shard or rerun downloads them all again, so run it once, unsharded. The archive answers 429 for a while after a run downloads many day files, as after a full backfill. A refused day file waits 5 minutes, or longer if Retry-After asks, up to 15, holding the other downloads back, and is asked for three times in all (each try retrying a few times over seconds); then the run stops, as for a source that blocks it. A station-year is written only once all its day files are read, so a later run starts those over: run it once the archive has had an hour or more.
- **Clock check:** run it as `clock-check <30 days ago>/<tomorrow>`; a range that reaches past the readings API's 30 days reads 60 MB archive files.
- **Limits:** none published. The API's default `_limit` is 500 readings, so the readings request asks for 10,000.

### Backfill

The Monthly workflow refreshes the EA's list on the 2nd. Ingest hourly and Compact daily skip the EA with a warning until the list is in the store, so its readings aren't collected. The archive reaches back only about a year and drops a day each day, so a backfill goes as far back as the archive reaches:

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill ea all 2025 2027 --with-inactive
```

Dispatch it with `timeout` at 360. `--with-inactive` takes the stations that have stopped too, whose last readings the archive still holds. That's two years at each of 47 stations with `--with-inactive` (the 43 listed and the four chart datum series they replaced), up to 94 station-years, from about 365 day files, 22 GB in all, downloaded four at a time; the run in November 2026 wrote 82 station-years in about 15 minutes (Mumbles and Stornoway, whose series have no readings, have no values in the store). Without `--first`: every station reads the same day files, so the order saves nothing, and the TICON inventory names no EA station until it has run with the EA's list. Ingest hourly keeps every station's readings, so the store's record reaches back as far as the first backfill. The archive is a copy taken after each day, and the API can deliver readings in batches later, so a day's file can lack late readings that hourly ingest kept; a later backfill keeps those, since it never drops a stored value. The EA never finalizes a reading (`finalizes` false), so Monthly's refetch skips `ea`, as it does PEGELONLINE; a gap longer than hourly ingest's 12 hours, such as an Actions outage, stays until someone backfills that stretch, from the API within 28 days or the archive within the year.

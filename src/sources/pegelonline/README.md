# PEGELONLINE (`pegelonline`)

Observed water levels at about 690 German federal gauges on the coast, rivers and canals, from the WSV's [PEGELONLINE](https://www.pegelonline.wsv.de/) REST API and the raw-data download on its station pages.

- **License:** [DL-DE/Zero-2.0](https://www.pegelonline.wsv.de/gast/nutzungsbedingungen); commercial use and redistribution allowed.
- **Contact for errors:** the [Kontakt & Rückmeldung form](https://pegelonline.wsv.de/adminmail), or pegelonline@itzbund.de from the [imprint](https://www.pegelonline.wsv.de/gast/impressum). PEGELONLINE gives no contact for the local WSV offices that run the gauges.
- **Stations:** every station with a `W` (water level) series and a position, by PEGELONLINE number.
- **Levels:** raw and unchecked, so `preliminary`. Most series are centimeters above gauge zero (PNP), with gauge zero's height in NHN or NN as the datum offset. 67 series are meters above NN and two are meters above PNP; the adapter asks each series for its unit, scales by it, and refuses a series whose unit puts it in another datum than the station's. The gauge zero's `validFrom` is the station's datum reference: a later date opens a new datum epoch, leaving that day out of both, while a new height under the same date is a re-survey that only changes the offset ([docs/stations.md](../../../docs/stations.md)). Tide gauges report every minute, others every 15.
- **History:** the REST API keeps 31 days, so `recent` uses it and anything older comes from the raw-data download ("Download Wasserstände (Rohdaten)" on each station page). That goes back to 1 January 2000 at most gauges. Each station's `history_since` is the first day it offers, read from the download dialog, or null where it offers only the last 31 days (the Hamburg Port Authority gauges, Este AP and the Oste barrier) or no download at all. Of 137 gauges on the North Sea and tidal rivers, 94 start on 2000-01-01, 26 later, and 17 have no history.
- **Download:** `GET /gast/download/modal?uuid=…&parameter=W` gives the range in its date inputs' `min` and `max`. `POST /gast/download/prepare` with `uuid`, `parameter=W`, `start` and `end` (whole local days, both included) and `format=json` answers with a one-time `downloadUrl` to a zip. The zip holds the values, `zeitreiheninformation.txt` with the series' unit and interval, and the terms. JSON timestamps carry their offset; CSV's are local time and repeat an hour each autumn, and ZRXP's are UTC+1 all year with `-777.0` for gaps. Days outside the range come back empty. The form has a box to accept the [terms of use](https://www.pegelonline.wsv.de/gast/nutzungsbedingungen), which Open Waters accepted before scripting it. Downloads are one at a time and aren't cached, since each URL works once. After each, the next waits as long as it took, at least 5 seconds and at most a minute, so a slower site gets fewer requests; a download of a few seconds now takes several times as long, and one of 5 to 60 seconds about twice as long. A Retry-After longer than the HTTP layer will wait holds the next request as long, and one over 15 minutes stops the run. The station lookup before each download waits its turn in the same queue. A lookup, `prepare` or file download that ends in a 403, 429, 500, 502, 503, 504, timeout or dropped connection isn't asked again (the HTTP layer has already retried the ones worth retrying). Refusals at three different stations with no download completed between them throw `Blocked`: every download after, including those already queued, fails at once, and backfill stops instead of going on through its stations. One gauge refused year after year is a broken series rather than a block, so it fails only its own years. A file that downloads but isn't a readable zip prepares again after a rest, up to three times, since each prepared file can be downloaded only once. The block lasts for the life of the process, which suits a backfill run; the next run picks up where this one stopped.
- **Download and REST API disagree:** at Cuxhaven Steubenhöft and Husum, the download and the REST API give levels up to 6 and 8 cm apart for the same minutes, though both are unchecked raw data on one gauge zero, so the series can step where the REST API's 31 days take over (erratum `pegelonline-download-api-differ`, [#26](https://github.com/openwatersio/water-levels/issues/26)). Cuxhaven's download also sits 4 to 6 cm above GESLA's copy from 13 May to 24 June 2025, so the WSV changed values it still labels raw (erratum `pegelonline-cuxhaven-2025-revised`). Both are kept as served.
- **Wachendorf's gauge zero:** PEGELONLINE dates it from 2026-06-18 without saying whether it moved that day (erratum `pegelonline-wachendorf-zero-date`, [#72](https://github.com/openwatersio/water-levels/issues/72)). The lake keeps the gauge on one epoch.
- **Datum of old values:** the download serves past values on each gauge's current zero, so the source sets `archive: "current"` and backfill reaches back to 2000 across a gauge zero's `validFrom` ([docs/stations.md](../../../docs/stations.md)). The evidence:
  - On 2019-11-01 the WSV gave every gauge zero a new height in DHHN2016, among them all 50 on the North Sea and Baltic, which went from a round NHN −5.00 m to values such as −4.966 m or −5.033 m. The download's monthly means from June 2018 to May 2021 differ from PSMSL's record (RLR, a fixed benchmark) by the same amount before and after, within 1.3 mm, at Cuxhaven, Wismar, Warnemünde, Sassnitz and Kiel-Holtenau, where the heights changed by 21–34 mm. Ten inland gauges show no step at local midnight that day.
  - At the 108 gauges whose download covers a `validFrom` after 2000 other than 2019-11-01, across 15 dates, none steps at local midnight that day by more than its ordinary steps: 71 by nothing, 31 by 1–3 cm, and 4 by 4–6 cm at regulated lock pools and a canal bridge, whose usual steps are that size. A zero moved and recorded as measured would show its move there, typically decimeters.
  - That doesn't say whether WSV rewrites its archive when a zero moves or whether no zero has moved since 2000, but either way the download is continuous across every `validFrom` it covers. Run `ingest stations` before a first backfill, so each station has its `history_since`. A station whose list predates `archive` keeps the epoch start it had, at its `validFrom`, and backfill stops there; the R2 lake's stations were seeded without one. A station whose `history_since` is null gets nothing from a backfill whose range starts more than 30 days ago, since the month it starts in would be only partly covered; `recent` keeps its live values.
- **Kind:** stations on the North Sea and Baltic are `coastal`; the rest aren't classified yet.

# PEGELONLINE (`pegelonline`)

Observed water levels at about 690 German federal gauges on the coast, rivers and canals, from the WSV's [PEGELONLINE](https://www.pegelonline.wsv.de/) REST API and the raw-data download on its station pages. The history of the Hamburg Port Authority's six Elbe gauges comes from the WSV's coastal data portal, [kuestendaten.de](https://www.kuestendaten.de/DE/Services/Messreihen_Dateien_Download/Download_Zeitreihen_node).

- **Stations:** every PEGELONLINE station with a `W` (water level) series and a position, as `pegelonline/<number>`. The Hamburg Port Authority (HPA) gauges, Bunthaus, Hamburg-Harburg, Schöpfstelle, Hamburg St. Pauli, Seemannshöft and Blankenese UF, also credit HPA, the WSV and www.kuestendaten.de.
- **Kind:** from the station's water and its `W` series' characteristic values. `coastal` is a gauge on the `NORDSEE` or `OSTSEE`. `estuary` is any other gauge with the WSV's tidal values (MThw, MTnw, HThw or NTnw), which it gives only for a gauge in tidal water: the tidal Elbe, Weser, Ems and Jade, their tidal tributaries, and two Dortmund-Ems-Kanal gauges on the tidal Ems. `lake` is Konstanz, on the `BODENSEE`. Every other gauge is null, since PEGELONLINE doesn't say which waters are rivers and which are canals. About 20 gauges in tidal water have no tidal values, such as siels, storm-surge barrier sides and Brunsbüttel MPM, and stay null too. No Baltic river or lagoon gauge has them.
- **Levels:** most series are centimeters above gauge zero (PNP). About 70 are meters above NN, and a few are meters above PNP. Most gauges report every minute or every 15 minutes, and a few every 5 or 10.
- **History:** the REST API keeps 31 days. Older values come from the raw-data download ("Download Wasserstände (Rohdaten)" on each station page), which reaches back to 1 January 2000 at most gauges. Each station's `history_since` is the first day its download offers. It is null where the download offers only the last 31 days, as at Este AP and the Oste barrier, or no download at all. Those stations, about 70, hold only the values the store has read from the REST API. PEGELONLINE offers only 31 days at the HPA gauges, so their history comes from each one's "Wasserstand (PNP)" series on kuestendaten.de: one value a minute in centimeters above gauge zero, from 1 November 1999 to the end of the last half-year the portal published.
- **Times:** the download's JSON times carry their UTC offset. kuestendaten.de's files are in UTC+1 all year.
- **Datum:** `PNP`, the gauge zero, with its height above NHN or NN in `datums`. A series in meters above NN is in `NN`, with no offsets. The gauge zero's `validFrom` is the station's datum reference. A later date opens a new datum epoch and leaves that day out of both, while a new height under the same date is a re-survey that changes only the offset ([when an epoch opens](https://openwatersio.github.io/stillwell/docs/guides/datums/#when-an-epoch-opens)).
- **Old values:** the download serves past values on each gauge's current zero, so `archive` is `current` and a station's history reaches back across its `validFrom`. Across the DHHN2016 re-survey of 1 November 2019, the download's monthly means at five coastal gauges keep the same difference from PSMSL's RLR record, and ten inland gauges show no step that day. At every gauge whose download covers another `validFrom` since 2000, it shows no step that day beyond the gauge's ordinary ones, where a moved zero would show decimeters. Whether the WSV rewrites its archive when a zero moves, or no zero has moved since 2000, isn't known. Either way the download is continuous across every `validFrom` it covers.
- **HPA zeros:** kuestendaten.de's files are on each gauge's current zero too. St. Pauli's file equals the CMEMS copy (`cmems/StPauliTG`, the same record as GESLA 4.1's), which is in NHN, at every shared 10-minute time from November 1999 to 1 March 2025 03:30 UTC. From 03:40 on that day to the end of June 2026 they differ at about a fifth of shared times, mostly by 1 or 2 cm and up to 42 cm, because the copy takes values from other minutes than their timestamps say (erratum `cmems-st-pauli-wrong-minute`). The store's REST values from 9 October to 23 November 2026 equal the copy at every shared time, so the live feed may carry the same slips at St. Pauli (erratum `pegelonline-st-pauli-2026-11-09-dip`); no HPA file covers those months, so that isn't confirmed. The other five gauges have no copy to compare with. Their zeros moved by only 15 to 18 mm in 2019, less than their monthly means against St. Pauli vary, so the store takes them to be on the current zero, unchecked to that precision.
- **Flags:** PEGELONLINE sends no quality code, and its values are raw and unchecked, so every value is `preliminary`. Every row of kuestendaten.de's files has status 240, which the files don't explain, so those values are `preliminary` too.
- **Freshness:** the poller reads every station's last 12 hours from the REST API every 5 minutes. A gauge whose telemetry was down can send the missing days at once when it's back, so the poller also reads each station, a few times a day, from the first gap in its last days or from where its values stopped. kuestendaten.de updates its files about four months after each half-year ends.
- **Independent checks:** the [pair check](../../../docs/contributing/checks.md#pair-check) on the one PEGELONLINE gauge another agency also records, Eemshaven (9340010), against RWS's records of its harbor and Wadden Sea gauges (two from RWS and three copies in GESLA and CMEMS), in 2018, 2023 and 2026. The harbor gauge agrees at zero lag at 3.0 and 3.4 cm RMS, the Wadden Sea gauge at 10 minutes (3.8 to 4.8 cm against 6.7 to 9.3 cm), a difference of position. 4 pairs with the BfG's records and 1 with HPA's St. Pauli copy hold the same values as PEGELONLINE's, since the BfG and HPA are where its values come from. Datums are unchecked: PNP is against NAP. The other stations have no other originator's record.

## Known limits

- **Late values:** a gap the WSV fills after the poller's last read for it, four to five days after its times, stays in the store. Nothing reports such a gap. A backfill over the month restores it from the raw-data download, at a station that has one, and a monthly read of the last months would find it ([#544](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/issues/544)).
- **Corrections:** the raw-data download can hold corrected values where the REST API still serves the first ones, as at Nienburg on 6 and 7 November 2026, so a correction reaches the store only through a backfill ([#544](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/issues/544)).
- **When values count as final:** PEGELONLINE marks no value final and states no revision window. A harmonic fit reads a year once it ended 12 months ago (`settles_after_months`), a figure decided here from the one revision the store has seen: the WSV moved Cuxhaven's values of 13 May to 24 June 2025 by 4 to 6 cm months after measuring them (erratum `pegelonline-cuxhaven-2025-revised`). That sets no upper bound, so a later revision can reach a year a fit has read. The store learns of one only when a backfill with `--refetch` reads the year again, and the next `fit` of the source then fits the station again ([#526](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/issues/526)).
- **HPA gaps:** each kuestendaten.de file ends at 23:00 UTC on its last day, so the store lacks that month's last hour at the HPA gauges until the next file restores it. Values between the last file's end and the start of the poller wait for the next update too.
- **Download date:** kuestendaten.de's terms (DL-DE/Zero-2.0) suggest a source note naming the series, the WSV, www.kuestendaten.de and the download date. The store doesn't record when it downloaded each file.

## Running ingest

- **Refresh:** The poller reads the last 12 hours from the REST API, and Compact daily folds the last 6 days into the year files. The adapter sets `fillsLate`, since a gauge whose telemetry was down sends the missing days when it's back, so each station also has turns on which a pass reads it from the first gap in its last days ([workers/live](../../../workers/live/README.md)). The Monthly workflow doesn't refetch PEGELONLINE's values; it refreshes the station list on the 2nd. A series in another datum than its station's makes the poller fail that station until a refresh opens the epoch, which the poller starts at most once a day. A change of the gauge zero's height alone doesn't open an epoch, and `levels` can't tell a PNP series' reference, so a change to PNP starts that refresh every day until the station's rejection is removed.
- **Download:** `GET /gast/download/modal?uuid=…&parameter=W` gives the range in its date inputs' `min` and `max`. `POST /gast/download/prepare` with `uuid`, `parameter=W`, `start` and `end` (whole local days, both included) and `format=json` answers with a one-time `downloadUrl` to a zip. The zip holds the values, `zeitreiheninformation.txt` with the series' unit and interval, and the terms. JSON timestamps carry their offset; CSV's are local time and repeat an hour each autumn, and ZRXP's are UTC+1 all year with `-777.0` for gaps. Days outside the range come back empty. The form has a box to accept the terms of use, which Open Waters accepted before scripting it. Downloads are one at a time and aren't cached, since each URL works once. After each, the next waits as long as it took, at least 5 seconds and at most a minute, so a slower site gets fewer requests; a download of a few seconds takes several times as long, and one of 5 to 60 seconds about twice as long. A Retry-After longer than the HTTP layer will wait holds the next request as long, and one over 15 minutes stops the run. The station lookup before each download waits its turn in the same queue. A lookup, `prepare` or file download that ends in a 403, 429, 500, 502, 503, 504, timeout or dropped connection isn't asked again (the HTTP layer has already retried the ones worth retrying). Refusals at three different stations with no download completed between them throw `Blocked`: every download after, including those already queued, fails at once, and backfill stops instead of going on through its stations. One gauge refused year after year is a broken series rather than a block, so it fails only its own years. A file that downloads but isn't a readable zip prepares again after a rest, up to three times, since each prepared file can be downloaded only once. The block lasts for the life of the process, which suits a backfill run; the next run picks up where this one stopped.
- **Old values:** run `ingest stations` before a first backfill, so each station has its `history_since`. A station whose list predates `archive` keeps the epoch start it had, at its `validFrom`, and backfill stops there; the R2 store's stations were seeded without one. A station whose `history_since` is null gets nothing from a backfill whose range starts more than 30 days ago, since the month it starts in would be only partly covered; the poller keeps its live values.

### Hamburg Port Authority files

kuestendaten.de's [catalog](https://www.kuestendaten.de/DE/dynamisch/appl/data/daten_prod/prodNeuProd/direct_download/wiski/WISKI_datenDownload.json) lists each series of its WISKI database; each gauge's "Wasserstand (PNP)" series is one zip of about 60 MB holding a tab-separated text file of about 390 MB, with a header before the rows. The adapter refuses a file with any status but 240. Backfill downloads a gauge's zip once a run, starting each request, retries included, 60 seconds after the last as robots.txt asks, and keeps it in memory, about 110 MB a gauge. A file that fails to download or read fails that gauge's every year for the rest of the run.

The weekly canary reads the catalog, not the zips, and fails when it doesn't list each gauge's file under the gauge's number in UTC+1, so a renamed file shows before the next backfill, and when a file's `aufnahmeEnde` passes `BACKFILLED_TO`, the end of the files the store was last backfilled from, so a new update shows too. That failure names any of the six still waiting, since they're published days apart. Once all six have moved, dispatch Ingest with `backfill pegelonline 5952020,5952025,5952030,5952050,5952060,5952065 <year> <year + 1>` over the update's year ([#125](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/issues/125) lists the checks after it), then set `BACKFILLED_TO` to the new `aufnahmeEnde`. Until then the canary stays red, so any other failure in those weeks shares the red run; a zip moved while the catalog stays put, or a change inside a file, still shows only then.

A range that starts more than 30 days back comes from the file alone, even where it runs into the REST API's days, since backfill replaces whole months and the REST API covers only part of its first; the poller asks for hours, so it never reaches the file. Each update's file ends at 23:00 UTC on its last day, so a backfill over that month replaces it without its last hour, which the store then lacks until a backfill over the next update's file restores it. The gap between the last file's end and the start of the poller is filled by a backfill over those years after an update.

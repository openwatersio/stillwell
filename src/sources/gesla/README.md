# GESLA 4.1 (`gesla`)

[GESLA](https://gesla787883612.wordpress.com/) (Global Extreme Sea Level Analysis) 4.1, released in July 2026: 6,682 sea level records from more than 40 agencies (the store lists 6,657, less SANHO's 25 below), from 1-minute to hourly, back to 1800 at the oldest. Each record is the contributor's series in a common format, with GESLA's quality-control flags. Many records copy gauges another store source already serves from its originator; GESLA is in the store for the years and gauges those sources don't have, and `stations.parquet` prefers the originator's series wherever both exist (below).

- **License:** GESLA can be used freely for research. Its [license page](https://gesla787883612.wordpress.com/license/) lists each contributor's data as usable for "research and consultancy", or for research only (CMEMS, the University of Zagreb and the City of Venice), and says access isn't restricted. GESLA serves every record openly, so `redistribution` is true and every record is in the public store, except copies of UHSLC records whose contributor refused republication (below). Commercial use is tagged per station: the stricter of GESLA's table and the originator's own terms where this repo has checked them (BOM, CHS for MEDS's Canadian gauges, UNAM, JODC and NWFWMD aren't commercial; CRMS is, since CPRA puts its data in the public domain, and DMI is, since it licenses its tide-gauge data under CC BY 4.0 however the user obtained it). A contributor GESLA's table doesn't list, since the table is GESLA-3's, is non-commercial until its own terms are read, except Toitū Te Whenua, whose sea level data is CC BY 3.0 NZ. UHSLC's records take their terms from UHSLC's documentation files, as the `uhslc` adapter does: a research-quality record its own file's, a fast-delivery record the strictest terms and every credit of its number's versions. The [table of terms by contributor](contributors.md) lists them, and the [coverage report](coverage.md) says which records the store also has from their originator and which years each side lacks. GESLA asks users to cite Haigh et al. (2023) and Woodworth et al. (2017) and acknowledge gesla.org, and to cite Caldwell et al. (2015) for UHSLC's data; `terms` carries that. Attribution is "GESLA-4.1 (Haigh et al. 2023; Woodworth et al. 2017)" followed by the contributor's credit.
- **South Africa:** GESLA serves the South African Navy Hydrographic Office's gauges openly, 25 records in all: 23 UHSLC records named `-zaf-`, and two of Walvis Bay, in Namibia, whose UHSLC documentation names SANHO. SANHO refused permission to republish any of its data ([#80](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/issues/80), [#82](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/issues/82)), so every UHSLC record that carries SANHO's notice is withheld, and so is every record of a UHSLC station number the `uhslc` source withholds, a version UHSLC's scan hasn't read included: they aren't listed and aren't in the store. The rule covers GESLA's copies of UHSLC records only. GESLA files Lüderitz, in Namibia, under South Africa and names SANHO as the originator of its research-quality record, so both of GESLA's Lüderitz records are withheld, and so is UHSLC's own Lüderitz series, `uhslc/702`, though UHSLC's documentation names no contributor for it.
- **Contact for errors:** `gesla.help@gmail.com`, the address every 4.1 file's header and UHSLC's ERDDAP give for GESLA. The [contact page](https://gesla787883612.wordpress.com/contact/) names two people, whose addresses the store doesn't carry.

## Where the data comes from

GESLA publishes 4.1 three ways: a zip of every record on iCloud (7.94 GB, 69 GB unzipped), a link per record on CloudFront (`d2lyy96te42c83.cloudfront.net/v4-1/<file>`, given in the metadata CSV), and UHSLC's ERDDAP (`global_hourly_gesla`), which holds all 6,682 records. All three serve the same build: a CloudFront file is byte for byte the zip's entry, and ERDDAP's values match.

The store reads the zip. Ingest checks that the zip is the 4.1 release, so a new release under the same link stops ingest rather than mixing two releases' values in the store.

## Stations and series

- **Stations:** one per record, `gesla/<file name>`, as GESLA names its files and TICON its records (`gesla/cuxhavensteubenhft-5990020-deu-wsv`), from the metadata CSV. A character in the name that a URL would have to encode is `_` in the ID, and the list keeps the name as `file`: `byu_queue_de_tortue_@_riceville_la-8012300-usa-usgs` is `gesla/byu_queue_de_tortue___riceville_la-8012300-usa-usgs`. Each record's download link is kept as `source_url`. Names have GESLA's underscores as spaces; country codes are GESLA's ISO alpha-3 as alpha-2; `kind` is GESLA's gauge type (5,307 coastal, 1,160 river and 190 lake in the store). The store's list keeps `contributor` (GESLA's abbreviation), `originator`, `datum_information`, `record_quality` and the record's `record_start` and `record_end`. Every station but Uddevalla's (below) is active, and none is live: the release is static (`hourly` and `finalizes` are false), so the values don't change after the backfill, and only the station list is refreshed, monthly. Records end all along the way: in November 2026, 3,051 end before 2020, 2,061 from 2020 to 2025 and 1,545 in 2026.
- **Linking:** GESLA copies records from originators already in the store, so every GESLA station counts as a copy ([`src/same-as.ts`](../../same-as.ts)), and any other series with levels and the same commercial terms is preferred over it, whether or not that series still reports. Where GESLA's site code is the originator's own ID, the adapter links it: NOAA and Stefan Talke's data archaeology to CO-OPS, USGS (with the leading zero GESLA drops from USGS's parts 01 to 09: every 7-digit number, and longer ones between 100° W and the prime meridian), WSV to PEGELONLINE, SMHI, REFMAR, the Norwegian Hydrographic Service to Kartverket, SFWMD, California DWR (Water Data Library numbers to `dwr`, CDEC codes to `cdec`), JODC's Coast Guard, GSI and port records to `jodc`, SMN's sensor records to `unam`, UHSLC, and CRMS, whose site code links to the site's first surface-water gauge, `crms/CRMS<site>-H01`, which the `crms` adapter links to the site's later gauges, except at the two records that are the site's marsh well throughout. Forty of GESLA's CRMS records are an hour early (erratum `gesla-crms-hour-early`), and most of the others no longer match CPRA's values (see the `crms` README). A UHSLC research-quality record links to GESLA's fast-delivery record of its number where there is one, which links to UHSLC's series of that number, since UHSLC lists the version that series carries under the number alone. The rest are matched by nearness and name. In November 2026, 4,924 of the 6,657 records are linked to another store series. The levels rule (`stations/matches.parquet`, [`src/matches.ts`](../../matches.ts)) links records whose hourly levels agree within 1 cm, including two of GESLA's own records of one gauge, which nearness never links. On 15 November 2026 it linked CMEMS's Uddevalla record to SMHI's `2541`, which the copy rule skips since GESLA calls the record a river gauge and SMHI's a coastal one, though both join CMEMS's own Uddevalla series. It also covered pairs the other rules leave apart: three Delfzijl records; CMEMS's Eemshaven haven and Eems Meetpaal copies of RWS's gauges and its Eider barrier copy of PEGELONLINE's; and CMEMS's record of RWS's Hoek van Holland, whose name shares its words with four RWS gauges. The rule skips non-tidal stations, so decisions in `LINKS` link CMEMS's records of FMI's Föglö Degerby and of WSV's Kiel-Holtenau, Rostock and Lübeck gauges on the Baltic, where Lübeck's tide of 9 cm is at the edge of the store's 8 cm, and of Wangerooge West, which the store marks non-tidal: GESLA places Kiel-Holtenau 3.3 km, Lübeck 4.8 km and Wangerooge 5.2 km from WSV's positions, Rostock's names no side of the Mühlendamm weir, and nearness and the copy rule leave Degerby's two GESLA records apart. Of the IDs, 77 name a station no list has (39 California gauges neither `dwr` nor `cdec` carries, 17 UHSLC series, among them its South African ones, 11 USGS sites, 6 data-archaeology CO-OPS numbers and 4 SFWMD) and 15 sit more than 10 km off; both are dropped and reported at `publish`. GESLA often holds several agencies' copies of one gauge, so two GESLA records may share a group (`ASSEMBLED` in [`src/same-as.ts`](../../same-as.ts)), and a series GESLA names by ID is still matched to its own originator: adding GESLA leaves the store's other links as they were, but for two CO-OPS–UHSLC links and one GESLA record that joins USGS's and DWR's gauges at Rio Vista Bridge. DWR's NAVD88 series end in a Q that GESLA's codes drop, and SFWMD's tailwaters, which GESLA names `…_t`, are SFWMD's `_T` series.
- **Datum:** each record is in its contributor's own datum, which GESLA describes in free text (`DATUM INFORMATION`, kept as `datum_information`; "Unspecified" at about 1,280 of the store's records). The store labels every record `ZERO`, the record's own zero, and gives no offsets. GESLA's RWS records give "Normal Amsterdam Level" (NAP), except four offshore platforms that give "Mean sea level": Borssele Windpark Alpha, Goeree lichteiland, Hollandse kust noord deelgebied and Hollandse Kust Zuid platform Alpha. RWS serves its own platforms against MSL too ([RWS README](../rws/README.md)).
- **Tidal rivers:** many of GESLA's river records are tidal, and QC treats them as tidal water by measurement, as it does every station ([how a station is judged tidal](https://openwatersio.github.io/stillwell/docs/guides/quality-control/#how-a-station-is-judged-tidal)): six hours before a flatline is suspect, or two days where their tide is under 30 cm and their values move in whole centimeters, with the outlier check off, since a tidal river floods too and GESLA is a record of extremes. In November 2026, 689 of the 947 river records with values measure as tidal. Their `kind` stays GESLA's, since the copy rule in [`src/same-as.ts`](../../same-as.ts) matches on it: listing them as `estuary` would link Sacramento River at Hood to CDEC's Middle River near Holt and drop their links to USGS's own river gauges. The verdict comes from a record's last year, so a river that gained or lost its tide during the record, behind a new barrier say, is judged by how it ends. On the 302 tidal records in the store on 2026-11-09, the six hours flag under 0.5% more values than two days at 290, 0.5 to 3% at nine, and over 3% at three: Harris County's Brays Bayou (13%) and Goose Creek (12%) gauges, and the Delaware at Penns Landing (6.5%), whose flagged runs hold one millimeter-resolution value for a median of 9 to 18.5 hours against a lunar tide of 18 cm or more: stuck gauges, not a tide too small to see.
- **Levels:** meters. A missing value (QC flag 5, or the file's `NULL VALUE`) is dropped, as is a level outside the store's span of −500 to 9,000 m (`SPAN_M`), a fill value (erratum `gesla-fill-values`).
- **Flags:** GESLA's QC flag is 0 (no quality control) or 1 (correct), both `good`, since a static release never finalizes anything; the store's own QC still runs on them. Then 2 is interpolated (`interpolated`), 3 doubtful (`suspect`) and 4 a spike or wrong (`bad`). A value whose use-in-analysis flag is 0 is at least `suspect`. `source_flag` keeps these, so the datum-jump check below doesn't hide GESLA's own.
- **Records whose datum jumps can't be judged again:** in November 2026, 52 coastal records' copies in the store miss an end of their record by more than a year, because GESLA's stated span counts the rows its own file marks missing. Their datum jumps can't be judged again from the store, so when QC rules change they keep the flags they have, among them the datum jumps found in GESLA's whole file, with their errata spans added and the current QC run over them, which can only make a flag worse.
- **CRMS records:** CPRA's marsh wells (`W01`) measure water in the marsh soil, not open water, and the store's `crms` adapter leaves them out. Eleven of GESLA's CRMS records hold a well. Nine (CRMS0008, 0058, 0059, 0065, 0089, 0114, 0115, 0117 and 0120) begin with the site's well, from 2007 or 2008 to between 2008 and 2013, and switch to the site's surface-water gauge in the same record. Two more, CRMS0090 and CRMS0128, are the well for the whole record. In CPRA's export of 8 November 2026 each span equals the well's adjusted elevation to the millimeter on every hour, 511,964 hours in all, 0.085 to 0.271 m below it before October 2013. The spans are flagged `suspect`. Neither CRMS0090 nor CRMS0128 is linked to a CRMS series: CPRA lists no surface-water gauge at CRMS0090, and CRMS0128's `H01` ends a year before the record starts. On the clock, 40 of GESLA's CRMS records are an hour early and are moved (erratum `gesla-crms-hour-early`). CRMS0090 and CRMS0128 are an hour early too, but are flagged whole and their times kept as served. Those 42 are the only CRMS records that hold their own site's gauge. GESLA's other 298 CRMS records hold another site's values, also an hour early, and are listed inactive with no products or links (erratum `gesla-crms-wrong-site`); CPRA's own series carry those gauges.

## Errata

- **WSV's hourly means are 30 minutes early** (erratum `gesla-wsv-hourly-means-early`). GESLA stamps WSV's hourly means at the start of the hour they average, so the store moves every hourly WSV record's times 30 minutes later.
- **Hong Kong is an hour early** (erratum `gesla-hong-kong-hour-early`). GESLA copies UHSLC's Hong Kong records (329, 329a, 329b) with UHSLC's hour-early stamps, and the store moves them an hour later.
- **Vigo and Las Palmas's older UHSLC versions are an hour early** (erratum `gesla-spain-uhslc-hour-early`). GESLA copies UHSLC's Vigo 208a and Las Palmas 217a, 217b and 217c with UHSLC's hour-early stamps, and the store moves them an hour later.
- **Santa Cruz de la Palma is an hour early to 1986** (erratum `gesla-santa-cruz-de-la-palma-hour-early`). IEO's record is an hour early before its gap from 1987 to 1995 and on time after it, and the store moves its values from before 1990 an hour later.
- **Progreso's UHSLC version A is 6 hours late** (erratum `gesla-progreso-uhslc-721a-six-hours-late`). GESLA copies UHSLC's 721a with UHSLC's times, and the store moves them 6 hours earlier.
- **Scott Base is on NZST** (erratum `gesla-scott-base-ttw-nzst`). Toitū Te Whenua's Scott Base record is on UTC+12 all year, and the store moves it 12 hours earlier.
- **Fill values are dropped** (erratum `gesla-fill-values`). A level outside the store's span of −500 to 9,000 m (`SPAN_M`), such as CMEMS's 999,999.06 m, is left out as missing, like GESLA's null.
- **Queensland's records are on AEST from 2020** (erratum `gesla-queensland-2020-aest`). Seven Queensland records switch from UTC to UTC+10 at the start of 2020, and the store moves their times from then on 10 hours earlier. From 2020 their labels on day 1 to 12 of a month also have the day and month swapped, which the store swaps back.
- **Vardø's UHSLC copies are 5 minutes late from 2007 to 2018** (erratum `gesla-vardo-uhslc-2007-2018-five-minutes-late`). GESLA copies UHSLC's Vardø records with UHSLC's times; they're kept as served, as UHSLC's are.
- **SFWMD's records are on EST** (erratum `gesla-sfwmd-est`). All 43 South Florida Water Management District records are on UTC−5 all year, and the store moves them 5 hours later.
- **40 CRMS records are an hour early** (erratum `gesla-crms-hour-early`). GESLA's copies of 40 Louisiana CRMS gauges match CPRA's own series once moved an hour later, and the store moves them over each whole record.
- **298 CRMS records hold another site's values** (erratum `gesla-crms-wrong-site`). Every GESLA CRMS record but the 42 above holds another CRMS site's series an hour early, often the next site in GESLA's own list, so the store lists them as inactive with no products or links.
- **11 CRMS records hold a marsh well** (one per record: erratum `gesla-crms0008-marsh-well`, erratum `gesla-crms0058-marsh-well`, erratum `gesla-crms0059-marsh-well`, erratum `gesla-crms0065-marsh-well`, erratum `gesla-crms0089-marsh-well`, erratum `gesla-crms0090-marsh-well`, erratum `gesla-crms0114-marsh-well`, erratum `gesla-crms0115-marsh-well`, erratum `gesla-crms0117-marsh-well`, erratum `gesla-crms0120-marsh-well`, erratum `gesla-crms0128-marsh-well`). The spans that hold the site's marsh well rather than open water are flagged `suspect` (see CRMS records above).
- **Wewak's time zone** (erratum `gesla-wewak-time-zone`). Wewak's record (BOM 56070) is labeled UTC+1 but its values are on UTC, so its times are kept as served.
- **Old UHSLC positions** (erratum `gesla-uhslc-positions`). 22 of GESLA's copies of UHSLC series sit 5 to 112 km from their gauge, at positions UHSLC has since corrected, and the store moves each to the gauge's position from another source.
- **Uddevalla's record is Arkö's** (erratum `gesla-uddevalla-is-arko`). SMHI's Uddevalla record holds the same rows as its Arkö record, from a Baltic gauge 300 km east, so the store lists it as inactive with no values, products or links.
- **St. Pauli's values from the wrong minute** (erratum `gesla-st-pauli-wrong-minute`). From March 2025, about 16% of CMEMS's St. Pauli record's 10-minute values come from another minute than the one stated, and are kept as served.
- **Datum jumps flagged usable** (erratum `gesla-datum-jumps`). Some coastal records change datum partway with every value still flagged correct and usable, and the store flags the segments off the record's main level `suspect`.

## Running ingest

- **Withheld records:** the adapter withholds every UHSLC record that carries SANHO's notice (`refused` in [UHSLC's notice list](../uhslc/notices.ts)) and every record of a UHSLC station number the `uhslc` adapter withholds; `withheld` names them, and the adapter never lists or fetches them. UHSLC's Lüderitz series, `uhslc/702`, is withheld by hand (`ALSO_WITHHELD`). A refusal from one of GESLA's other contributors, such as BOM, would need a rule of its own. [CONTRIBUTING's section on withheld stations](../../../CONTRIBUTING.md#withheld-stations) has how withheld records leave the store.
- **Terms and coverage files:** [`contributors.ts`](contributors.ts) holds each contributor's terms. `npm run gesla:contributors` writes the table of terms by contributor, and `npm run gesla:coverage` the coverage report.
- **Metadata CSV:** its header runs its last two names together (`OVERALL RECORD QUALITYDOWNLOAD LINK`), so columns are found by name and the download link, kept as `source_url`, is each row's last field. Each record's `record_start` and `record_end` bound what backfill asks for. Nothing is fetched after the backfill but the monthly list refresh.
- **Flags:** GESLA's flags 0 and 1 are `good` rather than `preliminary`, since `preliminary` would keep backfill refetching every year.
- **CRMS marsh wells:** `CRMS_WELLS` in [the dropped-records list](dropped.ts) keeps CRMS0090's and CRMS0128's site codes from linking, and `UNLINKS` in [`src/same-as.ts`](../../same-as.ts) keeps CRMS0128 from the `H01` 25 m away, which nearness would link.
- **Uddevalla and the CRMS records of other sites:** `isDropped` in [the dropped-records list](dropped.ts), from Uddevalla's file name in `DROPPED` and, for CRMS, every record but `CRMS_EARLY` and `CRMS_WELLS`, lists each record as inactive with no products and no links, and the adapter refuses to fetch it, so `backfill all` skips it. No link joins it to a group ([`src/same-as.ts`](../../same-as.ts)), a link to it from an adapter's `same_as` or from `LINKS` is dropped and reported, it is its own preferred series, and the TICON inventory doesn't offer it as a GESLA copy. A release that drops or renames Uddevalla's file fails the list refresh until `DROPPED` is updated. `ingest drop gesla` deletes their stored files and backfilled years, except those of the ten CRMS records in `KEEP_FILES`, whose values match nothing else in the store.

### Reading the release

The adapter reads the zip. Each entry is deflated on its own and the iCloud link answers HTTP Range requests, so a record is two ranged reads (its local header, then its data) and an inflate, after one read of the zip's central directory (765 KB) per run. A whole backfill moves the 8 GB compressed. CloudFront would move the 69 GB uncompressed, and it blocked further requests ("Request blocked", 403) for about ten minutes after some 15 files in a few minutes. ERDDAP answers per query, so a backfill a year at a time would be some 130,000 queries on UHSLC's small server, the same one the `uhslc` adapter uses.

The iCloud links are CloudKit shares: a POST of the share ID (`092OldNDh1CEOTcC1atuThsww` for the zip, `02fFab01rq4LdVcwRCf5QCyCQ` for the metadata CSV) to CloudKit's `records/resolve` returns `rootRecord.fields.fileContent.value.downloadURL`, with `${f}` standing for a file name. The URL is signed for 15 minutes, its expiry the `e` query parameter in Unix seconds, so the adapter keeps no copy on disk: it shares one URL among a run's workers until a minute before `e`, resolves a new one then, and resolves again once when a read on it fails. A failure on an older URL leaves a newer one in place, so a burst of failures resolves once, not once per worker. The share's size must be the 4.1 zip's (`release.bytes`), so a new release under the same share stops the adapter rather than mixing two releases' values. This isn't a documented API; if it changes, the canary fails, and the CloudFront links or ERDDAP are the fallbacks.

### Backfill

The Monthly workflow refreshes GESLA's list on the 2nd with every other source's; a first run dispatches Monthly with `sources: gesla`. Then backfill through the Ingest workflow, the ended years first, then 2026 on its own, since backfill fetches a year still under way on every run and `--skip-written` never skips it:

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill gesla all 1800 2026 --skip-written
```

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill gesla all 2026 2027
```

Years whose flags were written under other rules follow a station's analysis once `analyze gesla --requalify` requalifies it, or a requalify of every record:

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts requalify gesla all 1800 2027 --shard 1/8
```

`all` reads every record's files and writes those whose flags change: the stations whose analysis changed their rules, and any other year that backfill judged before the year after it was written. A coastal record whose copy in the store misses an end of its record by more than a year, as 52 do in November 2026 because GESLA's stated span counts the rows its own file marks missing, can't have its datum jumps judged again from the store, so requalify keeps the flags it has, among them the datum jumps backfill found in GESLA's whole file, adds its errata spans and runs the current QC, which can only make a flag worse, and lists it under warnings.

Years outside each record's span ask for nothing. For the seven Queensland records the span runs a year either side of the labels, since a swapped day can move a value to any month of its year. Each record is read once per run and kept while backfill asks for its years (`KEEP`, the eight used last), the largest, Fremantle Fishing Boat Harbour, being 197 MB of text, which peaks at about 1.3 GB while it's parsed and keeps about 100 MB. A run cut short resumes with `--skip-written`. A written year is complete, so neither that nor a plain backfill reads it again: a change to how the adapter reads records, such as a clock fix, reaches the store only through `--refetch` of the records and years it touches.

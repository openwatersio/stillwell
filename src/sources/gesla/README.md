# GESLA 4.1 (`gesla`)

[GESLA](https://gesla787883612.wordpress.com/) (Global Extreme Sea Level Analysis) 4.1, released in July 2026: 6,682 sea level records from more than 40 agencies (the store lists 6,657, less SANHO's 25 below), from 1-minute to hourly, back to 1800 at the oldest. Each record is the contributor's series in a common format, with GESLA's quality-control flags. Many records copy gauges another store source already serves from its originator; GESLA is in the store for the years and gauges those sources don't have, and `stations.parquet` prefers the originator's series wherever both exist (below).

- **License:** GESLA can be used freely for research. Its [license page](https://gesla787883612.wordpress.com/license/) lists each contributor's data as usable for "research and consultancy", or for research only (CMEMS, the University of Zagreb and the City of Venice), and says access isn't restricted. GESLA serves every record openly, so `redistribution` is true and every record is in the public store, except copies of UHSLC records whose contributor refused republication (below). Commercial use is tagged per station: the stricter of GESLA's table and the originator's own terms where this repo has checked them (BOM, CHS for MEDS's Canadian gauges, DMI's notice on UHSLC's records of the same gauges, UNAM, JODC and NWFWMD aren't commercial; CRMS is, since CPRA puts its data in the public domain). A contributor GESLA's table doesn't list, since the table is GESLA-3's, is non-commercial until its own terms are read, except Toitū Te Whenua, whose sea level data is CC BY 3.0 NZ. UHSLC's records take their terms from UHSLC's documentation files, as the `uhslc` adapter does: a research-quality record its own file's, a fast-delivery record the strictest terms and every credit of its number's versions. [contributors.md](contributors.md), from `npm run gesla:contributors`, has the table by contributor; [`contributors.ts`](contributors.ts) holds the terms. GESLA asks users to cite Haigh et al. (2023) and Woodworth et al. (2017) and acknowledge gesla.org, and to cite Caldwell et al. (2015) for UHSLC's data; `terms` carries that. Attribution is "GESLA-4.1 (Haigh et al. 2023; Woodworth et al. 2017)" followed by the contributor's credit.
- **South Africa:** GESLA serves the South African Navy Hydrographic Office's gauges openly, 25 records in all: 23 UHSLC records named `-zaf-`, and two of Walvis Bay, in Namibia, whose UHSLC documentation names SANHO. SANHO refused permission to republish any of its data ([#80](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/issues/80), [#82](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/issues/82)), so the adapter withholds every UHSLC record that carries SANHO's notice (`refused` in [notices.ts](../uhslc/notices.ts)), and every record of a UHSLC station number the `uhslc` adapter withholds, a version UHSLC's scan hasn't read included: `withheld` names them, and the adapter never lists or fetches them, so they aren't in the store. The rule covers GESLA's copies of UHSLC records only; a refusal from one of GESLA's other contributors, such as BOM, would need a rule of its own. GESLA files Lüderitz, in Namibia, under South Africa and names SANHO as the originator of its research-quality record, so both of GESLA's Lüderitz records are withheld, and so is UHSLC's own Lüderitz series, `uhslc/702`, by hand (`ALSO_WITHHELD`), though UHSLC's documentation names no contributor for it. [CONTRIBUTING.md](../../../CONTRIBUTING.md#withheld-stations) has how withheld records leave the store.
- **Contact for errors:** `gesla.help@gmail.com`, the address every 4.1 file's header and UHSLC's ERDDAP give for GESLA. The [contact page](https://gesla787883612.wordpress.com/contact/) names two people, whose addresses the store doesn't carry.

## Where the data comes from

GESLA publishes 4.1 three ways: a zip of every record on iCloud (7.94 GB, 69 GB unzipped), a link per record on CloudFront (`d2lyy96te42c83.cloudfront.net/v4-1/<file>`, given in the metadata CSV), and UHSLC's ERDDAP (`global_hourly_gesla`), which holds all 6,682 records. All three serve the same build: a CloudFront file is byte for byte the zip's entry, and ERDDAP's values match.

The adapter reads the zip. Each entry is deflated on its own and the iCloud link answers HTTP Range requests, so a record is two ranged reads (its local header, then its data) and an inflate, after one read of the zip's central directory (765 KB) per run. A whole backfill moves the 8 GB compressed. CloudFront would move the 69 GB uncompressed, and it blocked further requests ("Request blocked", 403) for about ten minutes after some 15 files in a few minutes. ERDDAP answers per query, so a backfill a year at a time would be some 130,000 queries on UHSLC's small server, the same one the `uhslc` adapter uses.

The iCloud links are CloudKit shares: a POST of the share ID (`092OldNDh1CEOTcC1atuThsww` for the zip, `02fFab01rq4LdVcwRCf5QCyCQ` for the metadata CSV) to CloudKit's `records/resolve` returns `rootRecord.fields.fileContent.value.downloadURL`, with `${f}` standing for a file name. The URL is signed for 15 minutes, its expiry the `e` query parameter in Unix seconds, so the adapter keeps no copy on disk: it shares one URL among a run's workers until a minute before `e`, resolves a new one then, and resolves again once when a read on it fails. A failure on an older URL leaves a newer one in place, so a burst of failures resolves once, not once per worker. The share's size must be the 4.1 zip's (`release.bytes`), so a new release under the same share stops the adapter rather than mixing two releases' values. This isn't a documented API; if it changes, the canary fails, and the CloudFront links or ERDDAP are the fallbacks.

## Stations and series

- **Stations:** one per record, `gesla/<file name>`, as GESLA names its files and TICON its records (`gesla/cuxhavensteubenhft-5990020-deu-wsv`), from the metadata CSV. Its header runs its last two names together (`OVERALL RECORD QUALITYDOWNLOAD LINK`), so columns are found by name and the download link, kept as `source_url`, is each row's last field. Names have GESLA's underscores as spaces; country codes are GESLA's ISO alpha-3 as alpha-2; `kind` is GESLA's gauge type (5,307 coastal, 1,160 river and 190 lake in the store). The store's list keeps `contributor` (GESLA's abbreviation), `originator`, `datum_information`, `record_quality` and the record's `record_start` and `record_end`, which bound what backfill asks for. Every station but Uddevalla's (below) is active, and none is live: the release is static (`hourly` and `finalizes` are false), so nothing is fetched after the backfill but the monthly list refresh. Records end all along the way: in November 2026, 3,051 end before 2020, 2,061 from 2020 to 2025 and 1,545 in 2026.
- **Linking:** GESLA copies records from originators already in the store, so every GESLA station counts as a copy ([`src/same-as.ts`](../../same-as.ts)), and any other series with levels and the same commercial terms is preferred over it, whether or not that series still reports. Where GESLA's site code is the originator's own ID, the adapter links it: NOAA and Stefan Talke's data archaeology to CO-OPS, USGS (with the leading zero GESLA drops from USGS's parts 01 to 09: every 7-digit number, and longer ones between 100° W and the prime meridian), WSV to PEGELONLINE, SMHI, REFMAR, the Norwegian Hydrographic Service to Kartverket, SFWMD, California DWR (Water Data Library numbers to `dwr`, CDEC codes to `cdec`), UHSLC, and CRMS, whose site code links to the site's first surface-water gauge, `crms/CRMS<site>-H01`, which the `crms` adapter links to the site's later gauges. Forty of GESLA's CRMS records are an hour early (erratum `gesla-crms-hour-early`), and most of the others no longer match CPRA's values (see the `crms` README). A UHSLC research-quality record links to GESLA's fast-delivery record of its number where there is one, which links to UHSLC's series of that number, since UHSLC lists the version that series carries under the number alone. The rest are matched by nearness and name. In November 2026, 4,924 of the 6,657 records are linked to another store series. Decisions in `LINKS` include CMEMS's Uddevalla record, which GESLA calls a river gauge and SMHI's `2541` a coastal one, so the copy rule skips it. Others join records the cross-station checks found to hold one gauge's values: three Delfzijl records, CMEMS's Eemshaven haven and Eems Meetpaal copies of RWS's gauges, CMEMS's Eider barrier copy of PEGELONLINE's, and CMEMS's copy of FMI's Föglö Degerby. Nearness and the copy rule leave these pairs unlinked, five of them because both are GESLA records. Four more link CMEMS's records of WSV's Rostock, Wangerooge, Kiel-Holtenau and Lübeck gauges to PEGELONLINE: GESLA places three of them beyond the copy rule's 2 km, and Rostock's names no side of the Mühlendamm weir. Of the IDs, 77 name a station no list has (39 California gauges neither `dwr` nor `cdec` carries, 17 UHSLC series, among them its South African ones, 11 USGS sites, 6 data-archaeology CO-OPS numbers and 4 SFWMD) and 15 sit more than 10 km off; both are dropped and reported at `publish`. GESLA often holds several agencies' copies of one gauge, so two GESLA records may share a group (`ASSEMBLED` in [`src/same-as.ts`](../../same-as.ts)), and a series GESLA names by ID is still matched to its own originator: adding GESLA leaves the store's other links as they were, but for two CO-OPS–UHSLC links and one GESLA record that joins USGS's and DWR's gauges at Rio Vista Bridge. DWR's NAVD88 series end in a Q that GESLA's codes drop, and SFWMD's tailwaters, which GESLA names `…_t`, are SFWMD's `_T` series.
- **Datum:** each record is in its contributor's own datum, which GESLA describes in free text (`DATUM INFORMATION`, kept as `datum_information`; "Unspecified" at about 1,280 of the store's records). The store labels every record `ZERO`, the record's own zero, and gives no offsets. GESLA's RWS records give "Normal Amsterdam Level" (NAP), except four offshore platforms that give "Mean sea level": Borssele Windpark Alpha, Goeree lichteiland, Hollandse kust noord deelgebied and Hollandse Kust Zuid platform Alpha. RWS serves its own platforms against MSL too ([RWS README](../rws/README.md)).
- **Tidal rivers:** many of GESLA's river records are tidal, and QC treats them as tidal water by measurement, as it does every station ([stations.md](../../../docs/stations.md#analysis)): six hours before a flatline is suspect, or two days where their tide is under 30 cm and their values move in whole centimeters, with the outlier check off, since a tidal river floods too and GESLA is a record of extremes. In November 2026, 689 of the 947 river records with values measure as tidal. Their `kind` stays GESLA's, since the copy rule in [`src/same-as.ts`](../../same-as.ts) matches on it: listing them as `estuary` would link Sacramento River at Hood to CDEC's Middle River near Holt and drop their links to USGS's own river gauges. The verdict comes from a record's last year, so a river that gained or lost its tide during the record, behind a new barrier say, is judged by how it ends. On the 302 tidal records in the store on 2026-11-09, the six hours flag under 0.5% more values than two days at 290, 0.5 to 3% at nine, and over 3% at three: Harris County's Brays Bayou (13%) and Goose Creek (12%) gauges, and the Delaware at Penns Landing (6.5%), whose flagged runs hold one millimeter-resolution value for a median of 9 to 18.5 hours against a lunar tide of 18 cm or more: stuck gauges, not a tide too small to see.
- **Levels:** meters. A missing value (QC flag 5, or the file's `NULL VALUE`) is dropped.
- **Flags:** GESLA's QC flag is 0 (no quality control) or 1 (correct), both `good`, since a static release never finalizes anything and `preliminary` would keep backfill refetching every year; the store's own QC still runs on them. Then 2 is interpolated (`interpolated`), 3 doubtful (`suspect`) and 4 a spike or wrong (`bad`). A value whose use-in-analysis flag is 0 is at least `suspect`. `source_flag` keeps these, so the datum-jump check below doesn't hide GESLA's own.

## Errata

- **WSV's hourly means are 30 minutes early** (erratum `gesla-wsv-hourly-means-early`). 4.1 fixes 4.0's local time labeled UTC at WSV, but its WSV values are hourly means stamped at the start of the hour they average: Cuxhaven's value labeled T in 2015 equals the mean of PEGELONLINE's 1-minute values from T to T + 60 minutes, to 3.7 mm. The adapter moves every hourly WSV record's times 30 minutes later. BfG's copy of Cuxhaven is instantaneous and on time.
- **Hong Kong is an hour early** (erratum `gesla-hong-kong-hour-early`). GESLA copies UHSLC's Hong Kong records (329, 329a, 329b) with UHSLC's stamps, an hour early, as the `uhslc` adapter's erratum `uhslc-hong-kong-hour-early` describes. The adapter moves them an hour later.
- **40 CRMS records are an hour early** (erratum `gesla-crms-hour-early`). GESLA's copies of 40 Louisiana CRMS gauges, at sites CRMS0002 to CRMS0148, equal CPRA's own series to the millimeter on 94 to 100% of hours in every year CPRA's export covers once moved an hour later, after removing each year's median offset. The adapter moves the records the erratum lists, over each whole record. GESLA's other CRMS records match CPRA's values too rarely to show their clock, or have none to compare, so they're kept as served.
- **Wewak's time zone** (erratum `gesla-wewak-time-zone`). Wewak's record (BOM 56070) is the only one not labeled UTC: it says UTC+1, but fits UHSLC's TICON record of the gauge within 10 minutes as served, and 70 minutes off moved by the hour. Its times are kept as served; any other record labeled off UTC is refused.
- **Old UHSLC positions** (erratum `gesla-uhslc-positions`). GESLA's copies of UHSLC series keep the positions UHSLC had when GESLA copied them, and UHSLC has corrected most since. 22 records in the store sit 5 to 112 km from their gauge, such as Meulaboh 111 km north and Langkawi 49 km north in Thailand. `POSITIONS` in [src/positions.ts](../../positions.ts) moves each to the gauge's position from another source, which also lets their links to UHSLC's own series hold within 10 km. Ambon, Sabang and Davao 372A disagree with UHSLC by 5 to 7 km too, but no other source settles which is right, so they're kept as served.
- **Uddevalla's record is Arkö's** (erratum `gesla-uddevalla-is-arko`). SMHI's Uddevalla record (`uddevalla-2541-swe-smhi`) holds the same rows as its Arkö record (`arko-2545-swe-smhi`), from a Baltic gauge 300 km east: both match SMHI's own Arkö series to the millimeter over 2014–2026, and correlate with SMHI's Uddevalla at only r 0.2 to 0.36. TICON-4's Uddevalla record, fitted from it, has Arkö's constants. The adapter lists the record as inactive, with no products and no links (`DROPPED` in [dropped.ts](dropped.ts)), and refuses to fetch it, so `backfill all` skips it. No link joins it to a group ([`src/same-as.ts`](../../same-as.ts)), a link to it from an adapter's `same_as` or from `LINKS` is dropped and reported, it is its own preferred series, and the TICON inventory doesn't offer it as a GESLA copy. A release that drops or renames the file fails the list refresh until `DROPPED` is updated. SMHI's own series (`smhi/2541`) and CMEMS's record (`uddevalla-udd-swe-cmems`) carry Uddevalla.
- **St. Pauli's values from the wrong minute** (erratum `gesla-st-pauli-wrong-minute`). From March 2025 to the record's end in May 2026, CMEMS's St. Pauli record (`stpaulitg-stp-deu-cmems`) has about 16% of its 10-minute values taken from another minute than the one stated, most 1 or 2 cm off and some up to 42 cm at mid-tide, all flagged correct and usable. Until March 2025 it matches HPA's archive at every shared time, once the 5.004 m between NHN and the gauge zero is applied. The values are kept as served; the group's preferred station, `pegelonline/5952050`, takes its history from HPA's archive on kuestendaten.de, which agrees with the neighboring gauges where the two copies differ.
- **Datum jumps flagged usable** (erratum `gesla-datum-jumps`). Some records change datum partway with every value still flagged correct and usable: Cape d'Or's 1965 values sit about 20 m above its 1969–79 ones, Mazatlán's float and radar gauges are 0.9 to 2.5 m off for months of 2021, Ōminato's 1952 values are 2 m above the rest, Gorge at Craigflower's 1969 and 2011 values sit 1.3 m apart, and Papeete's CMEMS record of UHSLC's gauge is 0.95 m high from September 2010 to September 2011. `datumJumps` checks every coastal record on the raw levels: it takes daily means of the usable values on days they span at least 12 hours of, finds where the median of the next 30 such days differs from the previous 30's (as few as 5 near the record's ends) by more than 0.75 m, places the jump at the largest day-to-day step nearby (at least half of 0.75 m, since a jump partway through a day splits across two days), and flags every segment whose median is more than 0.75 m from the longest segment's as `suspect`. Rivers and lakes aren't checked, tidal ones included, since their floods move monthly medians that far: the check would flag 30% of the Connecticut at Hartford's values and 15% of the Columbia at Vancouver's. On 80 coastal records picked at random, it flagged two, both of which GESLA itself marks "Possible datum issues". It leaves the earthquake steps at Ayukawa and Onahama (Tōhoku, 2011), Kaikōura and Wellington (2016) alone.
- **Checks of 4.1:** JODC's Japan Coast Guard records (`jodc_jcg`) keep every year-end hour, which slackwater-database found missing in 4.0 after local midnight on December 31: Monbetsu, Urakawa, Hakata and Syowa. RWS's records are on UTC.

**Clock check** ([`scripts/clock-check.ts`](../../../scripts/clock-check.ts), 2015, against TICON records of the same gauges from other contributors where TICON has them): with the corrections, Cuxhaven (against UHSLC's and BfG's records), Helgoland (CMEMS), Hoek van Holland and Vlissingen (RWS's 4.1 records against CMEMS), Hong Kong 329 and 329b (against slackwater-database's re-fit) and Seattle fit best at 0 minutes, and K1 within 16 minutes. Travemünde and Stockholm's Baltic tides are too small to say (r 0.24 and 0.25).

## Backfill

The Monthly workflow refreshes GESLA's list on the 2nd with every other source's; a first run dispatches Monthly with `sources: gesla`. Then backfill through the Ingest workflow, the ended years first, then 2026 on its own, since backfill fetches a year still under way on every run and `--skip-written` never skips it:

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill gesla all 1800 2026 --skip-written
```

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill gesla all 2026 2027
```

Years whose flags were written under other rules follow a station's analysis once `analyze gesla --requalify` requalifies it, or a requalify of every record:

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts requalify gesla all 1800 2027 --shard 1/8
```

`all` reads every record's files and writes those whose flags change: the stations whose analysis changed their rules, and any other year that backfill judged before the year after it was written. A coastal record whose copy in the store misses an end of its record by more than a year, as 52 do in November 2026 because GESLA's stated span counts the rows its own file marks missing, can't have its datum jumps judged again from the store, so requalify keeps the flags it has, among them the datum jumps backfill found in GESLA's whole file, adds its errata spans and runs the current QC, which can only make a flag worse, and lists it under warnings.

Years outside each record's span ask for nothing. Each record is read once per run and kept while backfill asks for its years (`KEEP`, the eight used last), the largest, Fremantle Fishing Boat Harbour, being 197 MB of text, which peaks at about 1.3 GB while it's parsed and keeps about 100 MB. A run cut short resumes with `--skip-written`.

# GESLA 4.1 (`gesla`)

[GESLA](https://gesla787883612.wordpress.com/) (Global Extreme Sea Level Analysis) 4.1, released in July 2026: 6,682 sea level records from more than 40 agencies, from 1-minute to hourly, back to 1800 at the oldest. Each record is the contributor's series in a common format, with GESLA's quality-control flags. Many records copy gauges another lake source already serves from its originator; GESLA is in the lake for the years and gauges those sources don't have, and `stations.parquet` prefers the originator's series wherever both exist (below).

- **License:** GESLA can be used freely for research. Its [license page](https://gesla787883612.wordpress.com/license/) lists each contributor's data as usable for "research and consultancy", or for research only (CMEMS, the University of Zagreb and the City of Venice), and says access isn't restricted. GESLA serves every record openly, so `redistribution` is true and every record is in the public lake, except copies of UHSLC records whose contributor refused republication (below). Commercial use is tagged per station: the stricter of GESLA's table and the originator's own terms where this repo has checked them (BOM, CHS for MEDS's Canadian gauges, DMI's notice on UHSLC's records of the same gauges, UNAM, JODC, CRMS and NWFWMD aren't commercial). A contributor GESLA's table doesn't list, since the table is GESLA-3's, is non-commercial until its own terms are read, except Toitū Te Whenua, whose sea level data is CC BY 3.0 NZ. UHSLC's records take their terms from UHSLC's documentation files, as the `uhslc` adapter does: a research-quality record its own file's, a fast-delivery record the strictest terms and every credit of its number's versions. [docs/gesla-contributors.md](../../../docs/gesla-contributors.md), from `npm run gesla:contributors`, has the table by contributor; [`contributors.ts`](contributors.ts) holds the terms. GESLA asks users to cite Haigh et al. (2023) and Woodworth et al. (2017) and acknowledge gesla.org, and to cite Caldwell et al. (2015) for UHSLC's data; `terms` carries that. Attribution is "GESLA-4.1 (Haigh et al. 2023; Woodworth et al. 2017)" followed by the contributor's credit.
- **South Africa:** GESLA serves the South African Navy Hydrographic Office's gauges openly: 23 UHSLC records named `-zaf-`, and two of Walvis Bay, in Namibia, whose UHSLC documentation names SANHO. SANHO refused permission to republish any of its data ([#80](https://github.com/openwatersio/water-levels/issues/80), [#82](https://github.com/openwatersio/water-levels/issues/82)), so the adapter withholds every UHSLC record that carries SANHO's notice (`refused` in [notices.ts](../uhslc/notices.ts)), and every record of a UHSLC station number the `uhslc` adapter withholds, a version UHSLC's scan hasn't read included: `withheld` names them, and the adapter never lists or fetches them, so they aren't in the lake. The rule covers GESLA's copies of UHSLC records only; a refusal from one of GESLA's other contributors, such as BOM, would need a rule of its own. GESLA files Lüderitz, in Namibia, under South Africa and names SANHO as the originator of its research-quality record, so both of GESLA's Lüderitz records are withheld, and so is UHSLC's own Lüderitz series, `uhslc/702`, by hand (`ALSO_WITHHELD`), though UHSLC's documentation names no contributor for it. [CONTRIBUTING.md](../../../CONTRIBUTING.md#withheld-stations) has how withheld records leave the lake.
- **Contact for errors:** `gesla.help@gmail.com`, the address every 4.1 file's header and UHSLC's ERDDAP give for GESLA. The [contact page](https://gesla787883612.wordpress.com/contact/) names two people, whose addresses the lake doesn't carry.

## Where the data comes from

GESLA publishes 4.1 three ways: a zip of every record on iCloud (7.94 GB, 69 GB unzipped), a link per record on CloudFront (`d2lyy96te42c83.cloudfront.net/v4-1/<file>`, given in the metadata CSV), and UHSLC's ERDDAP (`global_hourly_gesla`), which now holds all 6,682 records. All three serve the same build: a CloudFront file is byte for byte the zip's entry, and ERDDAP's values match.

The adapter reads the zip. Each entry is deflated on its own and the iCloud link answers HTTP Range requests, so a record is two ranged reads (its local header, then its data) and an inflate, after one read of the zip's central directory (765 KB) per run. A whole backfill moves the 8 GB compressed. CloudFront would move the 69 GB uncompressed, and it blocked further requests ("Request blocked", 403) for about ten minutes after some 15 files in a few minutes. ERDDAP answers per query, so a backfill a year at a time would be some 130,000 queries on UHSLC's small server, the same one the `uhslc` adapter uses.

The iCloud links are CloudKit shares: a POST of the share ID (`092OldNDh1CEOTcC1atuThsww` for the zip, `02fFab01rq4LdVcwRCf5QCyCQ` for the metadata CSV) to CloudKit's `records/resolve` returns `rootRecord.fields.fileContent.value.downloadURL`, with `${f}` standing for a file name. The URL is signed and expires within an hour or two, so it's resolved for every record and never cached. The share's size must be the 4.1 zip's (`release.bytes`), so a new release under the same share stops the adapter rather than mixing two releases' values. This isn't a documented API; if it changes, the canary fails, and the CloudFront links or ERDDAP are the fallbacks.

## Stations and series

- **Stations:** one per record, `gesla/<file name>`, as GESLA names its files and TICON its records (`gesla/cuxhavensteubenhft-5990020-deu-wsv`), from the metadata CSV. Its header runs its last two names together (`OVERALL RECORD QUALITYDOWNLOAD LINK`), so columns are found by name and the download link, kept as `source_url`, is each row's last field. Names have GESLA's underscores as spaces; country codes are GESLA's ISO alpha-3 as alpha-2; `kind` is GESLA's gauge type (5,332 coastal, 1,160 river, 190 lake). The lake's list keeps `contributor` (GESLA's abbreviation), `originator`, `datum_information`, `record_quality` and the record's `record_start` and `record_end`, which bound what backfill asks for. Every station but Uddevalla's (below) is active, and none is live: the release is static (`hourly` and `finalizes` are false), so nothing is fetched after the backfill but the monthly list refresh.
- **Linking:** GESLA copies records from originators already in the lake, so every GESLA station counts as a copy (`src/same-as.ts`), and any other series with levels and the same commercial terms is preferred over it, whether or not that series still reports. Where GESLA's site code is the originator's own ID, the adapter links it: NOAA and Stefan Talke's data archaeology to CO-OPS, USGS (with the leading zero GESLA drops from USGS's parts 01 to 09: every 7-digit number, and longer ones between 100° W and the prime meridian), WSV to PEGELONLINE, SMHI, REFMAR, the Norwegian Hydrographic Service to Kartverket, SFWMD, California DWR (Water Data Library numbers to `dwr`, CDEC codes to `cdec`) and UHSLC. A UHSLC research-quality record links to GESLA's fast-delivery record of its number where there is one, which links to UHSLC's series of that number, since UHSLC lists the version that series carries under the number alone. The rest are matched by nearness and name. Against the lake's lists on 2026-11-08, 4,602 of the 6,682 records are linked to another lake series, through 3,502 links by ID, 1,553 by nearness, 473 as copies and one by a person's decision (a record can have several). That one is CMEMS's Uddevalla record, which GESLA calls a river gauge and SMHI's `2541` a coastal one, so the copy rule skips it. Of the IDs, 77 name a station no list has (39 California gauges neither `dwr` nor `cdec` carries, 17 UHSLC series, among them its South African ones, 11 USGS sites, 6 data-archaeology CO-OPS numbers and 4 SFWMD) and 15 sit more than 10 km off; both are dropped and reported at `publish`. GESLA often holds several agencies' copies of one gauge, so two GESLA records may share a group (`ASSEMBLED` in `src/same-as.ts`), and a series GESLA names by ID is still matched to its own originator: adding GESLA leaves the lake's other links as they were, but for two CO-OPS–UHSLC links and one GESLA record that joins USGS's and DWR's gauges at Rio Vista Bridge. Any other series with levels and the same terms is preferred over GESLA's copy. DWR's NAVD88 series end in a Q that GESLA's codes drop, and SFWMD's tailwaters, which GESLA names `…_t`, are SFWMD's `_T` series.
- **Datum:** each record is in its contributor's own datum, which GESLA describes in free text (`DATUM INFORMATION`, kept as `datum_information`; "Unspecified" at 1,304 records). The lake labels every record `ZERO`, the record's own zero, and gives no offsets.
- **Tidal rivers:** a `river` or `lake` station gets two days before a flatline is suspect (`qcFor` in `src/lake/recent.ts`), since a pooled river holds one level for a day or more, but a tide gauge never does, and many of GESLA's river records are tidal. So 866 of its 1,349 river and lake records, those in `TIDAL_RIVERS` (`tidal-rivers.ts`), get the default six hours, through the source's `tidal`, or two days where their values move in whole centimeters, as SMHI's Göteborg gauges' do, since a 9 cm tide can sit on one centimeter for hours. They keep the outlier check off, as every river does, since a tidal river floods too and GESLA is a record of extremes. Their `kind` stays GESLA's, since the copy rule in `src/same-as.ts` matches on it: listing them as `estuary` would link Sacramento River at Hood to CDEC's Middle River near Holt and drop their links to USGS's own river gauges. `node scripts/gesla-tidal-rivers.ts` writes the list from the release: the records whose M2 and O1 amplitudes, fitted over their last 366 days of usable values (spanning at least 30 days, at most 3 hours apart), add to 8 cm or more (`tidal` and `TIDE_M` in `tidal.ts`). That year decides for the whole record, so a river that gained or lost its tide during the record, behind a new barrier say, is judged by how it ends. Only lunar constituents count, since a daily schedule such as a hydropower dam's puts power in the solar S2 and K1: CO-OPS's Ashland Avenue, on the Niagara, fits 74 cm across M2, S2, K1 and O1 but 2.5 cm of M2. O1 takes in the Gulf of Mexico's diurnal tides, where M2 is a few centimeters. Pooled rivers and the Great Lakes fit under 5 cm and most tidal rivers over 20 cm; 8 cm takes in Göteborg's and Uddevalla's harbors (about 9 cm) and leaves out Fredericton's freshets (6 cm). On the 302 tidal records in the lake on 2026-11-09, the shorter limit flags under 0.5% more values at 290, 0.5 to 3% at nine, and over 3% at three: Harris County's Brays Bayou (13%) and Goose Creek (12%) gauges, and the Delaware at Penns Landing (6.5%), whose flagged runs hold one millimeter-resolution value for a median of 9 to 18.5 hours against a lunar tide of 18 cm or more: stuck gauges, not a tide too small to see. Years written before this rule keep their flags until `requalify` runs over them (Backfill, below).
- **Levels:** meters. A missing value (QC flag 5, or the file's `NULL VALUE`) is dropped.
- **Flags:** GESLA's QC flag is 0 (no quality control) or 1 (correct), both `good`, since a static release never finalizes anything and `preliminary` would keep backfill refetching every year; the lake's own QC still runs on them. Then 2 is interpolated (`interpolated`), 3 doubtful (`suspect`) and 4 a spike or wrong (`bad`). A value whose use-in-analysis flag is 0 is at least `suspect`. `source_flag` keeps these, so the datum-jump check below doesn't hide GESLA's own.

## Errata

- **WSV's hourly means are 30 minutes early** (erratum `gesla-wsv-hourly-means-early`). 4.1 fixes 4.0's local time labeled UTC at WSV, but its WSV values are hourly means stamped at the start of the hour they average: Cuxhaven's value labeled T in 2015 equals the mean of PEGELONLINE's 1-minute values from T to T + 60 minutes, to 3.7 mm. The adapter moves every hourly WSV record's times 30 minutes later. BfG's copy of Cuxhaven is instantaneous and on time.
- **Hong Kong is an hour early** (erratum `gesla-hong-kong-hour-early`). GESLA copies UHSLC's Hong Kong records (329, 329a, 329b) with UHSLC's stamps, an hour early, as the `uhslc` adapter's erratum `uhslc-hong-kong-hour-early` describes. The adapter moves them an hour later.
- **Wewak's time zone** (erratum `gesla-wewak-time-zone`). Wewak's record (BOM 56070) is the only one not labeled UTC: it says UTC+1, but fits UHSLC's TICON record of the gauge within 10 minutes as served, and 70 minutes off moved by the hour. Its times are kept as served; any other record labeled off UTC is refused.
- **Old UHSLC positions** (erratum `gesla-uhslc-positions`). GESLA's copies of UHSLC series keep the positions UHSLC had when GESLA copied them, and UHSLC has corrected most since. 22 records in the lake sit 5 to 112 km from their gauge, such as Meulaboh 111 km north and Langkawi 49 km north in Thailand. `POSITIONS` in [src/positions.ts](../../positions.ts) moves each to the gauge's position from another source, which also lets their links to UHSLC's own series hold within 10 km. Ambon, Sabang and Davao 372A disagree with UHSLC by 5 to 7 km too, but no other source settles which is right, so they're kept as served.
- **Uddevalla's record is Arkö's** (erratum `gesla-uddevalla-is-arko`). SMHI's Uddevalla record (`uddevalla-2541-swe-smhi`) holds the same rows as its Arkö record (`arko-2545-swe-smhi`), from a Baltic gauge 300 km east: both match SMHI's own Arkö series to the millimeter over 2014–2026, and correlate with SMHI's Uddevalla at only r 0.2 to 0.36. TICON-4's Uddevalla record, fitted from it, has Arkö's constants. The adapter lists the record as inactive, with no products and no links (`DROPPED` in [dropped.ts](dropped.ts)), and refuses to fetch it, so `backfill all` skips it. No link joins it to a group (`src/same-as.ts`), and the TICON inventory doesn't offer it as a GESLA copy. A release that drops or renames the file fails the list refresh until `DROPPED` is updated. SMHI's own series (`smhi/2541`) and CMEMS's record (`uddevalla-udd-swe-cmems`) carry Uddevalla.
- **Datum jumps flagged usable** (erratum `gesla-datum-jumps`). Some records change datum partway with every value still flagged correct and usable: Cape d'Or's 1965 values sit about 20 m above its 1969–79 ones, Mazatlán's float and radar gauges are 0.9 to 2.5 m off for months of 2021, Ōminato's 1952 values are 2 m above the rest, and Gorge at Craigflower's 1969 and 2011 values sit 1.3 m apart. `datumJumps` checks every coastal record on the raw levels: it takes daily means of the usable values on days they span at least 12 hours of, finds where the median of the next 30 such days differs from the previous 30's (as few as 5 near the record's ends) by more than 0.75 m, places the jump at the largest day-to-day step nearby (at least half of 0.75 m, since a jump partway through a day splits across two days), and flags every segment whose median is more than 0.75 m from the longest segment's as `suspect`. Rivers and lakes aren't checked, tidal ones included, since their floods move monthly medians that far: the check would flag 30% of the Connecticut at Hartford's values and 15% of the Columbia at Vancouver's. On 80 coastal records picked at random, it flagged two, both of which GESLA itself marks "Possible datum issues". It leaves the earthquake steps at Ayukawa and Onahama (Tōhoku, 2011), Kaikōura and Wellington (2016) alone.
- **Checked and fixed in 4.1:** JODC's Japan Coast Guard records (`jodc_jcg`) no longer lose the hours after local midnight on December 31 that tide-database found in 4.0: Monbetsu, Urakawa, Hakata and Syowa keep every year-end hour. RWS's records are on UTC.

**Clock check** (`scripts/clock-check.ts`, 2015, against TICON records of the same gauges from other contributors where TICON has them): with the corrections, Cuxhaven (against UHSLC's and BfG's records), Helgoland (CMEMS), Hoek van Holland and Vlissingen (RWS's 4.1 records against CMEMS), Hong Kong 329 and 329b (against tide-database's re-fit) and Seattle fit best at 0 minutes, and K1 within 16 minutes. Travemünde and Stockholm's Baltic tides are too small to say (r 0.24 and 0.25).

## Backfill

The Monthly workflow refreshes GESLA's list on the 2nd with every other source's; dispatch Monthly with `sources: gesla` once after merging to start it. Then backfill through the Ingest workflow, the ended years first, then 2026 on its own, since backfill fetches a year still under way on every run and `--skip-written` never skips it:

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill gesla all 1800 2026 --skip-written
```

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill gesla all 2026 2027
```

Years written under the earlier rule for tidal rivers, two days before a flatline is suspect, keep those flags until a requalify of the records in `TIDAL_RIVERS`, run once the backfill above has written them:

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts requalify gesla all 1800 2027 --shard 1/8
```

`all` reads every record's files and writes those whose flags change: the tidal rivers', and any other year that backfill judged before the year after it was written. A coastal record whose copy in the lake misses an end of its record fails, since its datum-jump check refuses it, and is left as it was, and the run then exits non-zero.

Years outside each record's span ask for nothing. Each record is read once per run and kept while backfill asks for its years (`KEEP`, the eight used last), the largest, Fremantle Fishing Boat Harbour, being 197 MB of text, which peaks at about 1.3 GB while it's parsed and keeps about 100 MB. A run cut short resumes with `--skip-written`.

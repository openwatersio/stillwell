# South Florida Water Management District (`sfwmd`)

Stage at 43 tidal gauges in South Florida, back to 1974 at the oldest, from [DBHYDRO](https://www.sfwmd.gov/science-data/dbhydro), the District's hydrologic database. The adapter reads the JSON and CSV API behind [DBHYDRO Insights](https://insightsdata.sfwmd.gov/), `https://insightsdata.api.sfwmd.gov/v1/insights-data`, which needs no key. The [Insights user's guide](https://www.sfwmd.gov/sites/default/files/documents/dbhydroinsightsuserdocumentation.pdf) documents its report URLs, but not the API itself. The documented [Hydro Data Service](https://www.sfwmd.gov/sites/default/files/documents/hydrodataservicedocumentation.pdf) serves the same data with a client ID, which datarequests@sfwmd.gov issues on request; it caps a response at 100,000 rows.

- **License:** DBHYDRO states no license. The District is a Florida agency, and its records are public records under Chapter 119 of the Florida Statutes. Its [disclaimer](https://www.sfwmd.gov/disclaimer) gives the data as is, with no warranty and no liability, and asks users to get data "directly from the DISTRICT server and not through other sources which may have changed the data". Nothing in it restricts redistribution or commercial use, so `redistribution` and `commercial_use` are true and every station is in the public store, credited to the "South Florida Water Management District". Each CSV opens with a notice that provisional data may be revised, which `terms` carries.
- **Contact for errors:** DataRequests@sfwmd.gov or the [DBHYDRO contact form](https://www.sfwmd.gov/contact/dbhydro_contact_form), both named on the [DBHYDRO page](https://www.sfwmd.gov/science-data/dbhydro) for data questions.
- **Stations:** the 43 SFWMD records TICON keeps, `sfwmd/<DBHYDRO location>` (`sfwmd/S123_T`, and `sfwmd/GORDON_2_T` for `GORDON 2_T`). They are the tailwaters of the coastal structures on canals at tidewater (S-20F to S-44, G-54 to G-93 and others), G-58's headwater, estuary gauges on the Miami, Caloosahatchee, Cocohatchee, Gordon and Loxahatchee rivers, and three bay gauges in Barnes Sound and Florida Bay (MBTS, MDTS, TPTS), which are `coastal`; the rest are `estuary`. DBHYDRO has no tidal flag, so the list is kept in the adapter (`TIDAL`). In November 2026, 37 are active. The station list comes from DBHYDRO's series catalog, `POST /cont/ts`, which ignores any filter and returns all 35,000 series, about 40 MB of uncompressed JSON. Monthly reads it once. Latitude and longitude come packed as DDMMSS.sss, west positive.
- **Series:** a station has several stage series (dbkeys) of readings at an instant (`STG`, breakpoint `BK`, `INST`), all from the District's own gauges (`agency` `WMD`) and all ones DBHYDRO converts to NAVD88 (`datumAware` `Y`). Two matter. The derived series (`recorder` `ONEP`, "one parameter") is DBHYDRO's reviewed record, joined from the gauge's earlier recorders and usually reaching back furthest, but it ends some months back: in November 2026 it runs to May. The telemetry series (`TELE`) reaches to within minutes. A station's `series` is its latest-ending series other than the derived one (one with no end date counts as running), the earliest-starting of those that end together, and `history` is the derived one, with the dates the catalog gives for its start and end as `history_start` and `history_end`, each midnight EST at the start of that day. Levels between them come from `history`, and those before and after from `series`: G-56's telemetry starts in 1985, six years before its derived series. The store replaces each UTC month that has values, so the series switch only at the start of a month. The month `history_end` falls in comes from telemetry, unless telemetry starts later in that month, in which case the derived series runs to the first month start after telemetry begins. The month `history_start` falls in comes from telemetry where telemetry started earlier. A month therefore always comes from one series, and a series that sends nothing for a month leaves the store's copy of that month as it was. Where telemetry ended before the derived series did, the derived series runs to the end of the record. A derived series the catalog gives no end for is left out. Each station also keeps when its telemetry starts (`series_start`) and, once inactive, ends (`series_end`). A station with no derived series reads `series` alone. Earlier recorders not in the derived series are left out, such as S-197's chart recorder from 1970 to 1993. Each station keeps the first day either series has as `record_start`, and an inactive station the day after its last as `record_end`, so a backfill asks for nothing outside them. `stations.parquet` doesn't carry `series`, so a station read from there takes it from `source_url`, which names it.
- **Levels:** feet, as meters. Every request asks for NAVD88 (`datum=NAVD88`); DBHYDRO converts NGVD29 series on request. The catalog gives every stage series in `ft NAVD88`, and a station whose `series` or `history` is in any other unit fails the refresh. A reading that isn't a number fails the request. Readings are breakpoints: irregular, from about 30 seconds to an hour apart, as DBHYDRO stores them, so `interval_s` is null.
- **Requests:** `GET /cont/data?timeseriesIds=<dbkey>&reportType=timeseries&format=csv&startDate=<YYYYMMDD>&endDate=<YYYYMMDD>&datum=NAVD88`, both dates inclusive EST days, one request per UTC calendar year, so a backfill's year is one request. `format=csv` returns a zip holding one CSV, about 0.8 MB a year at 115,000 rows, in about 20 seconds; the same year as JSON is about 40 MB. A range with no readings comes back as an empty zip. DBHYDRO publishes no rate limit, so requests start a second apart.
- **Flags:** `QUALITY_CODE` is A (approved), `good`, or P (provisional), `preliminary`. The `CODE` column is undocumented here. M marks a missing reading and normally has no value; it's skipped either way. E marks an estimate, at best `interpolated`. V comes on ordinary approved readings in line with their neighbours, so it changes nothing. Any other code is `suspect`. Telemetry is approved within days to months, and the derived series still holds provisional values more than a year back, so `finalizes` is true.
- **Times:** Eastern Standard Time all year, with no daylight saving: on 8 March 2026 readings run through 02:00 to 03:00, and on 1 November 2026 01:00 to 02:00 comes once. `utc_offsets` is [-5, -5].
- **Datum:** NAVD88, with no other datums. `archive` isn't set: DBHYDRO converts each value to NAVD88 by the series' own offset, so a request returns the whole record in one datum.
- **Not USGS or NOAA:** every series read is the District's own (`agency` `WMD`). The catalog also lists USGS series, which the adapter doesn't read.
- **Clock check:** against NOAA's Virginia Key (8723214), January against July of 1995 and of 2010, S-123's tailwater lags Virginia Key by 45 to 55 minutes and S-155's by 25 to 40, with r 0.97 to 0.99, the same in winter and summer within 15 minutes. S-123 in January and July 1982 against Virginia Key's predicted tide lags 55 and 65 minutes. So the derived series is in EST all year back to 1982 at least, with no daylight-saving hour, as the telemetry is. Its January and July 1982 values are all quality A. S-21A's derived series in January and July 1975 lags 135 and 110 minutes, from fewer July readings, so no hour shift there either; earlier years aren't checked.

## Backfill

Monthly refreshes SFWMD's list on the 2nd. Dispatch Monthly with `sources: sfwmd` once the adapter is on `main`, since Ingest hourly and Compact daily skip SFWMD until its list is in the store. Then backfill through the Ingest workflow, active stations first and the six inactive ones (HALDEMAN_T, MBTS, MDTS, S79_T, TPTS, WCOCO_T in the November 2026 catalog) in a second run; check the inactive list in the store after the refresh:

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill sfwmd all 1974 2026 --skip-written
```

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill sfwmd HALDEMAN_T,MBTS,MDTS,S79_T,TPTS,WCOCO_T 1970 2026 --skip-written
```

That is about 1,400 station-years, at about 0.8 MB and 20 seconds a year, so about 1.1 GB and 8 hours, two Ingest runs with `--skip-written` resuming the second. The current year is fetched whole on every run; backfill it on its own with `backfill sfwmd all 2026 2027`. From then on, Ingest hourly fetches each active station's telemetry, and Monthly's refetch on the 9th reads again from each station's earliest provisional value, back to the refetch's three-year cutoff: about 37 stations × 3 years × 20 seconds, roughly 40 minutes.

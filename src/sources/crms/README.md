# Coastwide Reference Monitoring System (`crms`)

Hourly water levels at about 430 surface-water gauges on the Louisiana coast, from 2006, from Louisiana's Coastwide Reference Monitoring System (CRMS), which the Coastal Protection and Restoration Authority (CPRA) runs with USGS. The adapter reads CPRA's weekly export of the whole record, `CRMS_Continuous_Hydrographic.zip`, the "CRMS Data Only" Continuous Hydrographic file on the [Coastal Information Management System's Full Table Exports page](https://cims.coastal.la.gov/FullTableExports.aspx). There's no per-station API: a web form emails a CSV per station.

- **License:** the [CIMS disclaimer](https://cims.coastal.la.gov/About.aspx) says "CPRA-authored or produced data and information are in the public domain" and that the site's information "may be distributed or copied", asks for a credit, gives the data without warranty, and recommends getting CPRA data from a CPRA server. So `redistribution` and `commercial_use` are true. The attribution is CPRA's suggested citation, "Coastal Protection and Restoration Authority (CPRA) of Louisiana. YYYY. Coastwide Reference Monitoring System. Coastal Information Management System (CIMS) database. https://cims.coastal.la.gov. Accessed DD MONTH YYYY.", without the year and access date, which `terms` explains. The export's metadata (`HydroHourly.xml`) adds that the data are "intended for general informational purposes only" and not for navigation.
- **Access:** `cims.coastal.la.gov/robots.txt` disallows all automated access. CPRA's CIMS team, asked on 2026-11-08 whether a weekly download of the export was all right, pointed to the same page and file on 2026-11-10 ([#49](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/issues/49)). The store downloads it once a month, in Actions only; the weekly canary reads only its last 64 KB and the CSV's first 64 KB, whose header it checks with the adapter's parser.
- **Contact for errors:** cpra-cims@usgs.gov, the address the CIMS pages publish for questions.
- **Stations:** every hourly CRMS station in CIMS's [monitoring-stations layer](https://cimsgeo3.coastal.louisiana.gov/arcgis/rest/services/prot_rest/monitoring_stations/MapServer/0) (`DATA_TYPE` Hydrography, `PROJ_TYPE` CRMS) that is a surface-water gauge, `crms/CRMS<site>-H0<n>`, with the layer's position and status. CPRA numbers a gauge it moves at a site anew (`H01`, then `H02`), so a site can have an inactive series and an active one. In November 2026 the export holds 430 surface-water series with elevations, and the store lists 442: 383 active and 59 inactive, 13 with no values. Left out: marsh wells (`W01`), which measure water in the marsh soil; floating-marsh sensors (`M01`, `M02`); `M100` series, which have no elevations; and `H0X` series, which CPRA's comments say "are the same as" another site's gauge. `kind` comes from the site's vegetation type in CIMS's [CRMS sites layer](https://cimsgeo3.coastal.louisiana.gov/arcgis/rest/services/prot_rest/crms_points/MapServer/0): `lake` at the 53 series at swamp sites, whose water sits still, and `estuary` at the marsh sites, fresh to saline. A refresh reads the two layers, not the export. Some layer IDs end in a no-break space, which is trimmed.
- **The export:** one zip of about 1.3 GB holding one CSV of about 13 GB, about 70 million rows, sorted by station and time, rebuilt every Sunday morning. The server answers byte ranges, at about 150 to 550 KB/s a connection. The adapter reads the central directory from the last 64 KB (the zip is under 4 GB, so it ends in the classic end record, and only the CSV's entry carries ZIP64 sizes), downloads the CSV's deflated bytes as 16 MB ranges, eight at once, to the response cache (`STILLWELL_CACHE`, under `crms/<crc>-<size>-v<format>/`, the format of the split files and the rules that read them), reads the tail again to check the export wasn't rebuilt meanwhile, checks the bytes against the zip's CRC, and splits the rows by station into files of their own in one pass. Every later fetch in the run reads its station's file. A run therefore downloads the export once, on its first fetch; expect 10 minutes to an hour, and about 2.5 GB of disk. A failed load fails every station of the run without downloading again, except that an export rebuilt during the download, or one that doesn't match its CRC, is downloaded once more. A row whose number of fields isn't the header's, as a stray quote or line break would cause, is left out and counted in the log; the November 2026 export has none. An export with more than 100 such rows and more than 1% of all rows, or with no surface-water elevations at all, fails the run as a changed format, rather than leave every station empty. Old weeks' directories aren't removed, which matters only for a local cache.
- **Levels:** "Adjusted Water Elevation to Datum (ft)", NAVD88, corrected by CPRA for drift and fouling, in feet, as meters rounded to the millimeter. The raw and adjusted "Water Level" columns are measured from the sensor and aren't comparable between stations. A row without an adjusted elevation is dropped: CPRA deletes outliers and values from unsurveyed sites by leaving the column empty. In November 2026 the newest values are 3 to 4 months old.
- **Flags:** the export has no quality column. A value is `good` unless its row's `Comments` say the elevation isn't the water's: the water level 0.03 ft or less above the sensor ("Raw water level less than or equal to 0.03", about 770,000 rows, where the elevation is the sensor's own), "low water" as a note of its own, the sonde out of the water, a day's median kept in place of its readings, readings moved to the top of the hour, or a sonde whose desiccant cap kept it from venting, which a pressure gauge's level depends on. Those are `suspect`. A comment about another measurement, such as "Drastic SpCond drop likely due to low water level", leaves the elevation `good`. Comments that only record a correction, such as "WL to Datum and WL to Marsh Adjusted +0.31 based on field observation", leave the value `good`. CPRA re-adjusts recent months after field visits and sends no preliminary flag, so `finalizes` is true and the Monthly refetch reads every station's last three years again from each month's export, as for BODC, naming every station in the list, since `backfill … all` skips inactive ones and CIMS can retire a gauge before its last months reach the export. A month whose every elevation CPRA withdraws later stays in the store as it was, since a refetch replaces only the months it brings values for; a refetch that brings fewer values for a month replaces that month.
- **Times:** Central Standard Time all year (every row's `Time Zone` is `CST`; a row in any other zone fails the run), on the hour, though some stations' stamps fall 4 to 8 seconds past it and a few spans are 15- or 30-minute. Stamps are kept as CPRA gives them, moved to UTC (`utc_offsets` [-6, -6]). Clock check: CRMS0002 at Eden Isle, inside Lake Pontchartrain, lags NOAA's Shell Beach (8761305) on Lake Borgne by 75 minutes in K1 and 154 in O1 over four months of 2008, and Grand Isle (8761724) and Pilots Station East (8760922) by over 6 hours, as tide entering the store through the Rigolets must. GESLA's copies of 40 CRMS gauges are an hour earlier than these times, which the GESLA adapter moves an hour later (erratum `gesla-crms-hour-early`).
- **Datum:** NAVD88, with the geoid model on each row. Every series moved from GEOID99 to GEOID12B at 2013-10-01 00:00 CST, and one floating-marsh sensor (not listed) to GEOID12A. Across that hour levels fall by a median 0.91 ft (0.70 to 1.14 ft between the quartiles of stations) over a year either side, where other years move between −0.29 and +0.43 ft, so the switch moved each gauge's reference, by an amount CPRA doesn't publish. `archive` is `previous` and each station lists `GEOID12B` from 2013-10-01 as its `reference`, so the GEOID99 years are an epoch of their own, with `to_current` null until a conversion is known, and backfill fills both. A value against any other geoid for its time fails the fetch, so a later move, such as to GEOID18, stops ingest until the new geoid and its date are added to the adapter's check.
- **Links:** a site's series list each other in `same_as`, including one the layer no longer lists, since each later one is the site's gauge after CPRA moved it. `preferred` names the one still reporting where one is, and the earlier ones keep the years before the move. Two gauges running at once at one site would be linked the same way. In November 2026, 48 sites have more than one series. Only CRMS3667's `H01` and `H02` overlap, by seven months, 0.46 km apart. GESLA's CRMS records carry the site, link to the site's `H01` series (in November 2026, every one of those sites that CPRA lists has one), and through it join the site's later ones, which hold 373 of the 793 years of CPRA's values at the 47 GESLA records on those sites. They aren't today's series: compared hour by hour with the November 2026 export, 40 of GESLA's 340 CRMS records match it to the millimeter on nearly every hour an hour later, after removing each year's median offset. 270 match on fewer than 30% of hours at every shift from an hour earlier to two hours later, and 30 have no values in the years compared. At CRMS0002, one that matches, GESLA's values before October 2013 sit 0.107 m below the export's GEOID99 values. USGS relays 12 CRMS gauges as sites named for them (`CRMS0282-H01-RT`), which the USGS adapter links. TICON's 164 accepted CRMS records match the site's surface-water series still reporting, the latest of `H01` to `H03`, or the latest of them when none is.

## Backfill

Dispatch Monthly with `sources: crms` to start the list, then backfill through the Ingest workflow. Each run downloads the export once, and `backfill … all` skips inactive stations, about 50 of which hold years of record, so the history is one run naming every station in the list, active or not, from 2006, both epochs, dispatched with `timeout` at 360 minutes. The IDs come from the list:

```bash
duckdb -noheader -list -c "select string_agg(source_id, ',' order by source_id) from 'https://stillwell.openwaters.io/stations/crms.parquet'"
```

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill crms <those IDs> 2006 2026
```

Then the current year, after the next Sunday's export, so the store reads one export a week:

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill crms all 2026 2027
```

From then on, Monthly's refetch on the 9th reads every station's last three years from that month's export.

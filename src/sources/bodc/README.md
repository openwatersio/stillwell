# BODC (`bodc`)

Quality-controlled sea levels every 15 minutes from the UK National Tide Gauge Network, which the Environment Agency owns and BODC processes as part of the National Tidal and Sea Level Facility. They come from BODC's [ERDDAP](https://erddap.bodc.ac.uk/erddap/index.html): a dataset per gauge titled "Processed values of sea level from tide gauge station …", 42 gauges, from April 2022.

- **License:** each dataset's `license` attribute is the [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/). BODC confirmed in writing (ticket BODCREQ-11479, 9 November 2026, on openwatersio/stillwell#80) that the network's processed data is under the OGL, which allows republishing it under CC BY 4.0. So every station goes to the public store, tagged for commercial use. BODC's website has a stricter site-wide copyright page, but its processed-data page names no licence; the OGL in the datasets and BODC's reply are what the store goes by. The credit is BODC, as part of the National Tidal and Sea Level Facility, and the Environment Agency, the dataset's `creator_name`.
- **Contact for errors:** enquiries@bodc.ac.uk, BODC's published enquiries address. The datasets also name `tgi@noc.ac.uk` as publisher and the EA's enquiries address as creator.
- **Other routes:** BODC's [processed data page](https://www.bodc.ac.uk/data/hosted_data_systems/sea_level/uk_tide_gauge_network/processed/) has the whole record, from 1915 (15-minute from 1993, hourly before), as NTSLF text files, published about four weeks after each month ends, but only to a registered, logged-in BODC user. Its near-real-time files need a login too, and its last month's processed data is sold. The EA's real-time readings of the same gauges are open and already in the store as `ea`. GESLA 4.1's `-bodc` records are BODC's processed files to 2024 or 2025, so the older record is in the store as `gesla`.
- **Stations:** one `allDatasets` query lists every dataset whose ID has `_ukcff_`, such as `LER_ukcff_4109_001`. The ID starts with BODC's port code, which is the station's ID (`bodc/ler`); the number after it isn't documented, so each fetch looks the dataset up by code from the same query once a run. Names come from the title. Avonmouth, Felixstowe, Moray Firth and Port Ellen, which the network has had, have no dataset; GESLA's records of them name `bodc/avo` and so on, and the refresh reports those links as dropped. Every gauge is listed active: BODC's months arrive late, not because a gauge stopped. Jersey is `JE` and Port Erin `IM`; the rest `GB`.
- **Positions:** from the dataset's `minLatitude` and `minLongitude`. Workington's is at St Ives in Cornwall, 500 km from its gauge, though its levels match the EA's Workington readings exactly, and Immingham's and Newhaven's are -999. `POSITIONS` moves all three to the EA's position of the same gauge (erratum `bodc-erddap-positions`).
- **Levels:** meters, from the `ASLVBG02` channel. Each dataset has every sensor's channel (up to 12: two bubblers, pressure sensors, radar, a half-tide sensor and residuals), all labeled "unspecified datum" and none marked as the main one. On 1 June 2024, `ASLVBG02` matched GESLA's copy of BODC's processed files within 1.5 mm at 1,679 of 1,682 shared times, at all 24 gauges where both had values; no other channel matched at every gauge.
- **Flags:** each value's qualifier, quoted as `''` or `'M'`. Blank is `good`, `M` (improbable, BODC's QC) `suspect`, `T` (interpolated) `interpolated`, and `N` (null) and missing values are dropped. An `M` value within a millimeter of 0 is `bad`: BODC writes 0 or −0.001 m every quarter hour where a gauge has no reading. That is Bournemouth's whole record, Fishguard's from 2023 and Portbury's but for 6 to 11 March 2024, whose levels are `M` too. Elsewhere a few `M` values that close to 0 fall among low waters, as at Aberdeen in March 2025, and are marked `bad` with the rest. Both `flag` and `source_flag` are `bad`, since that's how the adapter reads BODC's qualifier there. They stay in the store rather than being dropped, so a refetch changes the flag of values already stored. The monthly refetch reaches back two years, so earlier years stay `suspect` until a backfill with `--refetch` reads them again. Any other letter is `suspect`; none occurred. BODC marks whole months improbable at times, such as every value at Lerwick from November 2025 to February 2026. GESLA's copy carries the same flags, as at Lerwick in March 2024.
- **Times:** UTC, but stamped a few milliseconds off the quarter hour (`07:59:59.998Z`), likely from storing them as fractional days. The adapter rounds each to the minute (erratum `bodc-erddap-times`) and asks each year with half a minute either side, so a value stamped just before New Year comes with the year it rounds into.
- **Gaps:** about one value in 32 is missing from ERDDAP that GESLA's copy has (at Lowestoft on 1 June 2024, 07:30, 15:15 and 23:00), so a 31-day month has 2,880 values rather than 2,976. Whole months are missing too: at Lerwick, May to December 2022, October 2023 to February 2024, May and July 2024, and April to September 2025. And most gauges' records end at one of three dates, December 2024, February 2026 or September 2026 (Mumbles in early December 2024, Workington in November 2025), though the processed page has the network to August 2026. The store keeps the gaps (erratum `bodc-erddap-gaps`).
- **Datum:** Admiralty Chart Datum, as `CD`, the datum of BODC's processed files. `datums.AOD` is chart datum's height relative to ordnance datum from [NTSLF's table](https://ntslf.org/tides/datum): Ordnance Datum Newlyn in Great Britain, and the local ordnance datum at Lerwick, Stornoway, St Mary's, Port Erin and St Helier, and Belfast's in Northern Ireland, as the EA's mAOD readings use. Where they overlap in unflagged months, the EA's readings less BODC's equal the table to the centimeter at 15 gauges (Aberdeen −2.25, Devonport −3.22, Workington −4.20, Jersey −5.88). The EA's differ by 2 to 6 cm at Kinlochbervie (−2.44 against −2.50), Lerwick (−1.24 against −1.22) and Cromer (−2.77 to −2.81 against −2.75). Harwich and Portbury aren't in the table, so they have no offset; the EA's Harwich readings are 2.05 m below BODC's.
- **Preferred series:** a BODC station joins its EA gauge, GESLA's and CMEMS's copies and, at Lerwick, Newlyn and Stornoway, UHSLC's. By `rank` in [`src/same-as.ts`](../../same-as.ts), it is the group's `preferred` series while its last value is within a year, since its record is longer than the EA's; once BODC's last value is more than a year old, as at Newlyn and the other gauges whose record ends in December 2024, the EA's live readings are. So `preferred` can name a series months behind the EA's. `rank` counts a record's length by its share of values not flagged suspect or bad, so months BODC flags improbable don't lengthen it. In November 2026 BODC flagged improbable nearly every value in the store at six gauges, placeholders or not: Bangor, Barmouth, Bournemouth, Fishguard, Mumbles and Portbury. Portbury, which no rule links, joins the EA's Avonmouth Portbury by `LINKS`, and the EA's series is preferred.
- **TICON:** TICON's `bodc` records and its UK CMEMS ones match an EA gauge by position, and TICON's NOC records of gauges outside the UK network stay `restricted`, since BODC's open data is the network's alone.
- **Clock check:** at those 15 gauges, BODC's values and the EA's readings in November 2025 or May 2026 fall on the same quarter hours a constant apart, with r of 1.0000, so neither clock is shifted.
- **Limits:** ERDDAP publishes none. Requests start at least a second apart, a calendar year per request; a year of one gauge is about 35,000 rows. A query with no matching rows answers 404 with "Your query produced no matching results" in the body, read as no values; any other 404 fails. Nothing is cached, since BODC adds months to a year, and may fill its gaps, late.

## Backfill

The Monthly workflow refreshes the list on the 2nd with every other source's. Compact daily skips the source until its list is in the store. A full backfill goes through the Ingest workflow, the finished years first, then the current year on its own, since end years are exclusive and a year still under way is fetched whole on every run:

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill bodc all 2022 2026
```

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill bodc all 2026 2027
```

That's 42 gauges at up to five years each (Hinkley Point has no values in the store), about 200 requests, a few minutes. BODC isn't hourly, so nothing else brings its new months: Monthly's refetch on the 9th reads every station's last three years again with `--refetch` (`backfill bodc all <two years ago> <next year> --refetch`), finished years included, so months that arrive up to two years late, filled gaps and changed flags all reach the store. `finalizes` is what puts BODC in the refetch. That's about 126 requests a month.

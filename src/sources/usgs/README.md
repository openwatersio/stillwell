# USGS (`usgs`)

Observed water levels at USGS tidal, estuary and river gages, from the [Water Data OGC API](https://api.waterdata.usgs.gov/ogcapi/v1) (`continuous`, `time-series-metadata` and `monitoring-locations`). The legacy waterservices, which retire on 2027-02-22, aren't used.

- **License:** U.S. Government work, public domain. Attribution "U.S. Geological Survey" is carried.
- **Series:** each station is one instantaneous series (statistic 00011, or none at the few series that carry no code, such as the Housatonic at Shelton, 01208760) in feet. Where a site has gage height (00065), that's the series, measured from the gage datum. Where it doesn't, the station takes the first of these it has: tidal elevation, NOS-averaged (72279), estuary or ocean elevation (62620) or stream level elevation (63160), all in NAVD88, then the NGVD29 elevations (62619, 63158). Of a site's series of that parameter, the primary one comes first, then the gage itself over a headwater or tailwater sublocation (Olmsted on the Ohio has both), then one reporting now over one that isn't, then one that reported in the last year over one that ended earlier (so an outage of weeks doesn't switch a site with one series), then the earliest start. The committed list keeps the series' `time_series_id` and `parameter_code`, and levels are fetched by `time_series_id`, so a sublocation's values never mix in. A refresh that lists a station against another `time_series_id`, or with its gage datum at another altitude in the same vertical datum, marks it inactive for a person to resolve, as a datum change would: the stored values and new ones aren't measured from the same zero.
- **Stations:** about 13,700 USGS sites have one of these series. The list keeps the ones crowd-depth and TICON need:
  - `coastal`: ocean and coastal sites (OC, OC-CO).
  - `estuary`: estuaries and tidal streams (ES, ST-TS); sites with a tidal-only series (72279, 62620, 62619), whichever series the station uses; sites USGS computes daily tidal highs and lows for (statistic 00021); streams whose gage datum is below sea level, which the tide can reach, except in the closed Salton Sea basin (HUC 1810); streams on Louisiana's coast (the Lower Mississippi-Lake Maurepas subregion, HUC 0809), whose gages sit in tidal bayous, canals and sector gates; and 141 sites tide-database fits tides at from TICON that USGS types as plain streams, canals or lakes, listed in `tidal-sites.ts`. A gage datum at exactly 0 isn't taken as sea level: that's where USGS sets the datum to NAVD88 itself, which it does inland too (the Black River in Arkansas).
  - `river`: streams in the Ohio (05), Tennessee (06), Upper (07) and Lower (08) Mississippi HUC regions. The Missouri (10) and Arkansas-White-Red (11) regions aren't in yet.
  - Sites run by other agencies but listed by USGS (a few in Virginia, Texas and on the Rio Grande) aren't included. `country_code` is US, or the territory's own code for Puerto Rico, the Virgin Islands, Guam, American Samoa and the Northern Mariana Islands.
  - A station whose series reported within the last 7 days is `live`. `interval_s` is null, since sites report every 5, 6, 15 or 60 minutes and the metadata doesn't say which.
- **Datum:** gage height is in `GAGE`, the gage's own datum. `datums` gives the gage datum's altitude from `monitoring-locations` in its vertical datum (NAVD88 at most sites, NGVD29, PRVD02, LMSL or IGLD at others), so `level_m + datums.NAVD88` is the level above NAVD88. At Abercorn Creek (02198820) the gage datum is NAVD88 itself, so `datums.NAVD88` is 0. A site with no altitude has no offsets. Elevation series are in NAVD88 or NGVD29 directly, with no offsets.
- **Units:** feet times 0.3048 (the international foot; the survey foot differs by 2 parts per million, a quarter of a millimeter at 400 feet), rounded to 0.1 mm. Altitudes become offsets rounded to the millimeter.
- **Levels:** `continuous`, a calendar year per request, since the API allows 1,100 days and a page holds 50,000 values, following `next` links where a year has more. `continuous` starts in October 2007 at most sites, earlier at some (October 2001 at St. Louis, 07010000) and later where the series began later (June 2014 at Abercorn Creek). Older history is only daily statistics (`daily`, from 1982 at St. Louis), which aren't fetched: a daily mean isn't a level, and at a tidal site it means little.
- **Flags:** Approved values are `good` and Provisional ones `preliminary`. Qualifiers ICE, EQUIP (equipment trouble), DRY, LESSTHAN and BLWMIN (below what the gage records) make a value `suspect`, and ESTIMATED makes it at best `interpolated`. Values qualified DIFFDATUM or DATUMCHANGE are measured from another datum than the series' current one, which means the gage datum moved during the series, so a fetch that holds one fails for a person to resolve rather than put two datums in one file. The rest (REVISED, SEASONAL, REGULATED, URBAN, TIDE, BACKWATER, DISCONTINUED, UNAVAIL) describe the site or the record, not the value; backwater changes discharge, not stage.
- **QC:** pooled rivers hold one stage to the hundredth of a foot for a day or more, so `river` stations, from any source, get 48 hours rather than 6 before a flatline is `suspect`. Tidal reaches are `estuary` and keep 6.
- **Rate limit:** keyless clients get 1,000 requests in any rolling hour per IP (`x-ratelimit-limit: 1000`, through api.data.gov), and every client behind that IP shares them: while this adapter was built, other traffic from the same address used up the hour twice. Past it the API answers 429 with a Retry-After of up to an hour, until the oldest request in the window ages out. Requests that go out start 3.7 seconds apart, retries included, which keeps one keyless run under the limit; cache hits don't wait. A 429 waits as long as its Retry-After asks (15 minutes without one), twice, and holds back every other request meanwhile; a Retry-After over a minute isn't retried at once, since a retry would only spend the budget. A station refresh is about 77 requests. A key in `USGS_API_KEY` (the Ingest workflow passes the `USGS_API_KEY` secret) gives a run its own budget, sent in the `x-api-key` header, never the URL; its limit hasn't been measured, so the pace stays the same.
- **Backfill:** a year per request per station (more for 5-minute series), so the 3,350 stations' records since 2007 are about 67,000 requests, 69 hours at one every 3.7 seconds. The Ingest workflow's 6-hour cap holds about 6,000, and backfill doesn't yet skip years already written (phase 3's resumable backfill), so the full record is a series of runs over station ranges.
- **Recent:** `recent` sweeps every station at once: `continuous` answers a time filter with no site filter, a parameter at a time, paging through every site's values (an hour of gage height across all sites is more than one 50,000-value page, so a day is about 40 pages), and keeps the values of the stations' own series, page by page. A station committed in another datum fails alone; a parameter the sweep finds no values for is an outage and fails its stations, and one with no values at all fails every station. `next` links are followed only on the API itself, since the key goes with them. Fetching each of the 2,600 live stations separately would take more than an hour's limit.
- **Freshness:** USGS approves values months to years after they're measured (Olmsted's 2024 gage height was still provisional in November 2026), so only a page whose values are all approved is cached, whatever its dates. An empty page isn't either, since values can arrive late and an outage can answer with none. Station metadata and `recent` sweeps are never cached. Nothing re-fetches a year once its values are approved yet; that's the monthly job's work (phase 4).

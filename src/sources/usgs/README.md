# USGS (`usgs`)

Observed water levels at USGS river, lake, estuary and coastal gages, from the [Water Data OGC API](https://api.waterdata.usgs.gov/ogcapi/v1) (`continuous`, `time-series-metadata` and `monitoring-locations`). The legacy waterservices, which retire on 2027-02-22, aren't used.

- **License:** U.S. Government work, public domain. Attribution "U.S. Geological Survey" is carried.
- **Series:** each station is one instantaneous series (statistic 00011, or none at the few series that carry no code, such as the Housatonic at Shelton, 01208760) in feet. A series that reported in the last year comes before one that stopped earlier, whatever its parameter, so a site whose gage height stopped years ago stays live on an elevation series. Then, where a site has gage height (00065), that's the series, measured from the gage datum. Where it doesn't, the station takes the first of these it has: tidal elevation, NOS-averaged (72279), estuary or ocean elevation (62620) or stream level elevation (63160), all in NAVD88, then the NGVD29 elevations (62619, 63158). Of a site's series of that parameter, the primary one comes first, then the gage itself over a headwater or tailwater sublocation (Olmsted on the Ohio has both), then one reporting now over one that isn't, then the one that ended latest (an outage of weeks or months doesn't count as ended), then the earliest start. The committed list keeps the series' `time_series_id` and `parameter_code`, and levels are fetched by `time_series_id`, so a sublocation's values never mix in. It also keeps when the series began and, once it stops reporting, when it ended, so backfill asks only for the years it covers. A refresh that lists a station against another `time_series_id`, or with its gage datum at another altitude in the same vertical datum, or given only against another vertical datum, marks it inactive for a person to resolve, as a datum change would: the stored values and new ones may not be measured from the same zero. A site that answers with no altitude keeps its offsets.
- **Stations:** every USGS-run site with one of these series, about 14,000 across the US and its territories, live or not. Which ones a consumer needs (crowd-depth's basins, tide-database's tide gauges) is the consumer's call, and decides only the order backfill runs in. `kind` comes from what USGS itself says about the site:
  - `coastal`: ocean and coastal sites (OC, OC-CO).
  - `estuary`: estuaries and tidal streams (ES, ST-TS), and sites where USGS measures a tidal-only parameter (72279, 62620, 62619) or computes daily tidal highs and lows (statistic 00021), whichever series the station uses.
  - `lake`: lakes, reservoirs and impoundments (LK), and wetlands (WE).
  - `river`: everything else: streams, canals, ditches, springs and facilities such as diversions. That includes streams the tide reaches that USGS doesn't mark as tidal, such as many in coastal Louisiana; telling them apart is left to whoever reads the levels, such as tide-database fitting tides.
  - Sites the `monitoring-locations` answer leaves out are asked for again, and any still missing fail the refresh, since a short answer would otherwise retire them.
  - Sites run by other agencies but listed by USGS (a few in Virginia, Texas and on the Rio Grande) aren't included. `country_code` is US, or the territory's own code for Puerto Rico, the Virgin Islands, Guam, American Samoa and the Northern Mariana Islands.
  - A station whose series reported within the last 7 days is `live`. `interval_s` is null, since sites report every 5, 6, 15 or 60 minutes and the metadata doesn't say which.
- **Datum:** gage height is in `GAGE`, the gage's own datum. `datums` gives the gage datum's altitude from `monitoring-locations` in its vertical datum (NAVD88 at most sites, NGVD29, PRVD02, LMSL or IGLD at others), so where the gage datum is given against NAVD88, `level_m + datums.NAVD88` is the level above NAVD88. At Abercorn Creek (02198820) the gage datum is NAVD88 itself, so `datums.NAVD88` is 0. A site with no altitude has no offsets. Elevation series are in NAVD88 or NGVD29 directly, with no offsets.
- **Units:** feet times 0.3048 (the international foot; the survey foot differs by 2 parts per million, a quarter of a millimeter at 400 feet), rounded to 0.1 mm. Altitudes become offsets rounded to the millimeter.
- **Levels:** `continuous`, a calendar year per request, since the API allows 1,100 days and a page holds 50,000 values, following `next` links where a year has more. `continuous` starts in October 2007 at most sites, earlier at some (October 2001 at St. Louis, 07010000) and later where the series began later (June 2014 at Abercorn Creek). Older history is only daily statistics (`daily`, from 1982 at St. Louis), which aren't fetched: a daily mean isn't a level, and at a tidal site it means little.
- **Flags:** Approved values are `good` and Provisional ones `preliminary`. Qualifiers ICE, EQUIP (equipment trouble), DRY, LESSTHAN and BLWMIN (below what the gage records) make a value `suspect`, and ESTIMATED makes it at best `interpolated`. Values qualified DIFFDATUM or DATUMCHANGE are measured from another datum than the series' current one, which means the gage datum moved during the series, so a fetch that holds one fails for a person to resolve rather than put two datums in one file. The rest (REVISED, SEASONAL, REGULATED, URBAN, TIDE, BACKWATER, DISCONTINUED, UNAVAIL) describe the site or the record, not the value; backwater changes discharge, not stage.
- **QC:** pooled rivers and lakes hold one level to the hundredth of a foot for a day or more, and sit nearly flat for months before rising meters in a flood, so `river` and `lake` stations, from any source, get 48 hours rather than 6 before a flatline is `suspect`, and no outlier rule (as USACE has for its pools). `estuary` and `coastal` stations keep the defaults. A tidal stream USGS types as a plain stream is `river`, so a stuck sensor there takes two days to flag rather than six hours; that's the cost of not guessing which streams the tide reaches.
- **Rate limit:** keyless clients get 1,000 requests in any rolling hour per IP (`x-ratelimit-limit: 1000`, through api.data.gov), and every client behind that IP shares them: while this adapter was built, other traffic from the same address used up the hour twice. Past it the API answers 429 with a Retry-After of up to an hour, until the oldest request in the window ages out. Requests that go out start 3.7 seconds apart, retries included, which keeps one keyless run under the limit; cache hits don't wait. A 429 waits as long as its Retry-After asks (15 minutes without one), twice, and holds back every other request meanwhile, including ones already waiting for their slot; a Retry-After over a minute isn't retried at once, since a retry would only spend the budget. A station refresh is about 77 requests. A key in `USGS_API_KEY` (the Ingest workflow passes the `USGS_API_KEY` secret) gives a run its own budget, sent in the `x-api-key` header, never the URL; its limit hasn't been measured, so the pace stays the same.
- **Backfill:** a year per request per station (more for 5-minute series), bounded by each series' begin and, for one that stopped, its end, so the 14,000 stations' records since 2007 are about 203,000 requests, 9 days at one every 3.7 seconds, and need the key. Every job that holds the key shares its budget, so matrix shards must split the pace between them. Nothing orders the runs yet; crowd-depth's basins and tide-database's TICON sites are the ones to run first. The Ingest workflow's 6-hour cap holds about 6,000, and backfill doesn't yet skip years already written (phase 3's resumable backfill), so the full record is a series of runs over station ranges.
- **Recent:** `recent` sweeps every station at once: `continuous` answers a time filter with no site filter, a parameter at a time, paging through every site's values (an hour of gage height across all sites is more than one 50,000-value page, so a day is about 40 pages), and keeps the values of the stations' own series, page by page. A station committed in another datum fails alone; a parameter the sweep finds no values for is an outage and fails its stations, and one with no values at all fails every station. `next` links are followed only on the API itself, since the key goes with them. Fetching each of the 11,000 live stations separately would take 11 hours' limit; the sweep costs the same however many stations there are. A 24-hour sweep of all 11,000 live stations is about 1.3 million values, 2.5 minutes and 1.1 GB of memory; the 7-day recent window holds about seven times that.
- **Freshness:** USGS approves values months to years after they're measured (Olmsted's 2024 gage height was still provisional in November 2026), so only a page whose values are all approved is cached, whatever its dates. An empty page isn't either, since values can arrive late and an outage can answer with none. Station metadata and `recent` sweeps are never cached. A cached approved page is kept for good, so a gap USGS fills in later or a value it revises after approval doesn't reach the lake until the cache is cleared; re-fetching approved years now and then is the monthly job's work (phase 4).

# USGS (`usgs`)

Observed water levels at USGS river, lake, estuary and coastal gages across the US and its territories, from the [Water Data OGC API](https://api.waterdata.usgs.gov/ogcapi/v1) (`continuous`, `time-series-metadata` and `monitoring-locations`).

- **Stations:** every USGS-run site with an instantaneous water-level series in feet that has a begin or an end date, live or not, or has a dated series of another kind. Sites USGS lists for other agencies, a few in Virginia, Texas and on the Rio Grande, aren't included. A station is live when its series reported in the last 7 days. `interval_s` is null in the list, since sites report every 5, 6, 15 or 60 minutes and the metadata doesn't say which; `stations.parquet` gives the step the store measures ([the `interval_s` column](https://openwatersio.github.io/stillwell/docs/reference/stations/#column-interval_s)).
- **Kind:** from USGS's site type. `coastal` is ocean and coastal sites (OC, OC-CO). `estuary` is estuaries and tidal streams (ES, ST-TS), and sites where USGS measures a tidal-only parameter (72279, 62620, 62619) or computes daily tidal highs and lows. `lake` is lakes, reservoirs, impoundments and wetlands (LK, WE). `river` is everything else. That includes streams the tide reaches that USGS doesn't mark as tidal, such as many in coastal Louisiana and the Santee near Jamestown. Their `tidal` is measured from their levels, so QC treats them as tidal.
- **Series:** each station is one series in feet. Gage height (00065) comes first. A site without it takes the first it has of tidal elevation (72279), estuary or ocean elevation (62620) and stream level elevation (63160), in NAVD88, then the NGVD29 elevations (62619, 63158). A series that reported in the last year comes before one that stopped earlier, whatever its parameter, so a site whose gage height stopped years ago stays live on an elevation series. Series that rank alike, such as a headwater and a tailwater series that began together, are told apart by their series ID, lowest first, so the choice doesn't change with the order USGS lists them in. Levels are read by series, so a headwater or tailwater sublocation's values never mix in.
- **Levels:** from `continuous`, which starts in October 2007 at most sites, earlier at some (October 2001 at St. Louis, 07010000). Older history is only daily statistics, which the store doesn't hold: a daily mean isn't a level, and at a tidal site it means little. Feet become meters at 0.3048, the international foot, rounded to 0.1 mm.
- **Datum:** gage height is in `GAGE`, the gage's own zero, and elevation series are in NAVD88 or NGVD29 with no offsets. USGS publishes no field for the gage datum. `monitoring-locations` gives the site's altitude in a vertical datum, with the method and accuracy it was found by. Where USGS surveyed it to under a foot, it's the gage datum's, and `datums` gives it to the millimeter, so `level_m + datums.NAVD88` is the level above NAVD88. Elsewhere the station has no offsets (errata `usgs-unsurveyed-altitudes` and `usgs-imprecise-altitudes`). Most Florida and Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta gages have no offsets. A station listed against another series, or with its gage datum at another altitude or only in another vertical datum, gets a new datum epoch from that refresh, since USGS gives no date for the change.
- **Flags:** Approved values are `good` and Provisional ones `preliminary`. ICE, EQUIP (equipment trouble), DRY, LESSTHAN and BLWMIN (below what the gage records) make a value `suspect`, and ESTIMATED makes it at best `interpolated`. Other qualifiers, such as REVISED, REGULATED, TIDE and BACKWATER, describe the site or the record and leave the flag alone. A value qualified DIFFDATUM or DATUMCHANGE is measured from an earlier gage datum, and a fetch that meets one fails, so none is stored.
- **Freshness:** USGS approves values months to years after they're measured.
- **Independent checks:** the [pair check](../../../docs/contributing/checks.md#pair-check) on all 1,039 pairs of a USGS site (525 stations with a pair that isn't a copy) and another agency's record of it that `stations/matches.parquet` finds within 3 cm RMS, from USACE (710), CDEC and DWR (211), CO-OPS (54), CRMS (50), SFWMD (12) and NCDEM (2), over the latest year both hold. 266 hold the same values twice (260 of them USACE's relays of USGS gauges), so they check no clock, and two pairs (one USACE, one CRMS) have too few shared readings in their latest datum epoch. Of the other 771, 712 fit best at zero lag or no better elsewhere. 59 fit best off zero by the rule: 34 USACE relays (see erratum `usace-relay-clock-offsets`), 18 pairs 10 minutes off in either direction at neighboring gauges (the Delta's DWR and CDEC gauges, and NOAA's Washington), 3 at CDEC's GSS that fit 50 to 60 minutes off, 2 at SFWMD's MDTS (30 and 40 minutes), and one each at CRMS's CRMS4690 and DWR's Liberty Island, two gauges at 9 to 12 cm RMS. 290 pairs share NAVD88 or NGVD29 with the other record, and 197 of them differ from the offset between the two by under 5 cm, 53 by 5 to 30 cm and 40 by more. The 40 are Barataria Waterway (292859090004000), listed at 0.0 ft NAVD88 and 1.38 to 1.40 m above three CRMS gauges (erratum `usgs-altitude-contradicts-crms`), and 1.10 m above a fourth in the year before CRMS's 2013 geoid change, and 36 pairs with USACE gauges whose offsets differ from USGS's by 0.3 to 549 m, 31 of them at RMS under 5 cm, which are the same gauge. Three of those, Abington, Williamsburg and Texarkana, differ by the feet-to-meters factor, so USACE's offset is in feet. 580 USGS sites are listed at exactly 0.0 ft NAVD88 or NGVD29, which Barataria shows can be wrong, and 12 of them have a pair to test it against.

## Known limits

- **Revisions:** the store reads each station again only from its earliest `preliminary` value. A gap USGS fills after approving a year, a value it revises after approval, and a Provisional value flagged `suspect` for ice don't reach the store.

## Running ingest

- **Station refresh:** about 77 requests. Sites the `monitoring-locations` answer leaves out are asked for again, and any still missing fail the refresh, since a short answer would retire them. A refresh also fails when too many sites lose their altitude method or accuracy (`checkAltitudes` in [`index.ts`](index.ts)), since it would clear nearly every offset.
- **Undated series:** a site whose every series has neither a begin nor an end date in `time-series-metadata` has held no values, so it isn't listed (a refresh fails instead when a live station loses its dates, or more than ten active stations lose theirs); a stored station for it is listed inactive, and no backfill asks for it. It's listed again at the first refresh that finds a series dated. A site with a dated series keeps the series `choose` picked, dated or not.
- **Datum decisions:** `GAGE_DATUMS` in [`index.ts`](index.ts) sets the offsets of sites whose altitude isn't the gage's zero, against the altitude USGS listed when it was decided. If USGS lists another, the decision is set aside and the site has no offsets. Where the store held the decision's offsets, the refresh opens a datum epoch at once, even when USGS only corrected the altitude to the decided value. A `rejected` decision in `overrides` undoes such a split, and a `since` dates one that USGS left undated, from the values. Each decision's note gives its evidence. A fetch that fails on DIFFDATUM or DATUMCHANGE is resolved by hand.
- **Requests:** `continuous` is read a calendar year per request, since the API allows 1,100 days, and a page holds 50,000 values, with `next` links followed where a year has more.
- **Rate limit:** keyless clients get 1,000 requests in any rolling hour per IP, through api.data.gov, shared with any other traffic from that address. Past it the API answers 429 with a Retry-After of up to an hour. Requests start 4.8 seconds apart (`pacing.requestMs`), about 750 an hour, which leaves a quarter of the limit for the poller's sweeps. Cache hits don't wait. A 429 holds back every request for as long as it waits, and a URL the API keeps refusing costs at most 12 requests. `USGS_API_KEY` goes in the `x-api-key` header. Actions and the poller share one key, whose limit is also 1,000 an hour (its `X-RateLimit-Limit`), counted per key across every api.data.gov API ([api.data.gov's rate limits](https://api.data.gov/docs/developer-manual/#web-service-rate-limits)), so keyless requests from another address don't use it. USGS raises a key's limit on request to wdfn@usgs.gov ([API keys](https://api.waterdata.usgs.gov/docs/ogcapi/keys/)). Each job paces itself as if alone, so jobs running at once on one key need `pacing.requestMs` raised in code or a key each.
- **Cache:** only a page whose values are all approved is cached, and it's kept for good, so a value USGS revises after approval, or a gap it fills, reaches the store only once the cache is cleared. Empty pages, station metadata and the poller's sweeps are never cached. `--refetch` reads approved years again: about 200,000 requests and nine days, which no schedule runs.

### Backfill

From nothing, through the Ingest workflow, with the key. The record since 2007 is about 203,000 requests, 11 days at the pace. A run holds about 4,500 in its 6 hours, so the past years take a series of runs, each resuming where the last stopped:

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill usgs all 2007 2026 --skip-written
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill usgs all 2026 2027 --shard 1/4
```

`--skip-written` is needed at this scale, since otherwise every run fetches each station's first, Provisional and latest years again before reaching new ones. It also skips for 30 days a year with no file that came back empty (`skip_empty`), such as a gap in a series, which would otherwise be asked for on every run. Nothing shows the API never answers an outage with an empty collection, so publish still counts such a year inside a station's record in `missing_years`. The current year is about 16 hours across the live stations, so it runs in four shards of about 4 hours, `--shard 2/4` to `4/4` following one after another, each dispatched with `timeout` at 360. Leave a gap over 00:40 UTC between runs for compaction, as [Running ingest](../../../docs/contributing/ingest.md) describes. Once the year ends, run it again without `--skip-written`, in shards, to fill in the last weeks of stations that stopped during it.

### Scheduled runs

The poller sweeps every station at once: `continuous` answers a time filter with no site filter, a parameter at a time, and the sweep keeps the stations' own series page by page. A day is about 40 pages, 1.3 million values and 2.5 minutes, however many stations there are. A station listed in another datum fails alone, a parameter with no values fails its stations, and no values at all fails every station. Compact daily rewrites the live stations' year files in about 5 minutes. A bounds rebuild reads about 28,000 files.

Monthly refreshes the list on the 2nd. Its refetch on the 9th runs `schedule.refetch`, which `node scripts/schedule.ts refetch usgs` prints: a twelfth of the stations each month, by `--shard`, from their earliest preliminary value in the last three years to the start of this year, so each comes round once a year. A refetch that stops partway needs a rerun within the month, before the shard moves on.

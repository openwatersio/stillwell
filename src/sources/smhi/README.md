# Swedish Meteorological and Hydrological Institute (`smhi`)

Hourly sea level in RH2000 at about 70 Swedish gauges, back to 1886, from SMHI's [Open Data Oceanographic Observations API](https://opendata-download-ocobs.smhi.se/api) (parameter 6, `Havsvattenstånd`). [SMHI's notes on sea level](https://www.smhi.se/data/oceanografi/havsvattenstand) describe the data.

- **License:** SMHI's open data is under [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (Sweden)](https://www.smhi.se/data/om-smhis-data/villkor-for-anvandning), which allows copying, redistribution and changes, commercial use included. Users must name SMHI as the source and say whether they changed the data. So `redistribution` and `commercial_use` are true, and every station goes to the public lake with the attribution "SMHI". The same page asks users to load SMHI's services only as needed, not to download the same data again and again, and to use SMHI's latest update. About half the gauges belong to others (the Swedish Maritime Administration, Port of Gothenburg, Ports of Stockholm, Chalmers), but SMHI publishes them under the same terms, and the station list names each owner.
- **Contact for errors:** dataleveranser@smhi.se, named for questions on the [oceanographic observations API](https://opendata-download-ocobs.smhi.se/api).
- **Stations:** every station SMHI lists for parameter 6, active or closed, `smhi/<SMHI station id>` (`smhi/2545` for Arkö), the same ids TICON's SMHI records carry (`arko-2545-swe-smhi`). In November 2026 that's 71 stations, 60 active and 11 closed. Closed stations are `inactive` and keep their archive. The Göta älv gauges upriver in Gothenburg (Götaälvbron, Tingstadstunneln, Lärjeholm, Agnesberg, Hisingsbron) are `estuary`; the rest are `coastal`. Every station is hourly and in RH2000, and the active ones are live. Each keeps its first hour as `record_start`, and a closed one its last as `record_end`, so a backfill asks for nothing outside them.
- **Parameters:** SMHI publishes sea level four ways: hourly and minute values in RH2000 (parameters 6 and 13), and hourly and minute values relative to each year's computed mean level ("RW", 12 and 14). The RW series move their zero every year, so they don't make one consistent record. The minute series make archives many times larger, for no gain in a harmonic fit. Parameter 6 is one value an hour, measured at the hour, over the whole record, in one fixed datum.
- **Levels:** centimeters to one decimal (whole centimeters at most gauges until 2004), as meters. Each station's record comes from two periods: `corrected-archive`, one CSV with every value from the first to midnight UTC today, and `latest-day`, the values since the previous UTC midnight. The archive comes only as CSV, and only the CSV names the datum, so both are read as CSV. A range that starts at or after the previous UTC midnight, where `latest-day` begins, reads `latest-day` alone, which covers Ingest hourly's 12 hours and `recent`'s default 24; any other reads the archive and adds `latest-day`'s values after its last hour. The archive is read once per process and kept in memory for the eight stations asked for most recently, so a backfill, which asks for a station a year at a time, fetches it once. There is no `latest-months` period for this parameter.
- **Flags:** SMHI's codes are G (checked and approved), Y (suspect or aggregated values; roughly checked archive data and unchecked real-time data from the last two hours) and O (unchecked). G is `good`, and O and Y are `preliminary`, since Y covers each station's unchecked last two hours as well as roughly checked archive data; QC marks a value that looks wrong. SMHI documents no others, so any other is `suspect`. A series that moves in whole centimeters, as SMHI's did until most gauges switched to millimeters in the middle of 2004, gets 48 hours rather than 6 before a flatline is `suspect`: the Baltic's tide is a few centimeters, and on calm days those gauges hold one value for up to a day and a half (Landsort 34 hours in 1890 and 32 in 1960, Björn and Draghällan 26). Values in millimeters, which a calm day doesn't hold, keep the 6 hours. QC judges each held value by the changes in the three days either side of it, so a year that switches gets each limit in its own months. SMHI corrects its data monthly, and many stations' values are G within the hour, so any value from this calendar month or the last is at most `preliminary`, which lets Monthly's refetch pick up the corrections. Much of the oldest data is O and stays `preliminary`: Kungsholmsfort's hourly values from 1886 to 1919, for example, are all O.
- **Times:** UTC (`Datum Tid (UTC)`), on the hour.
- **Clock check:** against UHSLC's records of Smögen (2020) and Göteborg-Torshamnen (2010) and CMEMS's of Kungsvik (2020), every series fits best within 10 minutes of zero. The tide on Sweden's west coast is about 10 cm and weather moves the level more (r 0.38 to 0.49), so K1's phase, with an amplitude of a centimeter or two, is noise there: it puts Smögen at 205 minutes and Kungsvik at −710. A 12-hour error would show in the weather too, which doesn't repeat with the tide: SMHI's Smögen against UHSLC's for 2020 correlates at 0.94 at zero lag, 0.90 an hour either side, and 0.75 at ±12 hours. The Baltic gauges' tides are smaller still, so the check says little there.
- **Datum:** RH2000, Sweden's national height system, named in each CSV's notes (`Höjdsystem: RH2000`). SMHI publishes the whole record in RH2000, its 19th-century years included, so the series show land uplift and sea-level rise directly; there are no older systems or station zeros in this parameter. A CSV that names another system is refused, and one that names none fails, except a `latest-day` of a few hours, whose rows end before the notes reach the height system. The station list doesn't name the system, so a station SMHI moves to another one fails each request until a person decides what to do; the refresh can't open an epoch for it. `datums` is empty. `archive` is `current`.
- **Limits:** SMHI publishes no rate limit, so requests start a second apart. Archives come gzipped: Arkö (2014 on) is 460 KB and 3 MB unzipped, in 1.5 seconds; Kungsholmsfort (1886 on) is 4.7 MB gzipped and about 30 MB unzipped. An unzipped download of Kungsholmsfort stopped after 8 MB and 34 seconds, so a range that runs past the archive's end is refused when the archive ends more than an hour before the previous UTC midnight, or, for a closed station, before the end its listing gives, since backfill would take the missing hours as gone and delete them from the lake. A closed station's last hours can have no values (Landsort's archive ends at 09:00 on its last day, though its note and the listing give 12:00), so an archive whose own note reaches the listed end, which a stale copy's can't, gets up to six hours' slack instead of one. The note sits in the file's header, so a download cut within those last six hours would also pass. The archive's own `Tidsperiod (t.o.m.)` note can't show an archive is current: a stale copy's note ends with its own rows. SMHI builds an active station's archive to today's UTC midnight. A range that ends inside the archive is read as it is, so a station whose gauge has been silent for more than a day still backfills its past years, and only its current year waits for the gauge to report again. A `latest-day` CSV is about 1.5 KB.
- **History:** hourly values from 1886 at the oldest gauges (Ystad, Landsort, Varberg, Kungsholmsfort), and from 2009 at most of the Maritime Administration's. About 2,700 station-years in all.
- **Freshness:** nothing is cached on disk. The station list, `latest-day` and the archive are fetched fresh, since SMHI adds to the archive every night and corrects it every month; the archive is kept in memory for the process instead. Values reach `latest-day` in batches: on 7 November 2026 the 00:00 UTC value was there at 01:36, but the 01:00 value still wasn't at 02:02, when the minute series also ended at 00:58. Ingest hourly's 12-hour window covers delays like that.

## Backfill

Monthly refreshes SMHI's list on the 2nd. Dispatch Monthly with `sources: smhi` once as soon as the adapter is on `main`, since Ingest hourly and Compact daily fail for SMHI until its list is in the lake. Then backfill through the Ingest workflow, or locally against a local lake. `all` takes active stations only, so the 11 closed ones are named in a second run:

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill smhi all 1886 2026 --skip-written
```

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill smhi 2062,2067,2073,2076,2093,2098,2104,2109,2321,33092,35154 1886 2026 --skip-written
```

`docs/ticon-replacement.csv` names no SMHI station until `npm run ticon:inventory` runs against a lake with SMHI's list, so `--first` has nothing to order by yet; every SMHI station fits in the one run anyway. That's about 2,700 station-years but only 71 archive downloads, about 90 MB gzipped in all, since each station's archive is fetched once and sliced by year. It should fit in one 6-hour Ingest run; `--skip-written` resumes one that stops. It ends before the current year, which is fetched whole on every run, so backfill it on its own with `backfill smhi all 2026 2027`. From then on, Ingest hourly fetches each active station's `latest-day`, and Monthly's refetch on the 9th (`backfill smhi preliminary`) reads again each station from its earliest preliminary value, which brings in SMHI's monthly corrections. Each refetch downloads that station's whole archive.

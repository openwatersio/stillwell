# Rijkswaterstaat (`rws`)

Observed water levels, observed high and low waters, and RWS's astronomical tide and its high and low waters, for about 690 locations in the Netherlands and the Caribbean Netherlands, from the [Waterwebservices](https://rijkswaterstaatdata.nl/waterdata/) (DDAPI 2.0).

- **License:** CC0. Attribution "Rijkswaterstaat" is optional but carried.
- **Contact for errors:** the [contact page of Rijkswaterstaat's data portal](https://rijkswaterstaatdata.nl/contact/), which [its water data page](https://rijkswaterstaatdata.nl/waterdata/) points to. Rijkswaterstaat publishes no mailbox for data questions.
- **Stations:** every catalog location with a `WATHTE` series against NAP, or against MSL where that's all it has: 14 locations, mostly offshore platforms (A12, D15, J6, K13a and others), plus Ameland Westgat and Kralendijk on Bonaire. Products come from the catalog: `extremes` where the location has the `GETETM2` grouping, `predictions` and `predicted_extremes` where it has `GETETBRKD2`. Those groupings are against NAP even at the platforms, so a station whose levels are against MSL lists levels only, and every request asks for the station's datum so one file never mixes two. If RWS changes a station's datum, the refresh marks it inactive for a person to resolve rather than relabeling its files (see `src/stations.ts`). RWS lists Ameland Westgat twice, as `ameland.westgat` and `amelandwestgat`, a few meters apart; `TWINS` links checked pairs like that with `same_as`. Distance alone doesn't, since the two sides of a lock can be meters apart.
- **Levels:** 10-minute, integer centimeters (millimeters where converted, below), relative to the station's datum: NAP, or MSL at those 14. Values above 10,000 cm are RWS's missing-value sentinels and are dropped.
- **Extremes:** RWS's own selection, paired from the `GETETTPE` type channel and the `WATHTE` height channel on the same timestamps. Where a tide has a double low water (an agger, as at Hoek van Holland, where it's about a third of all tides), the observed extremes give both lows, `laagwater 1` and `laagwater 2`, and the rise between them as `topagger`. Both lows are kept as `low`, and the rise is left out, since it isn't a high water, so those tides have two lows. The astronomical extremes give one high and one low per tide. A type the adapter doesn't know fails the request rather than being dropped.
- **Flags:** `Gecontroleerd` (checked) is `good` and `Ongecontroleerd` (unchecked) is `preliminary`. Quality code `00` is a normal value. We haven't found what code `25` means, so it and any other code are `suspect` until we do. The astronomical tide is always `good`.
- **Times:** every timestamp carries its offset (`+01:00`), and is read as the instant it names. GESLA's copies of RWS records were stamped in Amsterdam local time an hour off, but this service isn't. Checked in November 2026: observed levels at Hoek van Holland, Vlissingen, Den Helder and Harlingen line up with TICON's predictions there (CMEMS and RWS records) at 0 minutes, within ±10, in January and July of years from 1990 to 2024. A historical request for the last day and a half returns the same values as `recent/rws.parquet` at the same times, and the earliest value arrives 15 minutes after its timestamp.
- **NAP before 2005:** the 2005 NAP revision gave every gauge's zero post a new height, and RWS serves values from before 2005 against the old one, so a series steps by up to 3 cm on 1 January 2005. PSMSL records each main gauge's offset (its two RLR factors, from Dillingh et al. 2010), and at all of them but Delfzijl the series this service returns match PSMSL's monthly means to the millimeter. `NAP_2005` puts values from before 2005 on today's NAP at eight of those gauges: Vlissingen, Roompot Buiten, Hoek van Holland, Maassluis, IJmuiden Buitenhaven, Terschelling West, Den Helder and Harlingen. Each converted series matches PSMSL's RLR series, moved onto today's NAP, within 6 mm in every year from 1990 to 2006, and within 1 mm in most. Delfzijl is left out: RWS has since corrected its archive for subsidence, and its series differs from PSMSL's by 5 to 40 mm before 2006. No other gauge's offset is public, so every other Dutch NAP station lists the reference `NAP 2005` from 2005-01-01, and the adapter declares `archive: "previous"`: its values from before 2005 come as RWS serves them, in the old NAP, and go to an epoch of their own. A station's first refresh with that reference closes epoch 1 at 2005 and opens epoch 2 from there ([docs/stations.md](../../../docs/stations.md)). Epoch 1 keeps the label `NAP` and has no `to_current`, so it stays out of the combined series until a decision in `overrides` gives its offset; read alone, it's a consistent old-NAP series. A gauge whose offset turns up later gets that decision rather than an entry in `NAP_2005`. Bonaire's NAP and the MSL stations have no revision. Delfzijl's epoch 1 is RWS's archive as corrected for subsidence, which isn't the old NAP either, so an offset for it would have to vary with time. The revision starts at UTC midnight on 1 January 2005, which lines the epochs up with the year files; PSMSL's factors change between monthly means, so the hour is a guess. The astronomical tide is served as RWS gives it, before 2005 too: its mean level steps at 2005 anyway (from 0.07 to 0.09 m at Hoek van Holland, from −0.01 to 0.00 m at Vlissingen), as RWS updates the mean level its predictions use, and converting it would make that step 4 to 5 cm.
- **Limits:** at most 160,000 values per request, so requests go a year at a time. Fair use, no uptime guarantee, and no published rate; throttling comes back as 429 or 503, which `src/http.ts` retries. An empty range comes back as an empty body. A year takes 20 seconds and 15 MB for levels, 35 seconds for the astronomical tide, and 2 to 3 seconds for either set of extremes.
- **History:** 10-minute levels back to 1990 at the tide gauges, and hourly before that, unchecked. Observed extremes for the latest year appear months later.

## Backfill to 1990

Backfill asks for every product a station lists, a year per request, so a tide gauge with all four costs up to a minute a year. It writes each station's years before 2005 into its old-NAP epoch and the rest into its current one. Run each backfill from the Ingest workflow with `timeout` at 360.

1. Refresh the station list from the Monthly workflow, for `rws` with `accept_epochs` checked. That splits about 670 stations' epochs at 2005, more than a refresh opens without it. Their values from 2005 on in epoch 1's files, this year's compacted weeks among them, then fall outside epoch 1's span; the recent window's values move to epoch 2 on their own.
2. Refill this year into the new epochs. It's fetched in full on every run, so it goes in one run of about an hour, and runs again once RWS publishes 2026's observed extremes, months after the year ends:

   ```bash
   node scripts/ingest.ts backfill rws all 2026 2027
   ```

3. Refetch the years already in the lake from before the adapter kept both lows of a double low water and converted NAP before 2005. Hoek van Holland's 2024 is one; a `--skip-written` run would otherwise keep it as it is. Any other RWS year file before 2026 in a station's current epoch needs the same. At a split station, epoch 1's files from 2005 on, this year's compacted weeks among them, stay in place outside its span, and epoch 2's files hold those years again. A listing of `obs/rws/`, `extremes/rws/`, `predictions/rws/` and `predicted-extremes/rws/` in the bucket shows which files there are. For Hoek van Holland:

   ```bash
   node scripts/ingest.ts backfill rws hoekvanholland 2024 2025 --refetch
   ```

4. The history, with the 161 stations matched to TICON records first. `--skip-written` picks up where the last run stopped. At the default 4 stations at once, that's about 3 runs for the TICON stations and about 6 for every station; a smoke test ran Hoek van Holland's four products at about 30 seconds a year. A year RWS has nothing for writes no file, so each run asks for it again, at about a second a request, which comes to about 25 minutes of each run once most river stations' years before they opened have been asked:

   ```bash
   node scripts/ingest.ts backfill rws all 1990 2026 --skip-written --first docs/ticon-replacement.csv
   ```

Each run holds the `lake-rws` group for up to 6 hours. A job that finds the group taken waits, and the next hourly run cancels it, so start each run between 01:00 and 18:30 UTC, so it ends before Compact daily at 00:40, and not on the 2nd, when Monthly refreshes the station list at 01:25.

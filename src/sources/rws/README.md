# Rijkswaterstaat (`rws`)

Observed water levels, observed high and low waters, and RWS's astronomical tide and its high and low waters, for about 660 locations in the Netherlands and the Caribbean Netherlands, from the [Waterwebservices](https://rijkswaterstaatdata.nl/waterdata/) (DDAPI 2.0).

- **Stations:** each location is `rws/<code>`, with RWS's location code. The list holds every catalog location with a measured (`meting`) `WATHTE` series against NAP, or against mean sea level (MSL) where that's all it measures, as at the offshore platforms. A forecast (`verwachting`) series doesn't count, so about 30 locations with only forecasts, such as Katwijk, Hoofdplaat and the Botlek gauges, aren't listed. Knock, Dukegat and Zoutkamp have only RWS's astronomical tide, so they're listed for their predictions and aren't live. At Zoutkamp, behind the Lauwersmeer dam since 1969, and at 19 locations whose catalog lists a measured series, the API returns no values in any of the five years probed. They stay listed.
- **Products:** from the catalog. About 180 stations have observed extremes (the `GETETM2` grouping), and about 100 have predictions and predicted extremes (`GETETBRKD2`). Those groupings are against NAP even at the platforms, so a station whose levels are against MSL has levels only.
- **Kind:** `coastal` at the locations whose catalog description says platform, and null elsewhere unless a decision sets it. The catalog names no other location's water.
- **Levels:** 10-minute, in whole centimeters, except values converted from the old NAP (below), which are rounded to the millimeter. RWS can answer a request with a series per instrument and per way of deriving values. The store keeps readings as measured and leaves out daily means. Where two series share a time, one that spans a day or more of the request wins over one that doesn't, then the finer one. The others fill the times it lacks.
- **Extremes:** RWS's own selection. Where a tide has a double low water (an agger, as at Hoek van Holland, where it's about a third of all tides), RWS gives both lows and the rise between them. Both lows are kept as `low`, and the rise is left out, since it isn't a high water, so those tides have two lows. The astronomical extremes give one high and one low per tide. Dukegat's, Knock's and Pogum's, from their first 2027 value, name the type with RWS's numeric codes in place of the names. The type table in [Deltares' hatyan](https://github.com/Deltares/hatyan/blob/main/hatyan/timeseries.py), the tide-analysis package, lists `1` `hoogwater`, `2` `laagwater`, `3` `laagwater 1`, `4` `topagger` and `5` `laagwater 2`, and the adapter reads `1` and `2`. Checked in November 2026 over all 101 stations with predictions for 2027, 87 of which answered: only `1` and `2` appear, at those three stations, in 4,231 values, and every `1` stands above and every `2` below both neighbors. Codes `3` to `5` have not appeared and fail the request until one does. Where RWS answers with several selections of extremes, the store keeps the one with the most values and each other that doesn't overlap those already kept, so another selection never gives a tide a second extreme.
- **Times:** each timestamp carries its offset (`+01:00`) and is read as the instant it names. At Hoek van Holland, Vlissingen, Den Helder and Harlingen, observed levels line up with TICON's predictions there, from CMEMS and RWS records, within 10 minutes, in years checked from 1990 to 2024.
- **Datum:** NAP, or MSL at the stations that measure only against it, mostly offshore platforms. `datums` is empty at every station. The platforms report against RWS's own MSL, which isn't assumed to equal any other source's MSL.
- **NAP before 2005:** the 2005 NAP revision gave every gauge's zero post a new height, and RWS serves values from before 2005 against the old one, so a series steps by up to 3 cm on 1 January 2005. At eight main gauges, Vlissingen, Roompot Buiten, Hoek van Holland, Maassluis, IJmuiden Buitenhaven, Terschelling West, Den Helder and Harlingen, the store converts earlier values to today's NAP with the offsets PSMSL records (Dillingh et al. 2010). Each converted series matches PSMSL's RLR series, moved onto today's NAP, within 6 mm in every year from 1990 to 2006. Every other Dutch NAP station has the reference `NAP 2005` from 2005-01-01, so its values from before 2005 are in an epoch of their own, labeled `NAP`, in the old NAP as RWS serves them ([when an epoch opens](https://openwatersio.github.io/stillwell/docs/guides/datums/#when-an-epoch-opens)). That epoch has no `to_current`, so it stays out of the combined series until a decision gives its offset. Bonaire's NAP and the MSL stations have no revision. The astronomical tide is kept as served, before 2005 too. Its mean level steps at 2005 anyway, as RWS updates the mean level its predictions use, and converting it would make that step 4 to 5 cm.
- **Flags:** `Gecontroleerd` (checked) and `Definitief` (final) are `good`, and `Ongecontroleerd` (unchecked) is `preliminary`. Quality code `00` is a normal value. What code `25` means isn't known, so it and any other code are `suspect`. The astronomical tide is `good`.
- **History:** RWS's levels reach back before 1990, unchecked, at coarser steps the further back they go. Hoek van Holland has 10-minute levels from 1987, hourly from 1975, 3-hourly before that and 30-minute in 1900. The river gauges' oldest records are daily readings from 1800 (Arnhem Nederrijn).
- **Freshness:** a value arrives about 15 minutes after its time, and levels are read every hour. Observed extremes for the latest year appear months after it ends.

## Known limits

- **Instrument handovers:** where one instrument fills another's times, the record can step by their difference, which nothing marks. Where two instruments' extremes overlap for days, only the fuller one's are kept, and the other's outside the overlap are lost. Where one instrument's extremes have a gap of weeks that another's would fill, the gap stays.
- **Ranking per request:** series are ranked within each request, a year at most, so a week's request can rank them differently from a year's.
- **Delfzijl:** its values from before 2005 aren't converted. RWS has corrected its archive for subsidence, so it differs from PSMSL's series by 5 to 40 mm before 2006 and isn't the old NAP either, and an offset for it would have to vary with time.
- **Revision hour:** the revision starts at UTC midnight on 1 January 2005, which lines the epochs up with the year files. The hour isn't known.

## Running ingest

- **Limits:** at most 160,000 values per request, so requests go a year at a time. RWS publishes no rate. Throttling comes back as 429 or 503, which [`src/http.ts`](../../http.ts) retries. An empty range comes back as an empty body. During an outage the whole host answers every path, the catalog and the root included, with the body `404 page not found`, so the adapter waits out such a 404 on a catalog request or a read of a range over 7 days, then stops the run as blocked, as `outage` in [`index.ts`](index.ts) sets. A shorter read, as in the hourly run, the realtime poller and the canary, fails at once and the next run reads the same hours. RWS publishes no status page, and its [documentation](https://github.com/Rijkswaterstaat/WaterWebservices) lists no maintenance calendar.
- **Workflows:** Ingest hourly reads the last 12 hours at the stations with levels, and Compact daily folds the last 6 days into the year files. The Monthly workflow re-reads each station from its earliest preliminary value on the 9th and refreshes the station list on the 2nd.

### Backfill

Backfill to 1990 asks for every product a station lists, a year per request, so a tide gauge with all four costs up to a minute a year. It writes each station's years before 2005 into its old-NAP epoch and the rest into its current one. Run each backfill from the Ingest workflow with `timeout` at 360.

1. This year is fetched in full on every run, so it goes in one run of about an hour, and runs again once RWS publishes the year's observed extremes, months after it ends:

   ```bash
   node scripts/ingest.ts backfill rws all 2026 2027
   ```

2. The history, with the 161 stations matched to TICON records first. `--skip-written` picks up where the last run stopped. At the default 4 stations at once, that's about 3 runs for the TICON stations and about 6 for every station. A year RWS has nothing for writes no file, so each run asks for it again, at about a second a request, which comes to about 25 minutes of each run once most river stations' years before they opened have been asked:

   ```bash
   node scripts/ingest.ts backfill rws all 1990 2026 --skip-written --first docs/ticon-inventory.csv
   ```

Each run holds the `store-rws` group for up to 6 hours. A job that finds the group taken waits, and the next hourly run cancels it, so start each run between 01:00 and 18:30 UTC, so it ends before Compact daily at 00:40, and not on the 2nd, when Monthly refreshes the station list at 01:25.

# NOAA CO-OPS (`coops`)

Observed water levels and verified high and low waters for the US National Water Level Observation Network and the historical stations before it, from the [metadata API](https://api.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/mdapi/prod/) and the [data API](https://api.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/api/prod/).

- **Stations:** the historical water-level list (`type=historicwl`), which holds nearly every current station, and the current network (`type=waterlevels`). Stations on the current network are `live`. A station off it is `inactive` when its `removed` date is on or after its `established` date, or it has no `established` date. The rest are `active`, among them a reinstalled station whose removal predates its establishment. CO-OPS still lists inactive stations and serves their history. Only live stations have `interval_s` (360) in the list, since a historical station may have only hourly heights. For the rest, `stations.parquet` gives the step the store measures ([the `interval_s` column](https://openwatersio.github.io/stillwell/docs/reference/stations/#column-interval_s)). `country_code` comes from CO-OPS's `state`, which holds a US state, a code that is its own country code (PR, VI, GU, AS, MP, FM, MH, PW, UM), or a country's name. Anything else is null. GPS Buoy, San Francisco Bay (9414796) is withheld: erratum `coops-gps-buoy-9414796`.
- **Kind:** Great Lakes stations (`greatlakes`, or `tideType` "Great Lakes", which alone marks Point Aux Frenes, 9075199) are `lake`, and other tidal stations `coastal`. About 40 stations CO-OPS marks neither tidal nor Great Lakes are null. They are sounds, canals and lagoons in North Carolina, Florida, Louisiana, Texas, Alaska and Puerto Rico, and most were removed long ago.
- **Data periods:** a backfill reads each station's data inventory once (`datainventory/response.jsp` on opendap.co-ops.nos.noaa.gov, the page behind a station's Data Inventory), which lists the periods it has verified and preliminary 6-minute levels, hourly heights and high and low waters. Before 2001 it asks only from the start of the station's first period of any of them to its removed date, so a station with none listed costs nothing there; that is 1,197 of the 2,931 inventories read on 2026-11-21, which list only monthly or daily means or other measurements. From 2001 it asks for every month of the station's dates, because CO-OPS serves preliminary 6-minute values for months the inventory lists no period for: of the 16,312 written station-years at the stations whose inventory lists parameters, 10 at 8 stations fall outside every 6-minute and hourly period, all in 2001 or later and nearly all preliminary. Before 1997 a month's hourly heights are asked with its 6-minute values. The inventory's times are GMT. A live station's months in the last 31 days are asked whatever the inventory says, and a request wholly inside them skips it. An inventory that isn't whole, names a water level this doesn't know, lists no parameters (31 stations do, and 2 of the 13 probed have data) or fails falls back to the station's dates, retrying later, and three failures in a row end its use for the run. A date that isn't a real time, or a period that ends before it starts, fails the backfill. A station the inventory has no record of, which it answers with a fault and HTTP 200, uses the station list's established and removed dates, which the source's list in the store keeps (`stations.parquet` doesn't carry them): months outside them aren't fetched, except at a reinstalled station or a live one, which is running whatever its removed date says. Those dates can reach back past what CO-OPS serves, as at Alameda (9414750), dated from 1939 with nothing served before 1985.
- **Datum:** every level is in station datum (STND), the gauge's own fixed zero. MLLW, like every tidal datum, is recomputed against the gauge for each National Tidal Datum Epoch, so a series in MLLW would change datum when the next epoch is adopted. `datums` gives each datum CO-OPS publishes from STND (MHHW, MHW, DTL, MTL, MSL, MLW, MLLW, NAVD88, NGVD29, LAT, HAT, and LWD on the Great Lakes), so `level_m + datums.MLLW` is the level above MLLW. About a fifth of stations have none. Each refresh fetches them again, so a new epoch's values arrive. The span CO-OPS reduced them over (`DatumAnalysisPeriod`, from the earliest date it gives to the latest) is kept as `datums_period` and published as each tidal datum's `datums_epoch`. A refresh that gets no datums keeps the offsets and the span the station had. On the Great Lakes STND is IGLD 1985, checked against the data API at Algonac, Buffalo, Calumet Harbor, Duluth and Oswego, so `datums.IGLD` is 0 at stations CO-OPS marks `greatlakes`. Point Aux Frenes, marked only by `tideType`, hasn't been checked and gets no `IGLD`.
- **Levels:** the 6-minute `water_level` product, whose record starts between 1994 and 1996, depending on the station. Before 1997, the verified `hourly_height` is read for every month. In an hour with an hourly height, preliminary 6-minute values are dropped, and the hourly heights fill every hour the 6-minute record lacks, since preliminary 6-minute values at Chuuk (1840000) and Anchorage (9455920) sit 0.66 to 1.9 m off the verified hours (erratum `coops-preliminary-offset-before-1997`). A verified 6-minute value keeps its hour, and an hour with no hourly height keeps its preliminary values, as all of Nome's (9468756) do, which are not confirmed against any verified record. A pre-1997 year stored whole counts as done, so it takes `--refetch` to read the hourly heights. From 1997 on, a month without 6-minute values is a gap and isn't filled with hourly heights. CO-OPS's predictions aren't fetched, since Slackwater computes its own.
- **Extremes:** the verified `high_low` product at tidal stations. Higher high and lower low waters are kept as `high` and `low`. A row with no level, a level outside the store's span (`SPAN_M`) or a time that isn't a real date is left out, as for levels; rows an older build stored from such a response stay until a refetch of their month returns a row.
- **Flags:** verified (`v`) values are `good`, and preliminary (`p`) values are `preliminary`, unverified and open to change until CO-OPS verifies them. Hourly heights and high and low waters are verified products. For 6-minute levels, the flat (F) and rate-of-change (R) flags make a value `suspect`, and so does the limit flag (L) while the value is preliminary (erratum `coops-stale-limit-flag`). The outlier flag (O) counts rejected one-second samples and is ignored. For hourly heights L is ignored, and for high and low waters it makes a value `suspect`. On both, the inferred flag (I) makes a value at least `interpolated`.
- **Freshness:** live stations are read hourly, the last 12 hours each time. CO-OPS verifies preliminary 6-minute values a month or two later, and the monthly refetch brings the verified values in.

## Known limits

- **Flagged preliminary values:** a preliminary value CO-OPS flags is stored with `suspect` as its source flag, so the monthly refetch, which starts from a station's earliest preliminary value, doesn't come back for it. Its verified value arrives only through a `--refetch`.
- **Empty months:** a month CO-OPS answers with "No data" leaves that month's stored values alone. The store replaces only a month a fetch returned values for.

## Running ingest

- **Requests:** every data request sends `application=openwaters`, as CO-OPS asks. 6-minute levels go a calendar month per request (the API allows 31 days), so a backfill asks for a year as 12 monthly requests. Hourly heights, and high and low waters, go a calendar year per request. CO-OPS refuses a request over 365 days for them, so a leap year goes in two halves split at July 1. Hourly heights are asked for once a year, before 1997, from the first month asked to the end of the year, and each month takes its own share. So a year before the 6-minute record costs 12 requests for 6-minute levels and one or two for hourly heights.
- **Station list:** `backfill coops all` takes only active stations, and `--with-inactive` adds the rest, as a history backfill needs. The refresh that first lists them inactive reports each as retired by the source, which fails that run once for review. Since the refresh's guard against a cut-short list counts only active stations, the adapter refuses a historical list under half the length of the stored one. An empty current list fails the refresh rather than taking every station off the live feed.
- **Station dates:** a station the list gave no dates for has them looked up in its details when the inventory has no record of it. A date that doesn't parse, in either, fails the backfill rather than skipping every year.
- **Rate limit:** both APIs block a client for a few minutes once it passes a rate limit, answering 403 ForbiddenException from their AWS gateway. The data API did after about 16 seconds of a backfill asking for four stations' months at once with no pause. Requests to each API start at least a second apart (`pacing` in [`index.ts`](index.ts), which also describes how a 403 is waited out). A metadata 403 waits two and a half minutes, then five, before failing its request. A station refresh makes one datums request per station and takes about 50 minutes. A station whose datums still fail keeps the ones it had. Five failures in a row, more than a tenth of stations, or two hours in all fail the refresh at once, and the store's list stays as it was. A data 403 holds every data request for two and a half minutes, the next for five, and a third hold in a row throws `Blocked` (from [the shared HTTP client](../../http.ts)). Every data request after it fails at once without going out, and backfill stops instead of going on through its stations. That takes 7.5 minutes and at least three requests. Three URLs refused one after another with nothing answered between them look the same as a block and stop the run too. The next run picks up where it stopped. The data inventory, on a third host, goes through the metadata API's queue, a second after the last request. The two APIs are paced separately, so a backfill looking up details can send up to two requests a second to the host. Runs that overlap, such as shards running at once, each pace themselves as if alone and have to split the rate between them.
- **Cache:** data responses whose range ends within the last 120 days, error answers (which CO-OPS sends with HTTP 200), and all station metadata are never cached, since CO-OPS replaces preliminary values as it verifies them and updates datums with each epoch.

### Backfill

At a second a request, a backfill of every station (`all --with-inactive`) from 1996 on is about 187,000 requests: a month of 6-minute levels for each month of a station's established and removed dates from 2001 and, before then, from its first inventory period (182,000 months, against 205,000 by dates alone), plus one or two `hourly_height` requests per year before 1997 and a `high_low` request per year at the non-lake stations, two in a leap year. That's about 52 hours, nine or more of the Ingest workflow's runs with `--skip-written` and `timeout` at 360 (its default is 180 minutes). Years a station returns nothing for write no file, and `--skip-written` records them and skips them for 30 days. Without it, each run asks again for every empty year inside a station's dates before reaching new stations, at about 15 seconds a year.

### Scheduled runs

The poller reads the last 12 hours at the live stations, and its sweep takes about 5 minutes. A block there fails the stations after it, which keep what they had, and fails the run. On the 9th the Monthly workflow refetches each station from its earliest preliminary value, from January three years back on, so verified values replace preliminary ones.

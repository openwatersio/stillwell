# Japan Meteorological Agency (`jma`)

Hourly tide levels at the Japan Meteorological Agency's 70 open tide gauges and 20 closed ones, back to December 1924, from JMA's text files: the [historical tide data](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/kaiyou/db/tide/sea_lev_var/index_hourly.php) (歴史的潮位資料) to March 1997, the [final tide observations](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/kaiyou/db/tide/genbo/index.php) (潮汐観測資料) from April 1997, and the [provisional values](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/kaiyou/db/tide/sokuho/index.php) (速報値) for the current and previous month. The site is in Japanese only.

- **License:** JMA's website content is under the [Public Data License 1.0](https://www.jma.go.jp/jma/en/copyright.html) (公共データ利用規約 第1.0版), which allows copying, changes and commercial use, and says content under it may be used under CC BY 4.0 instead. No tide page carries a rights notice of its own. Users must credit the source, 出典：気象庁ホームページ with the page's URL, and say when they've edited the data. So `redistribution` and `commercial_use` are true, and every station goes to the public store with the attribution "Japan Meteorological Agency".
- **Contact for errors:** JMA's [website feedback form](https://ds.data.jma.go.jp/homepage.php/index.php) (気象庁ホームページに関するご意見・ご感想), in Japanese, linked from its [copyright and links page](https://www.jma.go.jp/jma/kishou/info/coment.html). JMA publishes no mailbox.
- **Stations:** every station in JMA's [historical station list](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/kaiyou/db/tide/sea_lev_var/station_history.php), which gives each one's first month and, for a closed one, its last, plus any the [current station list](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/kaiyou/db/tide/genbo/station.php) adds. IDs are JMA's two-character codes, `jma/<code>` (`jma/KS` for Kushimoto), the same ones TICON's records name as their originator (`jma/KS` for `kushimoto-353a-jpn-uhslc_rq` and `kushimoto-ma26-jpn-jodc_jma`). Names are in English from the adapter's own table, since JMA lists them in Japanese; a code the table lacks keeps its Japanese name. Open stations are placed from the current list, to the minute. The list has no positions for closed stations, so theirs are in the adapter, from each one's history page (`sea_lev_var_his.php?stn=<code>`); positions before 2003 are in the Tokyo datum, a few hundred meters off. A station that closes later keeps the position the store's list has for it. Each keeps its first month as `record_start`, and a closed one its last as `record_end`, so a backfill asks for nothing outside them. Several closed stations moved to a new code nearby (外之浦 `TU` to 浜田 `HA`, 対馬 `O1` to 対馬比田勝 `N5`), and JMA keeps them as separate records, so they're separate stations here.
- **Files:** one text file per station and JST month: `sea_lev_var/<yyyy>/<yyyymm>/hry<yyyymm><code>.txt` for the historical set, `genbo/<yyyy>/<yyyymm>/hry<yyyymm><code>.txt` for the final set, and `sokuho/<yyyymm>/z_hry<yyyymm><code>.txt` for the provisional one. Each line is a day: 24 three-column values for 0:00 to 23:00, blank where missing, then the two-digit year, month and day and the station code in columns 73 to 80 ([format](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/kaiyou/db/tide/genbo/format.html)). The final set adds the day's high and low waters after column 80, which aren't read. A line for another station or month is refused, and so is a day outside the month or stamped twice, unless the lines on either side are stamped as days of the month, two days apart, and the line isn't a copy of another: then the line is the day between them, as with Saigo's 22 October 1980, which is stamped the 20th (erratum `jma-saigo-1980-day-stamp`). A month JMA has no file for (404) brings no values, and nor does a historical file whose month is blank. Since a JST month starts at 15:00 UTC on the UTC month's last day, a UTC month's last 9 hours come from the next JST month's file; when its own JST month brought nothing or wasn't read, as at the start of a station's record, those hours are dropped, or a merge would take them for a fetch of the whole month and replace what the store holds for it. The opposite case isn't covered: when a month's final file is out but the next month's is late past the refetch that reads it, the merge drops the month's last 9 hours, which were the next month's provisional values, and the next refetch starts after them; a backfill of that year brings them back. A month is about 2.5 to 4 KB.
- **Levels:** centimeters, as meters. Every value is JMA's smoothed level (平滑値), with seiches, waves and other oscillations shorter than about 3 hours filtered out, not an instantaneous reading. Historical and final values are `good`, and provisional ones `preliminary`.
- **Times:** JST (UTC+9), 「時刻はすべてJST（日本標準時）で表しています」, hours 0 to 23, converted to UTC. A UTC year reads 13 monthly files, the last for its first 9 hours.
- **Clock check:** against TICON's records from UHSLC's fast delivery, which UHSLC converts to UTC itself, and from JODC's relay of JMA, every series fits best at a lag of 0 in 2020 (Kushimoto r 0.975, Naha 0.992, Hakodate 0.939, Chichijima 0.946) and in 1990, from the historical set (Kushimoto 0.976, Naha 0.986). K1's lag is within 4 minutes everywhere, so there's no 12-hour error either.
- **Datum:** each station's observation datum, 観測基準面 (DL), which JMA keeps fixed through gauge moves and resets: at Tokyo, the benchmark's height above DL changed with each move in 1968 and 1982 while DL's height in T.P. moved by about a centimeter. The historical set is shifted to each station's current DL (「現在の観測基準面に合わせる」), so `archive` is `current`. JMA publishes DL's height in T.P. (or, on remote islands, in a GSI datum or local mean sea level) in the current list, and revises it every year and with each GSI survey (June 2026 for 測地成果2024), but `datums` is empty: the list doesn't say which stations' heights are in T.P., and the offsets would change every year.
- **Freshness:** provisional values appear around noon JST the next day, and the final set around the 20th of the next month, so the source isn't `hourly`. On 8 November 2026 at 00:34 JST, Chichijima's provisional file ran to the end of the 5th. The current and previous JST month are fetched fresh, final file first and the provisional one where it's missing. Nothing runs hourly, so Monthly's refetch on the 9th is what brings new values: for JMA it backfills every open station from the start of the month two months back, whose final file is out by then, to the end of this month, then runs `backfill jma preliminary` like every source that `finalizes`. The store is therefore up to about five weeks behind JMA. Older files are cached on disk, and JMA lists corrections to final files on the final set's index page; the Ingest workflow starts with an empty cache, so a backfill there with `--refetch` reads them again, and so does one locally after clearing `.cache/`.
- **Limits:** JMA publishes no rate limit and has no robots.txt, so requests start 300 ms apart.
- **History:** from December 1924 at the oldest gauges (Hanasaki, Ayukawa, Kushimoto, Tonoura, Sakai, Iwasaki, Fukahori). About 4,900 station-years in all.
- **Links:** UHSLC's documentation files give the originator's station number, and where the contributor is JMA or JODC and it is a two-character JMA code, UHSLC's station gets a `same_as` link to this one. JODC's relay of JMA's gauges (GESLA's `JODC_JMA` records, TICON's `jodc_jma`) isn't read, since JMA's own files carry the same gauges. The `jodc` source reads JODC's other agencies' gauges.
- **Canary:** the weekly canary has no recorded response shape for JMA, since its paths name the month and its text has no shape; it passes when both the provisional and final sets return values.
- **Not used:** JMA's 15-second values from the [tide observation map](https://www.jma.go.jp/bosai/tidelevel/), in T.P. rather than DL, kept for about 32 days, which cover 166 gauges including other agencies'; the original ledger values (`orgdata/`), which the historical set corrects; and the residuals (`dep`) and tide tables.

## Compared with UHSLC

UHSLC's research-quality set carries 17 of these stations, 13 contributed by JMA and 4 by JODC under JMA's codes, through 2023. JMA's own files add its 74 other stations, the final values since 2024 and the provisional ones to yesterday, and earlier years at some gauges: Chichijima from 1933 rather than 1975, Hachinohe from 1936 rather than 1980. Both are hourly.

## Backfill

Monthly refreshes JMA's list on the 2nd. A full backfill goes through the Ingest workflow, TICON's stations first. `all` takes active stations only, so the 20 closed ones are named in a second run:

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill jma all 1924 2026 --skip-written --first docs/ticon-replacement.csv
```

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill jma CS,D5,D9,DS,FU,H1,HC,HD,HF,IW,L4,ME,MS,O1,O7,R1,SS,T2,TE,TU 1924 2026 --skip-written
```

That's about 4,900 station-years, 59,000 monthly files and about 200 MB. At 300 ms a request it needs about 6 hours, so expect a second run, which `--skip-written` resumes. The current year is backfilled on its own, `backfill jma all 2026 2027`.

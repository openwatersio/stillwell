# Bureau of Meteorology, National Tidal Centre (`bom`)

Hourly sea levels at 29 gauges that the Bureau of Meteorology's National Tidal Centre runs: 16 of the Australian Baseline Sea Level Monitoring Array (ABSLMP) and 13 of the Pacific Sea Level and Geodetic Monitoring Project (SPSLCMP), back to 1996 where BOM's archive has the year, from the yearly archives on BOM's anonymous FTP server, `ftp://ftp.bom.gov.au/anon/home/ntc/pacific/uhslc/`.

- **License:** BOM's reply to the permission request in [#80](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/issues/80), 12 November 2026, reference CAS-151567-N3L2D4, from its Climate Data Services mailbox: "As long as the products adhere to the conditions of use as displayed in the Bureau of Meteorology webpage, it should be fine to use data." The conditions are the [tide-information conditions of use](https://www.bom.gov.au/oceanography/projects/ntc/Tidal_Information_Conditions_of_Use.pdf), which are written for tide predictions. They ask that a publication acknowledge copyright in the Commonwealth of Australia represented by the Bureau of Meteorology and carry the Bureau's disclaimer of warranty and liability, and, for a product made from the data, the "based on Bureau of Meteorology information that has subsequently been modified" disclaimer. Both are in `terms` in [`sources.json`](https://stillwell.openwaters.io/sources.json) and linked from every station's `attribution`: "Bureau of Meteorology, © Commonwealth of Australia (conditions of use and disclaimers: …)", then the project's name. The store reads the reply as allowing use under those conditions, commercial use included, as it does for UHSLC's copies of the same gauges, whose notice (`NOTICES.bom` in [`src/sources/uhslc/notices.ts`](../uhslc/notices.ts)) this source's credit, conditions link and terms come from, so the two can't differ: the stations are tagged `commercial_use` true. The reply doesn't mention commercial use, though, and a copy can't carry looser terms than the original, so if BOM answers otherwise both sources change together. BOM's [anonymous FTP](https://www.bom.gov.au/catalogue/anon-ftp.shtml) is the route its website's block page names for automated access, under the default terms of its [copyright notice](https://www.bom.gov.au/other/copyright.shtml), which the GESLA contributor notes in this repo record as personal or in-house use, not supplied to others or used commercially. A request to BOM, not yet sent, asks it to confirm that the FTP files fall under the reply's permission, and to allow the ABSLMP CSVs and station sheets on its website to be fetched, which scripted requests can't reach (HTTP 403 from its bot protection, which the adapter doesn't try to get around).
- **Contact for errors:** the [National Tidal Centre's page](https://www.bom.gov.au/oceanography/projects/ntc/ntc.shtml). BOM's site is closed to scripted requests, so the mailbox it names there isn't recorded here.
- **Stations:** `bom/<number>`, the number in the files' names, `h<number>.dat`. The numbers are BOM's older ones, not UHSLC's, for the gauges whose UHSLC series have a version letter: Cape Ferguson (995, `uhslc/343a`), Rosslyn Bay (996, `330a`), Port Kembla (997, `342a`), Burnie (994, `344a`), Hillarys (958, `132a`), Port Stanvac (956, `100a`) and Milner Bay (959, `145a`). The others carry UHSLC's number: Spring Bay (335), Portland (129), Thevenard (128), Esperance (176), Broome (166), Darwin (168), Cocos (171) and the 13 Pacific gauges, Pohnpei (001), Tarawa (002), Nauru (004), Majuro (005), Honiara (009), Rarotonga (023), Funafuti (025), Nuku'alofa (038), Port Vila (046), Lombrum (400), Apia (401), Lautoka (402) and Alofi on Niue (598). Stony Point (993) and Lorne (992) have no UHSLC series. Positions are those BOM's records of the gauges carry in GESLA (UHSLC's for Alofi), since the files' own position lines are to a minute. A gauge is active when the newest year's archive holds a value for it: Port Stanvac isn't, and its last values are from 2010.
- **Not listed:** the archives also carry gauges BOM relays for others: Fremantle, Brisbane Bar, Bundaberg, Fort Denison, Townsville and Booby Island (port operators and state agencies), Flying Fish Cove on Christmas Island, Suva (Fiji) and Jackson Bay (New Zealand). They belong to their own agencies and aren't listed here. The ABSLMP's Thursday Island gauge has no file in the archives.
- **Files:** one archive a year, `<year>.ar` (a Unix `ar` archive, 0.2 to 0.9 MB), with a member `h<number>.dat.Z` for each gauge that year. Members are `compress` files, decoded in the adapter (`uncompress`). BOM has archives for 1996 and for 2005 on; 1997 to 2004, and a year not yet begun, return a 550, which reads as no values, and a 550 for any other year is an error. Every member of 1996's is a gzip file under the `.Z` name (erratum `bom-gzip-named-z`). The current year's archive is rebuilt in place: `2026.ar` and the `h<number>.dat` files beside it were written on 22 October 2026 and end on 30 September, and `2025.ar` was last written on 16 February 2026. An archive holds every gauge, so a run asks for each year once, whatever the number of gauges. The adapter doesn't read the `h<number>.dat` files, which are the current year's members of `2026.ar` line for line.
- **Levels:** the file's format is UHSLC's hourly ASCII: a position line, then two lines a day, each the station number, an 8-character name, the year, month and day, the half of the day (1 for hours 0 to 11, 2 for 12 to 23) and 12 levels of 5 characters in millimeters, `9999` where missing. BOM's file for Burnie in 2005 has a damaged position line, which the adapter skips (erratum `bom-burnie-2005-position-line`). Every file's lines must run from January 1 a half-day each without a gap or a repeat, and every level must be a whole number or `9999`, or the file is refused. A file cut off at the end of a line reads as a shorter year. Broome's levels pass 10 m, which fills the five characters.
- **Flags:** the files have none. Values in the year under way are preliminary, and earlier years are good. The Monthly refetch reads every active gauge again from last year to this one, since BOM rebuilds a finished year's archive in the new year's first weeks (`2024.ar` in January 2026 and `2025.ar` in February). That a year's archive is final once it has been rebuilt is an assumption.
- **Times:** UTC (the files' lines say `TIMEZONE=GMT`). Against the store's UHSLC fast-delivery series of the same gauges for 2026, the values are the same at the same hour at Thevenard, Portland, Darwin, Cocos, Esperance, Spring Bay, Pohnpei, Apia and Honiara (mean difference under 0.1 mm, standard deviation 0.4 to 4.2 mm, no lag), and at Broome, with the offset below. UHSLC's fast delivery of these gauges carries BOM's values.
- **Datum:** the files don't name their zero. The levels are heights above each gauge's tide gauge zero, `TGZ`: each gauge's mean level is 0.1 to 0.26 m above the height of the Australian Height Datum that the list beside the files, `readme.txt` (2015), gives as `AHD=`, which is the height of AHD above the gauge zero, so a level less that value is in AHD, and `datums` holds `AHD` as its negative. It holds it for the 11 gauges whose list gives one value that fits their levels: Cape Ferguson 1.59, Rosslyn Bay 2.36, Port Kembla 0.872, Burnie 1.938, Spring Bay 1.153, Portland 0.507, Thevenard 0.993, Esperance 0.707, Hillarys 0.763, Lorne 1.423 and Stony Point 1.6. Broome's value, 4.462, is 1.1 m below its mean level where the others are within 0.26 m, so it's left out (erratum `bom-broome-ahd`); Darwin has two (4.105 as `PD` and 3.975 as `TGZ`) and Port Stanvac's carries a question mark (1.2641, or 1.282 "more likely"). Cocos, Milner Bay and the Pacific gauges have none. The list is old, and its values aren't BOM's station sheets, which are on the website's ABSLMP page and not reachable here. UHSLC's own series of the gauges are in `ZERO` of UHSLC's choosing. At Broome UHSLC's levels are exactly 2.000 m below BOM's file's; at the others they're equal. `links.parquet` measures the offset between linked series that share no datum, so a level converts between them where both have values.
- **Freshness:** BOM writes the files about monthly, weeks behind: 22 October for data to 30 September. Nothing runs hourly, and BOM's FTP carries nothing newer for these gauges; the real-time displays on BOM's website aren't served as files. The Monthly refetch reads last year's and this year's archives again each month.
- **Limits:** BOM states no rate limit for its FTP server. The adapter asks for one archive at a time, a second apart, from one passive connection each, which is about 30 requests for a whole backfill and 1 to 3 a month after. The runner's FTP access to BOM hasn't been tried from GitHub Actions' network.
- **Not used:** the ABSLMP CSVs (`https://www.bom.gov.au/ntc/IDO71001/IDO71001_<year>.csv`, station sheets with each gauge's AHD offset) and the Pacific project's files on the website, which are behind the bot protection; the `IDO7…SLD.txt` monthly statistics on the FTP; and `jasl/jasl_2026.zip`, which is UHSLC's JASL archive (whole records of hourly values from 1979 for 54 gauges, UHSLC's station numbers), a copy of UHSLC's data rather than BOM's.

## GESLA's BOM records

GESLA 4.1 has 172 records whose contributor is BOM. 28 are gauges this source lists, and each is named in its `same_as` with the UHSLC series of the same gauge, so they group under `preferred` by [`rank`](../../same-as.ts). The other 144 aren't in BOM's archives and stay GESLA-only:

- 7 Papua New Guinea gauges of the Pacific project's earlier years, which ended between 1994 and 2003: Alotau, Kavieng, Lae, Madang, Wewak, Port Moresby and Rabaul. Lombrum, which has run since 1994, is listed.
- Suva in Fiji and Jackson Bay in New Zealand, which belong to those countries' agencies.
- 8 gauges of Australia's Antarctic and external territories: Casey, Davis, Mawson, Macquarie Island, Christmas Island, Lord Howe Island and two records of Norfolk Island.
- 127 gauges on the Australian coast: [#240](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/issues/240) found that the National Tidal Centre lists about 40 port gauges it doesn't run, whose raw observations its page sends users to ask their owners for, and that state agencies such as Manly Hydraulics Laboratory and Maritime Safety Queensland publish others. Records with a state agency's own station code (Queensland's `h…a`, Western Australia's `dv…`, `ff…`, `pl…` and similar) are theirs. Their owners' own publications are where a series of them would come from.

## Backfill

Monthly refreshes the list on the 2nd; dispatch Monthly with `sources: bom` once the adapter is on `main`. Then backfill through the Ingest workflow, in two runs, since `all` takes active stations only and Port Stanvac isn't one:

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill bom all 1996 2027 --skip-written
```

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill bom 956 1996 2012 --skip-written
```

Each year's archive is read once, so a run makes about 30 requests, and writes about 500 station-years. `--skip-written` records the years BOM has no archive for (1997 to 2004) and skips them for 30 days. The Monthly refetch covers last year and this one, so a later rebuild of an older archive is read with `backfill bom all <year> 2027 --refetch`.

# Bureau of Meteorology, National Tidal Centre (`bom`)

Hourly sea levels at 31 gauges that the Bureau of Meteorology's National Tidal Centre runs: 16 of the Australian Baseline Sea Level Monitoring Array (ABSLMP), 13 of the Pacific Sea Level and Geodetic Monitoring Project (SPSLCMP), and Suva in Fiji and Christmas Island, back to 1996 where BOM's archive has the year, from the yearly archives on BOM's anonymous FTP server, `ftp://ftp.bom.gov.au/anon/home/ntc/pacific/uhslc/`.

- **License:** BOM's reply to the permission request in [#80](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/issues/80), 12 November 2026, reference CAS-151567-N3L2D4, from its Climate Data Services mailbox: "As long as the products adhere to the conditions of use as displayed in the Bureau of Meteorology webpage, it should be fine to use data." The conditions are the [tide-information conditions of use](https://www.bom.gov.au/oceanography/projects/ntc/Tidal_Information_Conditions_of_Use.pdf), which are written for tide predictions. They ask that a publication acknowledge copyright in the Commonwealth of Australia represented by the Bureau of Meteorology and carry the Bureau's disclaimer of warranty and liability, and, for a product made from the data, the "based on Bureau of Meteorology information that has subsequently been modified" disclaimer. Both are in `terms` in [`sources.json`](https://stillwell.openwaters.io/sources.json) and linked from every station's `attribution`: "Bureau of Meteorology, © Commonwealth of Australia (conditions of use and disclaimers: …)", then the project's name. The store reads the reply as allowing use under those conditions, commercial use included, as it does for UHSLC's copies of the same gauges, whose notice (`NOTICES.bom` in [`src/sources/uhslc/notices.ts`](../uhslc/notices.ts)) this source's credit, conditions link and terms come from, so the two can't differ: the stations are tagged `commercial_use` true. The reply doesn't mention commercial use, though, and a copy can't carry looser terms than the original, so if BOM answers otherwise both sources change together. BOM's [anonymous FTP](https://www.bom.gov.au/catalogue/anon-ftp.shtml) is the route its website's block page names for automated access, under the default terms of its [copyright notice](https://www.bom.gov.au/other/copyright.shtml), which the GESLA contributor notes in this repo record as personal or in-house use, not supplied to others or used commercially. A request to BOM, not yet sent, asks it to confirm that the FTP files fall under the reply's permission, and to allow the ABSLMP CSVs and station sheets on its website to be fetched, which scripted requests can't reach (HTTP 403 from its bot protection, which the adapter doesn't try to get around).
- **Contact for errors:** the [National Tidal Centre's page](https://www.bom.gov.au/oceanography/projects/ntc/ntc.shtml). BOM's site is closed to scripted requests, so the mailbox it names there isn't recorded here.
- **Stations:** `bom/<number>`, the number in the files' names, `h<number>.dat`. The numbers are BOM's older ones, not UHSLC's, for the gauges whose UHSLC series have a version letter: Cape Ferguson (995, `uhslc/343a`), Rosslyn Bay (996, `330a`), Port Kembla (997, `342a`), Burnie (994, `344a`), Hillarys (958, `132a`), Port Stanvac (956, `100a`) and Milner Bay (959, `145a`). The others carry UHSLC's number: Spring Bay (335), Portland (129), Thevenard (128), Esperance (176), Broome (166), Darwin (168), Cocos (171), Suva (018), Christmas Island (170) and the 13 Pacific gauges, Pohnpei (001), Tarawa (002), Nauru (004), Majuro (005), Honiara (009), Rarotonga (023), Funafuti (025), Nuku'alofa (038), Port Vila (046), Lombrum (400), Apia (401), Lautoka (402) and Alofi on Niue (598). Stony Point (993) and Lorne (992) have no UHSLC series. Positions are those BOM's records of the gauges carry in GESLA (UHSLC's for Alofi), since the files' own position lines are to a minute. A gauge is active when the newest year's archive or the year before's holds a value for it: Port Stanvac and Christmas Island aren't, and their last values are from 2010 and 2011.
- **Not listed:** the archives also carry gauges BOM relays for others: Fremantle (Western Australia's Department of Transport), Brisbane Bar, Bundaberg and Townsville (Queensland's Department of Transport and Main Roads), Fort Denison (Manly Hydraulics Laboratory) and Booby Island. Their owners publish them under their own terms, so they aren't listed here, and Jackson Bay (LINZ) isn't in the archives. Suva and Christmas Island are listed, tagged non-commercial: GESLA credits its records of both to BOM, the National Tidal Centre supplies them to PSMSL (Christmas Island is PSMSL station 47), and UHSLC's research-quality Suva series, `018b`, comes from the Tidal Centre under BOM's notice, but BOM hasn't said in writing that either is its own to republish. The request to BOM asks; once it confirms, `unconfirmed` comes off both gauges in `GAUGES`. The ABSLMP's Thursday Island gauge has no file in the archives.
- **Files:** one archive a year, `<year>.ar` (a Unix `ar` archive, 0.2 to 0.9 MB), with a member `h<number>.dat.Z` for each gauge that year. Members are `compress` files, decoded in the adapter (`uncompress`). BOM has archives for 1996 and for 2005 on; 1997 to 2004, and a year not yet begun, return a 550, which reads as no values, and a 550 for any other year is an error. The archives for 2015 to 2018 hold one file each, `h598` (Alofi's), and none of the other gauges, so those four years have no values here except Alofi's. Suva is in the archives from 2005, Christmas Island's only for 2007 to 2011, where the 2011 file stops on 31 August, and the Australian gauges from 1996 or 2005. Every member of 1996's is a gzip file under the `.Z` name (erratum `bom-gzip-named-z`). The current year's archive is rebuilt in place: `2026.ar` and the `h<number>.dat` files beside it were written on 22 October 2026 and end on 30 September, and `2025.ar` was last written on 16 February 2026. An archive holds every gauge, so a run asks for each year once, whatever the number of gauges. The adapter doesn't read the `h<number>.dat` files, which are the current year's members of `2026.ar` line for line.
- **Levels:** the file's format is UHSLC's hourly ASCII: a position line, then two lines a day, each the station number, an 8-character name, the year, month and day, the half of the day (1 for hours 0 to 11, 2 for 12 to 23) and 12 levels of 5 characters in millimeters, `9999` where missing. BOM's file for Burnie in 2005 has a damaged position line, which the adapter skips (erratum `bom-burnie-2005-position-line`). Every file's lines must run from January 1 a half-day each without a gap or a repeat, and every level must be a whole number or `9999`, or the file is refused. A year two or more back has to run to December 31, or for Christmas Island's last file, 2011, to August 31, and last year's, which may be the first, unrebuilt one, reads as far as it goes. Broome's levels pass 10 m, which fills the five characters.
- **Flags:** the files have none. Values in the year under way are preliminary, and until April so are the two years before it, since BOM rebuilds an archive in the new year's first weeks and again a year later (`2025.ar` on 16 February 2026, `2024.ar` on 30 January 2026); earlier years are good. The Monthly refetch reads every active gauge again from the year before last to this one, since BOM rebuilds a finished year's archive in the new year's first weeks (`2024.ar` in January 2026 and `2025.ar` in February). That a year's archive is final once it has been rebuilt is an assumption.
- **Times:** UTC (the files' lines say `TIMEZONE=GMT`). Against the store's UHSLC fast-delivery series of the same gauges for 2026, the values are the same at the same hour at Thevenard, Portland, Darwin, Cocos, Esperance, Spring Bay, Pohnpei, Apia and Honiara (mean difference under 0.1 mm, standard deviation 0.4 to 4.2 mm, no lag), and at Broome, with the offset below. UHSLC's fast delivery of these gauges carries BOM's values. Suva's levels equal UHSLC's fast-delivery series hour for hour (all 8,605 hours of 2008, 8,727 of 8,760 in 2022). Christmas Island's equal it for all of 2008; from August 2009 UHSLC's is about 0.05 m lower, and UHSLC has no values for 2007, which BOM's file fills.
- **Datum:** the files don't name their zero. The levels are heights above each gauge's tide gauge zero, `TGZ`: each gauge's mean level is 0.1 to 0.26 m above the height of the Australian Height Datum that the list beside the files, `readme.txt` (2015), gives as `AHD=`, which is the height of AHD above the gauge zero, so a level less than that value is in AHD, and `datums` holds `AHD` as its negative. It holds it for the 11 gauges whose list gives one value that fits their levels: Cape Ferguson 1.59, Rosslyn Bay 2.36, Port Kembla 0.872, Burnie 1.938, Spring Bay 1.153, Portland 0.507, Thevenard 0.993, Esperance 0.707, Hillarys 0.763, Lorne 1.423 and Stony Point 1.6. Broome's value, 4.462, is 1.1 m below its mean level where the others are within 0.26 m, so it's left out (erratum `bom-broome-ahd`); Darwin has two (4.105 as `PD` and 3.975 as `TGZ`) and Port Stanvac's carries a question mark (1.2641, or 1.282 "more likely"). Cocos, Milner Bay and the Pacific gauges have none. The list is old, and its values aren't BOM's station sheets, which are on the website's ABSLMP page and not reachable here. UHSLC's own series of the gauges are in `ZERO` of UHSLC's choosing. At Broome UHSLC's levels are exactly 2.000 m below BOM's file's; at the others they're equal. `links.parquet` measures the offset between linked series that share no datum, so a level converts between them where both have values.
- **Freshness:** BOM writes the files about monthly, weeks behind: 22 October for data to 30 September. Nothing runs hourly, and BOM's FTP carries nothing newer for these gauges; the real-time displays on BOM's website aren't served as files. The Monthly refetch reads the archives of the year before last, last year and this year again each month.
- **Not used:** the ABSLMP CSVs (`https://www.bom.gov.au/ntc/IDO71001/IDO71001_<year>.csv`, station sheets with each gauge's AHD offset) and the Pacific project's files on the website, which are behind the bot protection; the `IDO7…SLD.txt` monthly statistics on the FTP; and `jasl/jasl_2026.zip`, which is UHSLC's JASL archive (whole records of hourly values from 1979 for 54 gauges, UHSLC's station numbers), a copy of UHSLC's data rather than BOM's.

## GESLA's BOM records

GESLA 4.1 has 172 records whose contributor is BOM. 30 are gauges this source lists, and each is named in its `same_as` with the UHSLC series of the same gauge, so they group under `preferred` by [`rank`](../../same-as.ts). The other 142 aren't in BOM's archives and stay GESLA-only:

- 7 Papua New Guinea gauges of the Pacific project's earlier years, which ended between 1994 and 2003: Alotau, Kavieng, Lae, Madang, Wewak, Port Moresby and Rabaul. Lombrum, which has run since 1994, is listed.
- Jackson Bay in New Zealand, which belongs to LINZ.
- 7 gauges of Australia's Antarctic and external territories: Casey, Davis, Mawson, Macquarie Island, Lord Howe Island and two records of Norfolk Island.
- 127 gauges on the Australian coast: [#240](https://github.com/openwatersio/stillwell/issues/240) found that the National Tidal Centre lists about 40 port gauges it doesn't run, whose raw observations its page sends users to ask their owners for, and that state agencies such as Manly Hydraulics Laboratory and Maritime Safety Queensland publish others. Records with a state agency's own station code (Queensland's `h…a`, Western Australia's `dv…`, `ff…`, `pl…` and similar) are theirs. Their owners' own publications are where a series of them would come from.

## Running ingest

- **Limits:** BOM states no rate limit for its FTP server. The adapter asks for one archive at a time, a second apart, from one passive connection each, which is about 30 requests for a whole backfill and 1 to 3 a month after. The runner's FTP access to BOM hasn't been tried from GitHub Actions' network.

### Backfill

Monthly refreshes the list on the 2nd; dispatch Monthly with `sources: bom` once the adapter is on `main`. Then backfill through the Ingest workflow, in two runs, since `all` takes active stations only and Port Stanvac and Christmas Island aren't:

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill bom all 1996 2027 --skip-written
```

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill bom 956,170 1996 2012 --skip-written
```

Each year's archive is read once, so a run makes about 30 requests, and writes about 500 station-years. `--skip-written` records the years BOM has no archive for (1997 to 2004) and skips them for 30 days. The Monthly refetch covers the year before last and the two after it, so a later rebuild of an older archive is read with `backfill bom all <year> 2027 --refetch`.

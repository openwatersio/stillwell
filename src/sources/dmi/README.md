# Danish Meteorological Institute (`dmi`)

Observed sea level at DMI's and the Danish Coastal Authority's tide gauges in Denmark, read from DMI's open [Oceanographic Observation API](https://www.dmi.dk/friedata/dokumentation/apis/oceanographic-observation-and-tidewater-api) (`oceanObs`, OGC API Features). [DMI's notes](https://www.dmi.dk/friedata/dokumentation/oceanographic-observations-data) describe the parameters and the quality control.

- **Stations:** each gauge is `dmi/<id>`, with DMI's station ID (`dmi/30017` for Hornbæk). DMI gives a gauge a new ID when it replaces it, so the long records are split: Hornbæk is `30018` (1891 to 1991) and `30017` (1991 on), Gedser `31618` and `31616`, Esbjerg `25148` and `25149`. Many sites have a primary gauge (`I`) and a secondary one (`II`) under separate IDs. A station whose record includes years the Coastal Authority owned it carries the Coastal Authority's credit too. DMI's [historical availability](https://www.dmi.dk/friedata/dokumentation/data/ocean-obs-data-stations-historical-availability) page gives each parameter's first year at each station.
- **Not listed:** the API also serves municipalities' and ports' gauges, which DMI's terms don't cover, and DMI's gauges that have only `sea_reg` (below). A gauge handed to another owner, such as a port, ends the day before the handover, so the store holds none of the new owner's values. A gauge whose first owner is neither DMI nor the Coastal Authority isn't listed.
- **Levels:** every 10 minutes since mid-2001; every 15 or 30 minutes, or hourly, before that.
- **Times:** UTC (`2024-01-15T16:40:00Z`). Levels match UHSLC's Hornbæk and Gedser and CMEMS's Copenhagen at zero lag. UHSLC's are copies of DMI's records, so they show only that the clock hasn't changed. The tide in the Danish straits is a few centimeters and weather moves the level more, so the tide's phase can't check the clock.
- **Datum:** DMI serves three sea-level parameters: `sealev_dvr` against DVR90 (Danish Vertical Reference 1990), `sealev_ln` against the gauge's local zero, and `sea_reg`, the gauge's own registration. A station's levels come from `sealev_dvr` where it has it (`DVR90`), otherwise from `sea_reg` at a gauge the Coastal Authority owned whenever it carried it (`DVR90`), otherwise from `sealev_ln` (`LN`). The parameter is kept as `parameter` in the store's list. At DMI's gauges `sea_reg` is an instrument reading, and at municipal and port gauges DMI doesn't describe it, so neither is read. `datums` is empty.
- **Coastal Authority before 2002:** the Coastal Authority's `sea_reg` is on an older reference, likely DNN, until 27 or 28 May 2002 (erratum `dmi-coastal-authority-dnn`). Each of the 36 gauges running then is split into a datum epoch of its own from 29 May 2002. The earlier epoch is labeled DVR90, as DMI serves it, and stays out of the combined series until its offset is measured. At Fåborg, Karrebæksminde and Kalvehave the `I` and `II` gauges disagree until 2004 (erratum `dmi-coastal-authority-eastern-pairs`).
- **Flags:** each value has a `qcStatus`. `manual` (checked by hand) is `good`; `auto` (automatic real-time checks) and `none` are `preliminary`. A value with a `qcResult` failed a check: 1 and 2 (too high, too low), 3 and 4 (step too big, value stuck) and 5 (water temperature) are `suspect`, and 6 ("data is erroneous") is `bad`. A value with no `qcResult`, or any other code, passed. DMI stopped checking values by hand in 2013, apart from a few, so nearly all later values stay `preliminary`. DMI says its observations are raw measurements that may be faulty, and that its databases change as it finds errors.
- **Freshness:** active stations are read hourly, and the latest value arrives 1 to 2 minutes after it's measured. Past months aren't read again, so a correction DMI makes reaches the store only through a manual backfill.
- **Independent checks:** the store holds no other originator's record of a DMI gauge: UHSLC's and CMEMS's records of Hornbæk, Gedser and Copenhagen are copies of DMI's own, so they show only that the clock hasn't changed. The one near pair, DMI's 30357 and SMHI's Flinten, is a pair of gauges, which agree at zero lag at 3.5 cm RMS (the [pair check](../../../docs/contributing/checks.md#pair-check)): the clocks agree to the 10-minute steps, and the datums are unchecked, since the two don't share a named one.

## Known limits

- **Before `sealev_dvr`:** `sealev_dvr` starts in 1997 to 1999 at DMI's long-running gauges, so their values from before that, on local zero only, aren't fetched. Hornbæk's `30017` starts in 1997, not 1991. DMI recommends `sealev_ln` for its data before 1997, and its replaced gauges' records (`30018` and the like) are on `LN`.
- **Handovers:** a handover during the day loses that day's hours before it. Values read hourly between a handover and the monthly refresh that first sees it stay in the store until they're deleted by hand.

## Running ingest

- **API key:** the API needs no key: `opendataapi.dmi.dk` is open, and only the old `dmigw.govcloud.dk` host still asks for one.
- **Handovers:** the refresh that first sees a handover warns with the new owner and the instant, at a gauge the store listed as active or as ending after the handover; one listed inactive with no end isn't read hourly, so it has nothing to delete and doesn't count toward the floor below. Values the poller stored between the handover and that refresh stay in the store, in the year files and for up to 30 days in the daily files under `recent/`, from which compaction folds them back, and the poller holds them in D1 for 8 days, and nothing in the store removes part of a station's record, so they have to be deleted by hand. The run fails on the station as withdrawn when it was active, but not when it was already inactive. Since a handed-over station's `record_end` is the day before the handover, no backfill or refetch reads the new owner's values.
- **List floor:** a list of fewer than 90 sea-level stations is refused as caught mid-reload, so the store keeps the one it has, and handovers the stored list doesn't end at yet don't count toward the 90, so a renamed owner reaching 10 or more gauges in one refresh fails it rather than retire them; fewer retire.
- **Parameter checks:** a request whose parameter isn't measured from the station's datum fails with a plain error, as does a value of another parameter: the datum follows from the parameter, so a refresh would have no epoch to open. A station whose levels move to another parameter under the same datum name, such as a Coastal Authority gauge gaining `sealev_dvr`, is another series: `seriesChanged` says so, and the refresh opens or holds a datum epoch. Backfill writes each epoch's span.
- **Limits:** DMI's fair-use limit is 500 requests per 5 seconds, with 429 past it; requests start at least 250 ms apart. A page holds at most 300,000 features, so a year of one parameter (about 52,600 values at 10 minutes) is one page, and offsets stop at 500,000. A page can hold fewer features than asked for and still link a `next` page, so pages are followed until an empty one, which has no `next`; each year is two requests. The `datetime` range includes both ends, so each request ends a millisecond before the next year. A station-year at 10 minutes is about 15 MB of JSON, 1.8 MB gzipped, and takes about 10 seconds; a month is 155 KB gzipped and takes about 2 seconds. The list and backfill ask only for the years from a day before a station's `record_start` to a day after its `record_end`.
- **Cache:** nothing is cached. DMI sends a range's newest values first and pages by offset, so a cached first page paired with a live second one would skip or repeat values once DMI corrects a year. Ended years with no preliminary value are still skipped by backfill (`yearDone`).

### Backfill

The first list has to be in the store before anything else runs: the poller and Compact daily skip DMI with a warning until it is, so dispatch Monthly with `sources: dmi` first, with `accept_epochs` checked: the first refresh opens the 36 epochs the May 2002 split gives the Coastal Authority's gauges, more than a first refresh accepts without it. Backfill runs through the Ingest workflow, or locally against a local store. `all` takes active stations only, and most of the record before the 1990s is at gauges DMI has since replaced and lists as ended, such as Hornbæk's 30018 (1891–1991), so a second run names the inactive stations, from the store's list (`SELECT string_agg(source_id, ',') FROM 'stations/dmi.parquet' WHERE status = 'inactive'`):

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill dmi all 1889 2026 --skip-written
```

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill dmi <inactive IDs> 1889 2026 --skip-written
```

That's about 2,600 station-years, most of them at 10 minutes (about 10 seconds and 1.8 MB each) and the older ones hourly or every 15 minutes (a second or two each), so about 5,100 requests, 3.5 GB of compressed JSON, and 6 hours one station at a time. At the default 4 stations at once, it fits in one Ingest run with `timeout` at 360 minutes, with a second to pick up where `--skip-written` finds the first one stopped. It ends before the current year, which is fetched whole on every run and so is backfilled on its own, `backfill dmi all 2026 2027`.

### Scheduled runs

The poller keeps the active stations current. The source sets `finalizes` false, so Monthly doesn't refetch it: `auto` values stay `preliminary`, and a refetch would find nothing new. A correction DMI makes reaches the store only through `backfill --refetch`.

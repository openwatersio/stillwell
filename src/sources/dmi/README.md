# Danish Meteorological Institute (`dmi`)

Observed sea level at about 100 of DMI's and the Danish Coastal Authority's tide gauges in Denmark, from DMI's open [Oceanographic Observation API](https://www.dmi.dk/friedata/dokumentation/apis/oceanographic-observation-and-tidewater-api) (`oceanObs`, OGC API Features), back to 1889. [DMI's notes](https://www.dmi.dk/friedata/dokumentation/oceanographic-observations-data) describe the parameters and the quality control. The API needs no key: `opendataapi.dmi.dk` is open, and only the old `dmigw.govcloud.dk` host still asks for one.

- **License:** [CC BY 4.0](https://www.dmi.dk/friedata/dokumentation/terms-of-use), which covers the oceanographic data from gauges owned by DMI and by the Danish Coastal Authority, and lets anyone share and adapt it for any purpose, commercial use included. So `redistribution` and `commercial_use` are both true. The license asks for appropriate credit, a link to the license, and a note of any changes; `terms` carries that and DMI's warning that its observations are raw, may be faulty, and may change when it finds errors. Attribution is "Danish Meteorological Institute (DMI)", followed by "Danish Coastal Authority (Kystdirektoratet)" at any gauge the Coastal Authority owned in any period, since the record holds every period's values. The API also serves municipalities' and ports' gauges, which the terms don't mention; they're left out (below). UHSLC's documentation files mark its copies of DMI's Gedser and Hornbæk records non-commercial; DMI's own terms for what this API serves have no such limit.
- **Contact for errors:** friedata_kontakt@dmi.dk, the open-data team, named in [DMI's open-data terms of use](https://www.dmi.dk/friedata/dokumentation/terms-of-use).
- **Stations:** every station in `/collections/station/items` with a sea-level series, by DMI's station ID (`dmi/30017` for Hornbæk). DMI lists a station once for each period of its metadata, so each ID is collapsed to its latest period (by `validFrom`), with `record_start` the earliest `operationFrom` of any period, or none when no period gives one, since `validFrom` dates a period of metadata, not the record. A station is listed for the periods DMI or the Coastal Authority owned it, from its first period. One handed to another owner, such as a port, stays listed as `inactive` with its last covered period's owner, name and position, and its `record_end` is the UTC day before the handover, so no backfill or refetch reads the new owner's values; a handover during the day loses the covered hours before it. A gauge DMI or the Coastal Authority takes back later stays ended at the first handover, and one whose first period is another owner's isn't listed. The refresh that first sees a handover warns with the new owner and the instant, at a gauge the store listed as active or as ending after the handover; one listed inactive with no end isn't read hourly, so it has nothing to delete and doesn't count toward the floor below. Values Ingest hourly stored between the handover and that refresh stay in the store, in the year files and for up to 7 days in `recent/`, from which compaction folds them back, and the realtime poller holds them in ClickHouse for 8 days, and nothing in the store removes part of a station's record, so they have to be deleted by hand. The run fails on the station as withdrawn when it was active, but not when it was already inactive. The parameter comes from the latest period, or from every period when the latest lists none this adapter reads, so a period that leaves a parameter out can move a station to another series. A station whose levels move to another parameter under the same datum name, such as a Coastal Authority gauge gaining `sealev_dvr`, is another series: `seriesChanged` says so, and the refresh opens or holds a datum epoch. A station whose latest period is `Inactive`, or has an `operationTo` or `validTo`, is `inactive`, with `record_end` the day its operation or period ends, or none when DMI gives no end; the rest are `active` and live. DMI gives a gauge a new ID when it replaces one, so the long records are split: Hornbæk is `30018` (1891 to 1991) and `30017` (1991 on), Gedser `31618` and `31616`, Esbjerg `25148` and `25149`. Many sites have a primary gauge (`I`) and a secondary one (`II`) under separate IDs. On 12 November 2026 the station list held 178 IDs in 233 periods ([the fixture](../../../test/fixtures/dmi-stations.json)), all with a sea-level series, of which 99 are read here (80 of them active): 36 of DMI's on DVR90, 15 old DMI records on local zero and 48 of the Coastal Authority's, which DMI names `Kystdirektoratet / Coastal Authority`. The 51 municipal and port gauges (`Kommuner/Havne`, `Harbours & municipalities`, or no owner) are left out, as are 28 of DMI's with only `sea_reg`. None of DMI's or the Coastal Authority's gauges had been handed to a port; the five that changed owner went from no owner to `Harbours & municipalities`. Every station is `coastal` and `DK`, and every 10 minutes, except the old local-zero records and any that ended before 2002, whose interval is left empty since it changed over their records; DMI's list has no Greenland or Faroe gauges, but `GRL` and `FRO` would read as `GL` and `FO`. A list of fewer than 90 sea-level stations is refused as caught mid-reload, so the store keeps the one it has, and handovers the stored list doesn't end at yet don't count toward the 90, so a renamed owner reaching 10 or more gauges in one refresh fails it rather than retire them; fewer retire.
- **Datum:** DMI serves three sea-level parameters, each in centimeters: `sealev_dvr` against DVR90 (Danish Vertical Reference 1990), `sealev_ln` against the gauge's local zero, and `sea_reg`, the gauge's own registration. Each station's levels come from one of them, chosen from its latest period's parameters and kept as `parameter` in the store's list, and the station's datum follows from it: `sealev_dvr` where the station lists it (`DVR90`), otherwise `sea_reg` at a gauge the Coastal Authority owned in every period that carries it (`DVR90`), otherwise `sealev_ln` (`LN`). DMI's notes say the Coastal Authority measures in DVR90, which holds from 29 May 2002 on (erratum `dmi-coastal-authority-dnn`). Before then its `sea_reg` is on an older reference, likely DNN (Dansk Normal Nul), which Denmark replaced with DVR90 in 2002: against DMI's `sealev_dvr` at the same or a nearby gauge, 29 of the 36 Coastal Authority gauges running in May 2002 read 5 to 20 cm higher over 12 to 25 May than over 29 May to 11 June (11 cm at Vidåslusen/Højer and 13 cm at Esbjerg, both beside a DMI gauge). Three in Ringkøbing Fjord, which doesn't follow the sea, drop 9 to 11 cm within an hour on 28 May instead. At the eastern harbors, running since late 2000, the `I` and `II` gauges are less settled: Bogense's and Haderslev's moved together, but at Fåborg, Karrebæksminde and Kalvehave the difference between them changed by 7 to 22 cm at the switch, and they disagree by up to 20 cm through late 2002 before agreeing within 1 cm by 2004 (erratum `dmi-coastal-authority-eastern-pairs`). Wherever the switch shows, it's over by 29 May. So the source splits each of those 36 gauges' records on 29 May 2002 (`overrides.splits`, as [stations.md](../../../docs/stations.md#when-an-epoch-opens) describes for unreported zero moves). Their values from before are kept in an epoch of their own, labeled DVR90 as DMI serves them, with `to_current` null, so they stay out of the combined series until a decision gives the offset; the hours on 27 and 28 May at the gauges that moved early fall in it too. Backfill writes each epoch's span, and `record_start` keeps DMI's dates, from September 1990 at the earliest. None of its gauges has any value before 1990. Municipal and port gauges have only `sea_reg`, which DMI doesn't describe: at Hirtshals the port's gauge (`20049`) and DMI's (`20047`, DVR90) read within 2 cm of each other in June 2024, but one site doesn't settle every gauge's reference, so they aren't listed. At DMI's own gauges `sea_reg` is an instrument reading: at Hornbæk it's 1 cm below DVR90 in 2020 and 142 cm above local zero in 1950, so a DMI gauge with nothing else (most of its `II` gauges) isn't listed. `sealev_dvr` starts in 1997 to 1999 at DMI's long-running gauges, so their values from before that, on local zero only, aren't fetched: Hornbæk's `30017` starts in 1997, not 1991. DMI recommends `sealev_ln` for its data before 1997, and its old records (`30018` and the like) are on `LN`. `datums` is empty. A request whose parameter isn't measured from the station's datum fails with a plain error, as does a value of another parameter: the datum follows from the parameter, so a refresh would have no epoch to open.
- **Levels:** centimeters, as meters. Every 10 minutes since mid-2001; every 15 or 30 minutes, or hourly, before that. A missing value is dropped.
- **Flags:** each value has a `qcStatus`: `manual` (checked by hand) is `good`; `auto` (automatic real-time checks) and `none` are `preliminary`. A value with a `qcResult` failed a check: 1 and 2 (too high, too low), 3 and 4 (step too big, value stuck) and 5 (water temperature) are `suspect`, and 6 ("data is erroneous") is `bad`; a value that passes has no `qcResult`, and any other code is read as passing. At Hornbæk, values to mid-2012 are `manual`, and from mid-2013 on nearly all are `auto`; DMI checks a few by hand (12 of 52,126 in 2023).
- **Times:** `observed` is UTC (`2024-01-15T16:40:00Z`), read as the instant it names. The latest value arrives about 1 to 2 minutes after it's measured.
- **Clock check:** against UHSLC's records of Hornbæk and Gedser (2010) and CMEMS's of Copenhagen (2020), every series fits best within 10 minutes of zero. The tide in the Danish straits is a few centimeters and weather moves the level more (r 0.21 to 0.47), so K1's phase is noise there (−118 minutes at Hornbæk). DMI's Hornbæk against UHSLC's version A of it for 2010 correlates at 0.9999 at zero lag, 0.98 an hour either side and 0.76 at ±12 hours, so the clocks agree.
- **Limits:** DMI's fair-use limit is 500 requests per 5 seconds, with 429 past it; requests start at least 250 ms apart. A page holds at most 300,000 features, so a year of one parameter (about 52,600 values at 10 minutes) is one page, and offsets stop at 500,000. A page can hold fewer features than asked for and still link a `next` page, so pages are followed until an empty one, which has no `next`; each year is two requests. The `datetime` range includes both ends, so each request ends a millisecond before the next year. A station-year at 10 minutes is about 15 MB of JSON, 1.8 MB gzipped, and takes about 10 seconds; a month is 155 KB gzipped and takes about 2 seconds. The list and backfill ask only for the years from a day before a station's `record_start` to a day after its `record_end`.
- **History:** back to 1889 at Esbjerg and 1891 at Hornbæk and Gedser, on local zero. DMI's [historical availability](https://www.dmi.dk/friedata/dokumentation/data/ocean-obs-data-stations-historical-availability) page gives each parameter's first year at each station.
- **Freshness:** nothing is cached. DMI sends a range's newest values first and pages by offset, so a cached first page paired with a live second one would skip or repeat values once DMI corrects a year. Ended years with no preliminary value are still skipped by backfill (`yearDone`).

## Backfill

The first list has to be in the store before anything else runs: Ingest hourly and Compact daily skip DMI with a warning until it is, so dispatch Monthly with `sources: dmi` first, with `accept_epochs` checked: the first refresh opens the 36 epochs the May 2002 split gives the Coastal Authority's gauges, more than a first refresh accepts without it. Backfill runs through the Ingest workflow, or locally against a local store. `all` takes active stations only, and most of the record before the 1990s is at gauges DMI has since replaced and lists as ended, such as Hornbæk's 30018 (1891–1991), so a second run names the inactive stations, from the store's list (`SELECT string_agg(source_id, ',') FROM 'stations/dmi.parquet' WHERE status = 'inactive'`):

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill dmi all 1889 2026 --skip-written
```

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill dmi <inactive IDs> 1889 2026 --skip-written
```

[`docs/ticon-inventory.csv`](../../../docs/ticon-inventory.csv) routes DMI's and Denmark's CMEMS records to `dmi` but gives none a station ID until `npm run ticon:inventory` runs against a store with DMI's list, so `--first` has nothing to order by.

That's about 2,600 station-years, most of them at 10 minutes (about 10 seconds and 1.8 MB each) and the older ones hourly or every 15 minutes (a second or two each), so about 5,100 requests, 3.5 GB of compressed JSON, and 6 hours one station at a time. At the default 4 stations at once, it fits in one Ingest run with `timeout` at 360 minutes, with a second to pick up where `--skip-written` finds the first one stopped. It ends before the current year, which is fetched whole on every run and so is backfilled on its own, `backfill dmi all 2026 2027`. Ingest hourly keeps the active stations current. The source sets `finalizes` false, so Monthly doesn't refetch it: DMI stopped checking values by hand in 2013, so `auto` values stay `preliminary` and a refetch would find nothing new; a correction DMI makes reaches the store only through `backfill --refetch`.

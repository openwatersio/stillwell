# California Data Exchange Center (`cdec`)

15-minute river stage at 41 tidal gauges in the Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta, Suisun Bay and the Petaluma River, 35 of them active, back to 1998 at the oldest, from the [California Data Exchange Center](https://cdec.water.ca.gov/) (CDEC), which the Department of Water Resources runs. These are the stations TICON names by CDEC code; TICON's DWR-numbered California stations come from DWR's Water Data Library, in `dwr`.

- **License:** CDEC states no license. Its pages fall under DWR's [Conditions of Use](https://water.ca.gov/Conditions-of-Use), a privacy notice and a disclaimer: DWR "does not guarantee the accuracy, completeness, timeliness, or correct sequencing of the information". Nothing restricts redistribution or commercial use, so `redistribution` and `commercial_use` are true and every station is in the public lake. CDEC's pages say its data is "preliminary and subject to revision". Many stations belong to other agencies, which CDEC names as each station's operator; each station's `attribution` credits them, by agency and without their divisions: the Department of Water Resources, the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation (whose data is a U.S. Government work), or the City of Petaluma, "via CDEC". The City of Petaluma's [Terms of Use](https://cityofpetaluma.org/terms-of-use/) are a privacy and monitoring notice: its site's information is "provided as a public service", and the city's records are public under California's Public Records Act, with nothing restricting reuse. The National Weather Service also republishes its gauge, as PTBC1.
- **Contact for errors:** flood.webmaster@water.ca.gov, the address [CDEC's contact page](https://cdec.water.ca.gov/CDECContact.html) gives as its help desk.
- **Stations:** the 60 CDEC codes TICON's `cdwr` records carry (`CODES`), `cdec/<code>` (`cdec/MRZ` for Martinez), all `estuary`. Each refresh reads every station's metadata page (`staMeta?station_id=<code>`), about 70 KB each, for its name, position, operator and the dates of its 15-minute stage. A station can list several 15-minute rows, one per way its data came in, and they're merged: the earliest start, and current if any is. A page without its sensor table, or with a stage row the adapter can't read, fails the refresh rather than drop the station as having no stage. That holds for the stations the last list had, as does a page that no longer shows a listed station's 15-minute stage; a bad page for a code the last list left out, one USGS runs or with no 15-minute stage, only warns. A station whose operator names USGS is left out, since `usgs` reads USGS's gauges: 14 of the 60, among them those USGS runs with the Bureau of Reclamation or DWR. Stations with no 15-minute stage are left out too: the Corps of Engineers' two (RYE, SKT) and three with only hourly stage that ended by 2002 (BRY, OLR, VNE). That leaves 41 in November 2026. A station whose 15-minute stage has an end date is `inactive`; six are.
- **Datum:** each refresh reads the datum from the station's page, where its gauge-zero table gives one. A zero of 0.0 ft in a named datum puts the stage in that datum: 12 stations are 0.0 ft NAVD 88, so `NAVD88`. Any other zero puts the stage above the gauge's own zero, `GZ`, with the zero's elevation in `datums`: Sacramento River above the Delta Cross Channel (SDC) is 5.0 ft NGVD 29, so `GZ` with an NGVD29 offset of 1.524 m. A station with no table takes a datum its notes give, from a short list in the adapter (`NOTED`: Beldon Landing, BDL, "Datum of this station is NAVD88"), and otherwise `GZ`; that's 27 stations. A table naming any other datum, such as MSL, fails the refresh. Each station also reports its page's gauge-zero row as its `reference` (`0 ft NAVD88`, `5 ft NGVD29`), and none without a table, so a table appearing later is recorded rather than read as a moved zero. So a page that changes its datum, or moves its gauge zero under the same `GZ` label, opens a datum epoch at that refresh, or holds it for a decision in `overrides.epochs` when it goes back to an earlier one ([docs/stations.md](../../../docs/stations.md)). A listed station whose page loses its gauge-zero table is reported with no datum (`datum: null`), which keeps it in the datum it was listed with, with a warning, since a template change or a gap is likelier than a gauge that dropped its datum. The refresh is monthly and CDEC's data carries no datum, so values from between the change and the refresh still land in the old epoch's files. Four stations moved to NAVD88 partway through their record, by their notes: Georgiana Slough (GSS) and Sutter Slough (SUT) were on an arbitrary datum until 1 October 2005, and Mallard Island (MAL) and Martinez (MRZ) show NGVD29 until 1 October 2006. Nothing before those dates is read (`NAVD88_SINCE`), from Pacific midnight, including for a station read from `stations.parquet`, so each station's files hold one datum; GSS and SUT lose about two years. The stage sensor some pages list as "RIVER STAGE NAVD88" (141) covers only months at most, and isn't read.
- **Levels:** river stage, sensor 1, at the 15-minute ("event", `dur_code=E`) duration, feet, as meters, every 15 minutes at most stations and about every 9 at Petaluma (PTB), whose `interval_s` is null: `CSVDataServlet?Stations=<code>&SensorNums=1&dur_code=E&Start=<date>&End=<date>`, a calendar year a request, about 2 MB. `End` runs only to the start of its day, so each request asks to the day after. A missing reading is `---` and is dropped. The hourly duration, which reaches back to 1987 at a few DWR stations, isn't read: before 2006 it's in other datums. CDEC's flag column is blank throughout and nothing is marked reviewed, so every value is `preliminary` and `finalizes` is false; DWR's reviewed record of its own stations is the Water Data Library.
- **Times:** Pacific local time, with daylight saving, though CDEC's pages say PST. On 8 March 2026 there's no 02:00 hour, and in July 2026 Fisherman's Cut (FCT) reads an hour later on CDEC's clock than in DWR's Water Data Library, which is PST all year. Every year checked has no 02:00 hour on its spring-forward day: GSS in 2004, PTB in 2001, DLC in 2010 and SJG in 2016. Times are converted to UTC with `America/Los_Angeles`. On the day daylight saving ends, CDEC sends the repeated hour once, as standard time; the switch to standard time comes at the 00:45 reading in some years (2014, 2015, 2022, 2024 and 2025 at Martinez) and at 01:00 in the others from 2009 to 2025, so readings from 00:45 to 01:00 that day are dropped. A stamp in the hour skipped when daylight saving starts doesn't exist, and is dropped too.
- **Freshness:** readings reach CDEC within the hour, so `hourly` is true and active stations are live. CDEC may revise any reading, so nothing is cached on disk, but since `finalizes` is false nothing reads a reading again after Ingest hourly's window; a correction made later reaches the lake only through a manual backfill, as for EA and PEGELONLINE.
- **Limits:** none published, so requests start half a second apart and `min_interval_s` is null.

## Backfill

Monthly refreshes CDEC's list on the 2nd. Dispatch Monthly with `sources: cdec` once the adapter is on `main`, then backfill through the Ingest workflow, active stations first and the six inactive ones in a second run:

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill cdec all 1998 2026 --skip-written
```

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill cdec BLL,LIB,M13,MRW,ROR,SUC 2009 2026 --skip-written
```

That's about 650 station-years at about 2 MB each, roughly 1.3 GB and 650 requests, which fits in one Ingest run. Then `backfill cdec all 2026 2027` for the current year. From then on, Ingest hourly keeps the active stations current.

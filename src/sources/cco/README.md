# Channel Coastal Observatory (`cco`)

Tide levels every 10 minutes at 15 gauges of the National Network of Regional Coastal Monitoring Programmes for England and Wales, from the Channel Coastal Observatory's [real-time API](https://www.coastalmonitoring.org/ccoresources/api/).

- **License:** [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/), which the API page names and links. GESLA's paper links version 2. Both allow republishing and commercial use with attribution, so every station goes to the public store with `commercial_use`. The page asks for the credit "Real time data displayed on this page are from the National Network of Regional Coastal Monitoring Programmes, made freely available under the terms of the Open Government Licence" and the note that data are not quality-controlled in real time, on pages and other outputs that use the data. The store's attribution says both.
- **Contact for errors:** the observatory's [feedback form](https://www.coastalmonitoring.org/contact_us) or cco@channelcoast.org, the contact the API page gives.
- **Key:** every request needs a key, which is tied to a domain. Ours was issued for `https://stillwell.openwaters.io/`, and the API checks that the request's `Referer` is that domain or a subdomain, and answers 403 otherwise. The adapter sends the key as `X-API-Key` and the domain as `Referer`, from the environment variables `CCO_API_KEY` and `CCO_REFERER`, and refuses to run without both. The key stays out of the URL, so logs and cache keys don't hold it.
- **Limits:** none published as a number. The page asks clients to cache responses, to request no more often than the data is recorded (every 10 minutes for tides), and says the observatory may limit or disable a key, or limit request rates, without notice. A request covers up to 168 hours for all sites, or 8,760 hours for one. A year of one site is about 50,000 readings, and the API takes from seconds to several minutes to answer one.
- **Stations:** `GET /observations/tides/latest.geojson` answers with every site as a GeoJSON feature: `id`, `sensor` (the name), `date` and `value` of the latest reading, `QCflag`, and a point position. It lists 46 sites. 31 have an `id` that is a link to another provider's page, such as the National Tidal and Sea Level Facility's, and no readings, so they are left out. The other 15 are `cco/<id>`, `coastal`, `GB`, with `source_id` the number: Arun Platform (86), Lymington (87), Brighton (88), Herne Bay (89), Deal Pier (90), Sandown Pier (92), Swanage Pier (93), West Bay Harbour (95), Port Isaac (102), Second Severn Crossing (106), Scarborough (111), Whitby Harbour (112), Exmouth (117), Penarth (119) and Hastings Pier (126). A station is active and live while its latest reading is under a year old, so Arun Platform, whose last reading is from 9 February 2024, is inactive. The list throws when no site has a readable date or a position isn't around Great Britain (latitude 49 to 61, longitude −9 to 3). Teignmouth Pier, which ended in 2014, isn't listed.
- **Same gauge elsewhere:** the 14 gauges other than Whitby Harbour each have a GESLA record with CCO as its originator, and 13 have a CMEMS copy at the same position, with another GESLA record of it (CMEMS's `TG` files); Penarth has none. Each is linked by `same_as` to the GESLA record of the gauge (`GESLA` in [`index.ts`](index.ts)), which the store joins to its CMEMS copy. Second Severn Crossing is GESLA's Severn Bridge. Whitby Harbour is a gauge of its own, 150 m from the EA's and BODC's Whitby: CMEMS, GESLA and TICON list the two apart, so its only link is the GESLA record of CCO's. No CCO gauge has an EA or BODC series.
- **Datum:** `CD`, chart datum: `value` is meters above Chart Datum. The tide QC manual measures from Ordnance Datum Newlyn and adds the Admiralty Tide Tables' offset, so there are no `datums` yet. No gauge with a known Chart Datum has checked the offset. The levels are positive through the cycle at every gauge, 9.5 m near high water at Penarth and 2.3 to 5.5 m between the low and high water of a day at Brighton, as heights above chart datum are and heights above Ordnance Datum Newlyn, which centers on zero, are not.
- **Levels:** `GET /observations/tides/<YYYYMMDDhhmmss>.geojson?sensor=<id>&duration=<hours>` answers with every reading from `duration` hours before the time up to the time, both ends included, newest first, as one feature each, with `id`, `date`, `value` and `QCflag`. The adapter asks a year at a time, from the end back, a second apart, each request starting one reading inside the last. `id`, `value` and `QCflag` arrive as text, such as `"3.88300"` and `"1"`. A window with no readings answers `{"type":"FeatureCollection","features":]}`, which isn't JSON, and reads as no values. Lymington and Brighton answer HTTP 500 for a window before their first reading, so their backfills start at their first year ([Backfill](#backfill)). An answer whose features carry none of the site's id, or a date or value it can't read, fails the request rather than read as no values. A `value` of null, empty or 9999 is not stored; Port Isaac's last readings, to 31 October 2026, have no value and flag 9.
- **Interval:** 10 minutes now. Brighton's readings in 2006 are 5 minutes apart, and a day of Brighton's in 2010 and of Scarborough's in 2008 and 2010 holds 288 readings. `interval_s` is 600. `min_interval_s` is 3600, since Stillwell told the observatory it reads each site once an hour.
- **Archive:** the API's readings start in the year below, at the June 1 of each year tested, so a first reading can be up to a year earlier. GESLA's CCO records start earlier at Herne Bay (March 1996, against 2005 here) and Lymington (2007, against 2009), and later at Brighton (2018), Scarborough (2011) and Penarth (2020), so the API has more years than GESLA at those three.

- **Flags:** `QCflag` is null until the monthly check, and the API page says an empty flag doesn't mean the value passed, so a reading with none is `preliminary`. In Herne Bay's record every reading before late 2025 has no flag, and every reading from about December 2025 to October 2026 is `"1"`; November's are null. Brighton's 2006 readings carry `"0"`. So the check's flags exist for the last year, and earlier readings stay unchecked for good. The check's flags follow the IOC's scheme ([tide QC manual](https://www.coastalmonitoring.org/ccoresources/dataqualitycontrol/TideQCManual_v1.0.pdf), table 1): 1 and 5 good, 2 interpolated, 4, 8 and 9 bad, and 3, 6, 7 and any other code suspect. 0 and a blank flag are `preliminary`. `finalizes` is true. Monthly's refetch reads every active station again from 12 months back to this month, finished years included (`backfill cco all <12 months back> <this month> --refetch`), since a flag arrives only on a reading from that window; reading from the earliest preliminary reading would be reading the archive from 2005. A reading flagged 9 (missing) with its value still sent is bad. One sent with no value isn't stored: a refetch replaces the stored readings of each month it brings readings for, so such a reading leaves the store in any month with other values, and a month whose every reading has none keeps what the store held.
- **Times:** UTC, `YYYYMMDD#hhmmss`; a stamp that isn't a real time fails the request. The clock check against TICON's records for 2025, a year that includes British Summer Time, fits Herne Bay and Penarth at 0 minutes (K1 5 and 17 minutes). TICON's records of these gauges were fitted to CCO's own series, so the fit shows only that the clock hasn't changed since; no other originator's record of these gauges exists to check against.
- **Freshness:** `hourly` is true: the hourly job reads each site's last 12 hours and the poller, which asks each site once an hour, its recent readings. The `latest` feed's `date` is a site's last reading, with or without a value.

## Errata

- **West Bay Harbour's position** (erratum `cco-west-bay-position`). CCO's list puts the gauge 3.9 km west of the harbour, and the store moves it to where GESLA's and CMEMS's records of it have it.
- **Windows with no readings** (erratum `cco-empty-window-answers`). A window with no readings answers a body that isn't JSON, which reads as no values, and Lymington and Brighton answer HTTP 500 before their first reading.

## Running ingest

The Ingest, Ingest hourly, Monthly and Canary workflows pass `CCO_API_KEY` and `CCO_REFERER` as the repository secrets of the same names. The poller reads them from `/etc/stillwell.env`, which a server set up before the source existed needs them added to by hand. The adapter starts requests a second apart. The canary reads Deal Pier's last few days and is skipped without the key. It isn't recorded: its levels path names the request's time, so it has only to return values.

### Backfill

The station list refreshes on the 2nd of each month by the Monthly workflow. Before the first backfill, dispatch Monthly with `sources` set to `cco`. Then read each site from its first year. The runs go separately because Brighton and Lymington answer 500 before their first reading; a year with nothing in it at another site answers empty. A year is one request, or two in a leap year, whose 8,784 hours pass the API's 8,760. The three runs are about 260 requests. Readings before late 2025 stay preliminary, so every year of a station counts as unfinished, and `--skip-written` keeps a run that stops partway from starting over:

```bash
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill cco 86,89,90,92,93,95,102,106,111,112,117,119,126 2005 2027 --skip-written
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill cco 88 2006 2027 --skip-written
node scripts/ingest.ts backfill cco 87 2009 2027 --skip-written
```

End years are exclusive. After the last run, `compact cco --rebuild-bounds` and a publish bring the store's bounds up to date.

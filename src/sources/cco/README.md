# Channel Coastal Observatory (`cco`)

Tide levels every 10 minutes at 15 gauges of the National Network of Regional Coastal Monitoring Programmes for England and Wales, from the Channel Coastal Observatory's [real-time API](https://www.coastalmonitoring.org/ccoresources/api/).

- **License:** [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/), which the API page names and links. GESLA's paper links version 2. Both allow republishing and commercial use with attribution, so every station goes to the public store with `commercial_use`. The page asks for the credit "Real time data displayed on this page are from the National Network of Regional Coastal Monitoring Programmes, made freely available under the terms of the Open Government Licence" and the note that data are not quality-controlled in real time, on pages and other outputs that use the data. The store's attribution says both.
- **Contact for errors:** the observatory's [feedback form](https://www.coastalmonitoring.org/contact_us) or cco@channelcoast.org, the contact the API page gives.
- **Key:** every request needs a key, which is tied to a domain. Ours was issued for `https://stillwell.openwaters.io/`, and the API checks that the request's `Referer` is that domain or a subdomain, and answers 403 otherwise. The adapter sends the key as `X-API-Key` and the domain as `Referer`, from the environment variables `CCO_API_KEY` and `CCO_REFERER`, and refuses to run without both. The key stays out of the URL, so logs and cache keys don't hold it.
- **Limits:** the observatory asks for one request at a time and switches a key off when requests slow its service. The adapter sends one at a time: each request waits for the one before it to finish, however many stations a run reads at once. No rate is published as a number. The page asks clients to cache responses, to request no more often than the data is recorded (every 10 minutes for tides), and says the observatory may limit or disable a key, or limit request rates, without notice. A request covers up to 168 hours for all sites, or 8,760 hours for one. A year of one site is about 50,000 readings, and the API takes from seconds to several minutes to answer one.
- **Stations:** `GET /observations/tides/latest.geojson` answers with every site as a GeoJSON feature: `id`, `sensor` (the name), `date` and `value` of the latest reading, `QCflag`, and a point position. It lists 46 sites. 31 have an `id` that is a link to another provider's page, such as the National Tidal and Sea Level Facility's, and no readings, so they are left out. The other 15 are `cco/<id>`, `coastal`, `GB`, with `source_id` the number: Arun Platform (86), Lymington (87), Brighton (88), Herne Bay (89), Deal Pier (90), Sandown Pier (92), Swanage Pier (93), West Bay Harbour (95), Port Isaac (102), Second Severn Crossing (106), Scarborough (111), Whitby Harbour (112), Exmouth (117), Penarth (119) and Hastings Pier (126). A station is active and live while its latest reading is under a year old, so Arun Platform, whose last reading is from 9 February 2024, is inactive. The list throws when no site has a readable date or a position isn't around Great Britain (latitude 49 to 61, longitude −9 to 3). Teignmouth Pier, which ended in 2014, isn't listed.
- **Same gauge elsewhere:** the 14 gauges other than Whitby Harbour each have a GESLA record with CCO as its originator, and 13 have a CMEMS copy at the same position, with another GESLA record of it (CMEMS's `TG` files); Penarth has none. Each is linked by `same_as` to the GESLA record of the gauge (`GESLA` in [`index.ts`](index.ts)), which the store joins to its CMEMS copy. Second Severn Crossing is GESLA's Severn Bridge. Whitby Harbour is a gauge of its own, 150 m from the EA's and BODC's Whitby: CMEMS, GESLA and TICON list the two apart, so its only link is the GESLA record of CCO's. No CCO gauge has an EA or BODC series. CMEMS's copies of 12 of the 13 gauges equal CCO's levels at every hour they share, so they agree on the datum and the clock without being an independent check, and they hold the same sentinel levels. West Bay Harbour's copy is 0.094 m lower than CCO's from mid-September 2024 to mid-November 2025 and equal otherwise, so one of the two has a step in that period.
- **Datum:** `CD`, chart datum: `value` is meters above Chart Datum. The tide QC manual measures from Ordnance Datum Newlyn and adds the Admiralty Tide Tables' offset, so there are no `datums` yet. Whitby Harbour's 2023 levels against BODC's Whitby, 150 m away and in Admiralty Chart Datum, differ by −0.13 m on average, so both are in chart datum, where Ordnance Datum would differ by meters on this coast. The other 14 gauges have no gauge of another originator to compare with, and their levels are positive through the cycle, 9.5 m near high water at Penarth and 2.3 to 5.5 m between a day's low and high water at Brighton, as heights above chart datum are and heights above Ordnance Datum Newlyn, which centers on zero, are not.
- **Levels:** `GET /observations/tides/<YYYYMMDDhhmmss>.geojson?sensor=<id>&duration=<hours>` answers with every reading from `duration` hours before the time up to the time, both ends included, newest first, as one feature each, with `id`, `date`, `value` and `QCflag`. The adapter asks a year at a time, from the end back, a second apart, each request starting one reading inside the last. `id`, `value` and `QCflag` arrive as text, such as `"3.88300"` and `"1"`. A window with no readings answers `{"type":"FeatureCollection","features":]}`, which isn't JSON, and reads as no values. An answer whose features carry none of the site's id, or a date or value it can't read, fails the request rather than read as no values. A `value` of null or empty, or of 999 m or more either way, is not stored: CCO's levels hold 999.9999 and −999.9999, and at some gauges exactly 99.999, −99.999, −10 and −9.99999, where a gauge sent nothing; Port Isaac's last readings, to 31 October 2026, have no value and flag 9. For stretches of 2011 at West Bay Harbour and of 2010 and 2011 at Port Isaac, each reading comes twice, stamped a second apart with the same level, and the adapter keeps the first (erratum `cco-duplicate-stamps`).
- **Interval:** 10 minutes now. Earlier years differ: Brighton's readings are 5 minutes apart to 2016 and Scarborough's to 2010, Arun Platform's 2019 has about 1,160 readings a day, one a minute, and Scarborough's 2006 has stretches at 1 minute. Brighton has no readings in 2017. `interval_s` is 600. `min_interval_s` is 3600, since Stillwell told the observatory it reads each site once an hour.
- **Archive:** the table gives each site's first reading in the store, in UTC. Herne Bay's is the API's own start, since a request for the day before answers with no readings. A request for a window that starts before Brighton's or Lymington's first reading answers 500, so their backfills start the day after the API's first reading (about 11 February 2006 and 12 February 2009), and the store lacks up to a day of each. GESLA's CCO records start earlier than the API's at Herne Bay (March 1996, against January 2005 here) and Lymington (2007, against 2009), and later at Brighton (2018), Scarborough (2012), Second Severn Crossing (2012) and Penarth (2021).

- **Flags:** `QCflag` is null until the monthly check, and the API page says an empty flag doesn't mean the value passed, so a reading with none is `preliminary`. The check arrives in blocks, since CCO is importing its quality-controlled archive: a gauge has stretches of years where 90 to 100 percent of its readings are flagged `"1"` and stretches with none. Herne Bay's flags start in 2005 (2005, 2009 to 2010, 2015, 2020 to 2021 and 2023 on), Whitby Harbour's in 2014, Arun Platform's in 2020 and Second Severn Crossing's in 2012; Exmouth, Penarth and West Bay Harbour have flags only in 2026. Brighton's 2006 readings carry `"0"`. Of the 13.1 million readings stored, 3.6 million are flagged good, 89,000 are flagged doubtful or bad by CCO, and the rest have none. The newest month has no flags yet. The check's flags follow the IOC's scheme ([tide QC manual](https://www.coastalmonitoring.org/ccoresources/dataqualitycontrol/TideQCManual_v1.0.pdf), table 1): 1 and 5 good, 2 interpolated, 4, 8 and 9 bad, and 3, 6, 7 and any other code suspect. 0 and a blank flag are `preliminary`. `finalizes` is true. The refetch on the 9th reads every active station again from 12 months back to this month, finished years included (`backfill cco all <12 months back> <this month> --refetch`), since a month's flags arrive within weeks (October's had them at every site by 16 November); reading from the earliest preliminary reading would be reading the archive from 2005. A flag on an older year reaches the store only when that year is read again: run the backfill commands below without `--skip-written`, which reads every year that still holds preliminary readings. A reading flagged 9 (missing) with its value still sent is bad. One sent with no value isn't stored: a refetch replaces the stored readings of each month it brings readings for, so such a reading leaves the store in any month with other values, and a month whose every reading has none keeps what the store held. The source checks its values: yes, monthly in arrears.
- **Times:** UTC, `YYYYMMDD#hhmmss`; a stamp that isn't a real time fails the request. The clock check against TICON's records for 2025, a year that includes British Summer Time, fits Herne Bay and Penarth at 0 minutes (K1 5 and 17 minutes). TICON's records of these gauges were fitted to CCO's own series, so that fit shows only that the clock hasn't changed since. Whitby Harbour's 2023 levels against BODC's Whitby levels, an independent gauge, fit best at 0 minutes within 30 either way (RMS difference 0.17 m, and 0.20 to 0.36 m at 10 to 30 minutes).
- **Freshness:** `hourly` is true: the hourly job reads each site's last 12 hours, and the poller fills ClickHouse from the files it writes. The `latest` feed's `date` is a site's last reading, with or without a value.

## Running ingest

CCO is on the server's job queue ([src/sources/server.json](../server.json), [docs/plans/jobs.md](../../../docs/plans/jobs.md)), whose jobs read `CCO_API_KEY` and `CCO_REFERER` from `/etc/stillwell.env`. The Canary workflow passes the repository secrets of the same names, as the other workflows do for a move back. The poller doesn't ask CCO: its metadata sets `poller: "recent"`, so the hourly job's read of each site's last 12 hours is the one request a site an hour that Stillwell told the observatory to expect. The adapter starts requests a second apart (a request that fails with a timeout or a 5xx is sent again, up to four times in all, and a backfill reads four stations at once), and waits up to 15 minutes for a request of more than a day, as a year takes minutes. The canary reads Deal Pier's last few days and is skipped without the key. It isn't recorded: its levels path names the request's time, so it has only to return values.

### Backfill

CCO's scheduled runs are [the job queue's](../../../docs/plans/jobs.md#the-queue), and a backfill is a job, queued on the box with `stillwell-jobs enqueue`. On a store with no CCO list yet, `stillwell-jobs enqueue stations cco --no-publish` starts it first. The years before 2009 of the sites that have them run by name, and Brighton's and Lymington's first years start the day after their first reading, as erratum `cco-empty-window-answers` describes. Every run takes `--concurrency 1`, and the adapter sends one request at a time whatever the run says. A full backfill is about 11 million rows and takes about an hour:

```bash
stillwell-jobs enqueue backfill cco 86,89,90,92,93,95,111 2005 2009 --skip-written --concurrency 1
stillwell-jobs enqueue backfill cco 88 2006-02-12 2009 --skip-written --concurrency 1
stillwell-jobs enqueue backfill cco 87 2009-02-13 2010 --skip-written --concurrency 1
stillwell-jobs enqueue backfill cco all 2009 2027 --skip-written --with-inactive --concurrency 1
```

End years are exclusive. Most years of most stations hold preliminary readings, so they count as unfinished, and `--skip-written` keeps a run that stops partway from starting over. After the last run, `stillwell-jobs enqueue compact cco --rebuild-bounds` brings the store's bounds up to date, and Compact daily's publish picks them up that night.
